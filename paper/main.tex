\title[The frog model on the 3-ary and 4-ary trees]{The frog model is recurrent on the 3-ary tree and transient on the 4-ary tree}
\author{\authorlink}
\thanks{2020 {\em Mathematics Subject Classification.} Primary: 60K35; Secondary: 60J80, 60J10, 60E15, 03B35.\\\indent
{\em Key words and phrases.} Frog model; interacting random walks; $d$-ary tree; transience; recurrence; stochastic domination; recursive distributional inequality; certificate; Lean~4.}

\begin{abstract}
We prove that the frog model on the rooted $d$-ary tree is recurrent for $d=3$ and transient for $d=4$, as conjectured by Hoffman, Johnson and Junge. One frog starts awake at the root and one sleeps at every other vertex; awake frogs perform independent simple random walks, waking the frogs they visit. For $d=4$ the mean number of visits to the root is at most $5.756$. The proof runs an exact recursion on the laws of the responses of subtrees, with a law given by a finite table that dominates its image, certified by two linear systems on $159786$ states. For $d=3$ a subtree of height $m\ge1778250$ entered by one frog sends back at least $(m+2)/5$ frogs on average; the proof combines a lower model, a certified chain of steps on finite tables of bounds, and an induction on the height. Both proofs are formalized in Lean~4.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

We prove that the frog model on the rooted $3$-ary tree is recurrent, and on the rooted $4$-ary tree transient: the root is visited infinitely many times in the first case and only finitely many times in the second, almost surely. These are the two cases left open by a conjecture of Hoffman, Johnson and Junge \cite{HJJ17}. Both proofs count the frogs that a subtree sends back through its top edge: for $d=4$ the mean of that count is below $1$, which a finite certificate proves, and for $d=3$ it grows linearly in the height of the subtree, which a finite certificate starts and an induction on the height carries on.

The frog model on the rooted $d$-ary tree $\T_d$ is the following system of random walks.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The vertices of $\T_d$ are the finite words over the alphabet $\{1,\dots,d\}$; the root is the empty word $\varnothing$, and the children of a vertex $v$ are the words $v1,\dots,vd$. So the root has degree $d$ and every other vertex has degree $d+1$.
\item At every vertex $v$ sits one frog $f_v$, with a path $(S_v(n))_{n\ge0}$, a simple random walk on $\T_d$ started at $v$; the paths of the frogs are independent.
\item At time $0$ the frog $f_\varnothing$ is awake and every other frog is asleep. A sleeping frog wakes at the first time an awake frog is at its vertex, and a frog woken at time $s$ is at $S_v(t-s)$ at every time $t\ge s$.
\end{enumerate}
The quantity of interest is the number of visits to the root after time $0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:V}
V=\#\{(v,t):\ t\ge1,\ f_v \text{ is awake at time } t \text{ and } S_v(t-s_v)=\varnothing\},
\end{equation}
where $s_v$ is the time at which $f_v$ wakes. The model is \emph{transient} if $V<\infty$ almost surely and \emph{recurrent} if $V=\infty$ almost surely.

Popov asked whether the model with one frog per vertex is recurrent on the tree in which every vertex has degree $d$ \cite[Open Problem~4.1]{Pop03}. Hoffman, Johnson and Junge proved that the frog model on $\T_2$ is recurrent and that the frog model on $\T_d$ is transient for every $d\ge5$ \cite[Theorem~1]{HJJ17}, and they left the two remaining degrees as a conjecture.

\begin{conjecture}[{Hoffman, Johnson and Junge \cite[Conjecture~2, p.~2828]{HJJ17}}]\label{conj:HJJ}\nolean{a quoted conjecture, whose two cases are Theorems main and three}
The frog model on $\T_d$ is recurrent a.s. for $d = 3$ and transient a.s. for $d = 4$.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}\lean{FrogModel.transient_four, FrogModel.meanVisits_four_le, FrogModel.Transient, FrogModel.transient_four_of_data, FrogModel.meanVisits_four_le_of_data, FrogModel.meanVisits_four_le_exact_of_data}\computed{code/gp/small_checks.gp, code/gp/small_checks.out}
For $d=4$, $V<\infty$ almost surely, and $\E V\le5.756$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:three}\lean{FrogModel.recurrent_three, FrogModel.Recurrent, FrogModel.D3.Assembly.recurrent_three_of_m1}
For $d=3$, $V=\infty$ almost surely.
\end{theorem}

So both halves of Conjecture~\ref{conj:HJJ} hold.

\subsection*{What was known}
For $d\ge5$ Hoffman, Johnson and Junge compare the projection of the frog model on the distance to the root with a branching random walk. For $d\ge6$ one type suffices, and a normalized exponential weight of the configuration is a martingale \cite[Proposition~18]{HJJ17}; for $d=5$ the walk has $27$ types, whose transition probabilities are computed with computer assistance. The same method, with $6$ types, gives transience on the tree whose levels alternate between $5$ and $6$ children \cite[Proposition~19]{HJJ17}. They write that ``conceptually our approach could extend to a computer-assisted proof for transience when $d = 4$, but the demands of this theoretical program seem well beyond current processing power'' \cite[p.~2829]{HJJ17}. Their Conjecture~2 rests on simulations of the model with stunning fences \cite[Section~5]{HJJ17}: a frog that reaches depth $k$ stops there until all frogs have stopped, and the growth in $k$ of the number $A_{d,k}$ of frogs that pile up at depth $k$ suggests recurrence for $d=3$ and transience for $d=4$. This staged picture is the nearest precursor of steps~2 to~5 of the method below. With independent Poisson numbers of frogs of mean $\mu$ per vertex, the model is transient when $\mu<(d-1)^2/(4d)$ \cite[Proposition~15]{HJJ16}, which is $9/16$ for $d=4$, and the critical density lies between $0.24d$ and $2.28d$ for large $d$ \cite[Theorem~1]{JJ16}. Johnson and Junge extend transience for $d\ge5$ to every configuration of mean at most one per vertex by stochastic orders \cite[Corollary~7]{JJ18}, while for every $\mu$ some law of mean $\mu$ for the initial numbers of frogs makes the model on $\T_d$ transient \cite[Theorem~1.1]{JR19}. Rosenberg proves recurrence on the tree whose levels alternate between $3$ and $2$ children \cite[Theorem~1.1]{Ros18}. In their study of the cover time, Hoffman, Johnson and Junge plant the $d$-ary tree of height $H$ below an extra vertex $y$ and freeze the frogs that reach $y$ \cite[Proposition~4.3]{HJJ19}, and in the proof of \cite[Corollary~4.4]{HJJ19} they sort the frogs that return to the root up to a time $2^j$ into generations, the mean of each generation at most $1+\mu$ times the mean frozen count times the mean of the previous one, with $\mu$ the mean number of sleeping frogs per vertex. Theorem~\ref{thm:A} runs this argument for all time and charges the frogs of the children of the root once in all instead of once per generation (Lemma~\ref{lem:children}), which turns the condition $2x_d<1$ of one frog per vertex into $x_d<1$; this matters here, since the bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:B} exceeds $1/2$. On $\mathbb{Z}^2$ with drift, D\"obler, Gantert, H\"ofelsauer, Popov and Weidner stop the frogs at hyperplanes and refill, before each particle branches, every vertex whose frog has woken, which gives a branching random walk with independent offspring that visits every vertex the frog model visits \cite[proof of Theorem~1.1(ii)]{DGHPW18}; Lemma~\ref{lem:refresh} plays the same role in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, where it charges each excursion with an independent copy of the frozen count. The cases $d=3$ and $d=4$ are stated as open in \cite{JJ16,BFJJT19,MR24,MW20,AJ25,Fra26}, and simulations of the model with $d=4$ point to transience \cite[Section~5]{HJJ17}. For $d=2$, Hoffman, Johnson and Junge compare the frog model with a self-similar model, whose frogs follow non-backtracking walks that may stop and whose number of returns to the root lies below that of the frog model; the generating function of that number is a fixed point of an explicit operator, monotone on a large class of functions, whose iterates tend to $0$ \cite[Section~2]{HJJ17}. Rosenberg adapts this method to the tree whose levels alternate between $3$ and $2$ children \cite[Section~2]{Ros18}, and writes that on $\T_3$, which appears close to criticality, a self-similar model below the frog model seems to stop being recurrent \cite[Section~1]{Ros18}. The simulations with stunning fences \cite[Section~5]{HJJ17} suggest recurrence for $d=3$ through the expected number of visits to the root, which is infinite if $\sum_kd^{-k}\E A_{d,k}=\infty$; Theorem~\ref{thm:crit} turns a linear growth of the mean of a truncated count into recurrence by a second moment bound and a zero-one argument on the planted tree, in the place of the zero-one law of \cite[Theorem~4]{HJJ17} (a zero-one law for general frog processes is \cite[Theorem~13]{KZ17}). Related work treats the frog model with drift on $\T_d$ \cite{BFJJT19,GTW22,BJL24,Mat24}, on Galton-Watson trees \cite{MR24,MW20} and on non-amenable trees \cite{MR20}, the cover time and the susceptibility on finite $d$-ary trees \cite{HJJ19,Her18}, and frogs with geometric lifetimes on trees \cite{GR18,Pen24}.

\subsection*{The method for $d=4$}
Freeze at $y$ the frogs of a planted copy of $\T_4$ and let $x_4$ be the mean number of frozen frogs (Definition~\ref{def:planted}). The proof has five steps.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The bound $\E V\le(d+1)x_d/(1-x_d)$ whenever $x_d<1$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, Section~\ref{sec:thmA}). The visits to the root are sorted into generations of excursions from the root, and each excursion into a subtree is charged with an independent copy of the frozen count.
\item The frozen count is the first value of a random response curve, the number of frozen frogs as a function of the number of frogs started at the root, and the law of the response curve of the tree truncated at depth $K$ is the image of the law at depth $K-1$ under an explicit map $\Psi$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:recursion}, Section~\ref{sec:curves}). The map is monotone for the stochastic order.
\item The response curve is dominated by a curve made of independent blocks of $J$ values, each with the law of the first $J$ values of the curve itself (Proposition~\ref{prop:renewal}, Section~\ref{sec:renewal}). So every law $H^*$ of blocks of $J$ values that dominates its own image under the induced map $\Phi_J$ bounds $x_d$ by the mean of its first coordinate (Theorem~\ref{thm:super}).
\item For $d=4$ and a law $H^*$ given by a finite table, the image $\Phi_4(H^*)$ is the output law of a Markov chain on states made of the four children and the frogs waiting at the root. A potential bounds the exponential moments of its output, replacing a child by a dominating one can only raise the output, and finitely many inequalities, on the table and on two linear systems over a finite set of states, imply $\Phi_4(H^*)\stle H^*$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:check}, Section~\ref{sec:criterion}).
\item A table with $31$ rows and data on $159786$ states satisfy these inequalities, with $\E B^*(1)=\cst=0.53513682\ldots$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:cert}, Section~\ref{sec:certificate}). So $x_4\le\cst$, and $\E V\le5\cst/(1-\cst)<5.756$.
\end{enumerate}
The bound needed, $x_4<1$, holds with room, since the table law has mean $0.535$. What makes a finite table enough is that the recursion of step~2 keeps the dependence between the frogs returned by one subtree: the response curve records how many frogs come back for every number of frogs sent in, and blocks of four values of it already suffice in step~5.

\subsection*{The method for $d=3$}
For $d=3$ the frozen count is infinite with positive probability, and the proof shows it by counting the frogs frozen at $y$ when only the frogs of the first $m$ levels are present. Let $G_m(1)$ be that count when one frog enters a planted tree of height $m$ (Definition~\ref{def:plantedcurve}). The proof has four steps.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If the mean of the frozen count of the truncated model grows linearly in $m$, then the model is recurrent, for every $d\ge2$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:crit}, Section~\ref{sec:crit}), by the second moment method and a zero-one argument. One step from the root turns a linear bound on $\E G_m(1)$ into one on that mean (Lemma~\ref{lem:rootstep}).
\item The planted curve of height $m+1$ is the output of a closure at its root whose three children answer with planted curves of height $m$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:closid}, Section~\ref{sec:closure}). Optional stopping along the entries into a child, with one coin per entry, compares the deficit $\Delta_m(k)=(m+1+k)/3-\E G_m(k)$ against the mean of the all-awake count from one height to the next.
\item Finite tables of upper bounds on the distribution functions and the deficits of the planted curves are carried from height $m$ to height $m+1$ by an explicit map $\Phi$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:step}, Sections~\ref{sec:onebounds} and~\ref{sec:states}). A lower model, which reads the frog paths with caps and ghost walks of bounded depth and whose count has a law given by a finite recursion, gives a valid table at height $100$ (Section~\ref{sec:lower}); $505$ certified steps of $\Phi$ and one table that $\Phi$ maps below itself give valid tables at every height of $[605,1778279]$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:m1run}(2)).
\item Above $m_1=1778279$, an induction on the height that tracks $\E G_m(J)$ for one growing index $J$ keeps $\E G_m(1)\ge\frac35\mu_m(1)=(m+2)/5$, by two inequalities checked on $95$ intervals up to $10^{30}$ and proved beyond (Theorem~\ref{thm:dprime}, Section~\ref{sec:dprime}).
\end{enumerate}
The deficit at one entrant is about $0.44$ of its scale at height $100$ and below $0.135$ of it from height $605$ to $m_1$, while the induction needs $0.4$: the certified steps are what bring the deficit down.

\section{Frozen frogs}\label{sec:reduction}

This section reduces Theorem~\ref{thm:main} to two statements about one planted subtree. Theorem~\ref{thm:A} bounds the expected number of visits to the root by the mean number of frogs that a planted subtree sends to the parent of its root, and Theorem~\ref{thm:B} bounds this mean when $d=4$. Throughout, $d\ge2$, $\Nb=\{0,1,2,\dots\}\cup\{\infty\}$, and $\T(v)$ is the subtree of $\T_d$ made of $v$ and its descendants.

\begin{definition}[the planted tree]\label{def:planted}
The planted tree $\Ts$ is $\T_d$ with one more vertex $y$, joined to the root of $\T_d$ only; we write $r$ for that root, so that $r$ has degree $d+1$ in $\Ts$ and $y$ is a leaf. The frog model on $\Ts$ has one awake frog at $r$, no frog at $y$ and one sleeping frog at every other vertex. The frogs follow independent simple random walks on $\Ts$, and a frog stops for good at its first visit to $y$: it is \emph{frozen}. Let $X\in\Nb$ be the number of frogs that are woken and frozen, and $x_d=\E X$.
\end{definition}

For a child $j$ of the root of $\T_d$, the graph $\T(j)\cup\{\varnothing\}$ is a copy of $\Ts$ in which $j$ plays the role of $r$ and $\varnothing$ that of $y$ (Figure~\ref{fig:planted}). Every vertex of $\T(j)$ has degree $d+1$ both in $\T_d$ and in this copy, so a simple random walk on $\T_d$ from a vertex of $\T(j)$, stopped at its first visit to $\varnothing$, is a simple random walk on $\Ts$ stopped at $y$. This is how $X$ enters: the frogs that leave a subtree of the root through its top edge are the frozen frogs of a planted tree.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:A}\lean{FrogModel.theoremA, FrogModel.transient_of_meanX_lt_one, FrogModel.meanX, FrogModel.frozenCount}
Let $d\ge2$ and suppose that $x_d<1$. Then
\[
\E V\le \frac{(d+1)\,x_d}{1-x_d},
\]
and the frog model on $\T_d$ is transient.
\end{theorem}

Each return of a frog to the root starts an excursion into one of the $d$ subtrees of the root, which can wake frogs there and send some of them back to the root; Theorem~\ref{thm:A} charges each such excursion with an independent copy of $X$. The numerator $(d+1)x_d$ bounds the expected number of returns caused by the frog of the root and by the frogs of the $d$ children of the root, each counted once, and the factor $1/(1-x_d)$ sums the returns of later generations. The freezing at $y$ is what makes this an upper bound: a frog frozen at $y$ is a return to the root of $\T_d$, and everything it does afterwards is charged to the next generation.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:B}\lean{FrogModel.lemmaXFour_of_data, FrogModel.LemmaX.lemmaXFour_of, FrogModel.LemmaX.LemmaXFour, FrogModel.LemmaX.meanHstar_holds}
For $d=4$,
\[
x_4\le\cst,\qquad \cst=\frac{9337230319347}{17448304640000}=0.53513682\ldots
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
By Theorem~\ref{thm:B}, $x_4\le\cst<1$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:A} applies with $d=4$. Since $t\mapsto t/(1-t)$ is increasing on $[0,1)$, it gives
\[
\E V\le\frac{5x_4}{1-x_4}\le\frac{5\cst}{1-\cst}=\frac{46686151596735}{8111074320653}=5.7558530\ldots<5.756,
\]
and in particular $V<\infty$ almost surely. This is \decl{FrogModel.transient_four} in the formalization, with \texttt{FrogModel.meanVisits\_four\_le} for the bound on $\E V$.
\end{proof}

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B} occupies Sections~\ref{sec:curves} to~\ref{sec:certificate}. Section~\ref{sec:curves} writes the number of frozen frogs of $\Ts$ as the first value of a random response curve, whose law satisfies an exact recursion through the subtrees of the children of $r$. Section~\ref{sec:renewal} shows that a law $H^*$ of blocks of response values that dominates its own image under the recursion bounds $x_4$ by the mean of its first coordinate. Section~\ref{sec:criterion} turns the domination of a law given by a finite table into finitely many inequalities, and Section~\ref{sec:certificate} gives the table and checks the inequalities.

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}}\label{sec:thmA}

This section proves Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, by the argument of the proof of \cite[Corollary~4.4]{HJJ19} run for all time. The visits to the root are sorted into generations, called stages: the frog of the root forms stage $1$, and an excursion from the root that starts at the end of an excursion of stage $i$ belongs to stage $i+1$, together with everything it wakes. Lemma~\ref{lem:visits} bounds $V$ by the number of excursions of all stages, and Lemma~\ref{lem:stage} bounds the expected number of excursions of stage $i+1$ by $x_d$ times the expected number of excursions of stage $i$ plus a correction, whose total Lemma~\ref{lem:children} bounds by $d$. The independence that Lemma~\ref{lem:stage} needs comes from Lemma~\ref{lem:refresh}.

\subsection{Reachability}\label{sec:reach}
The set of frogs that wake does not depend on the times at which the frogs move, only on their paths. Lemma~\ref{lem:reach}, used throughout the paper, says so.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:reach}\lean{FrogModel.woken_iff_reach, FrogModel.encard_visits}
Consider the frog model on $\T_d$ with given paths $S_v$, $S_v(0)=v$. Let $D$ be the directed graph on the frogs with an arc $f_u\to f_w$ whenever $w\neq\varnothing$ and $w=S_u(n)$ for some $n\ge0$. Then a frog is ever woken if and only if it is reachable from $f_\varnothing$ in $D$, and
\[
V=\#\{(v,n):\ f_v \text{ is reachable from } f_\varnothing,\ n\ge1,\ S_v(n)=\varnothing\}.
\]
\end{lemma}

The same holds on $\Ts$, with arcs to the vertices other than $r$ and $y$ and with the stopped paths. There we take it as the definition of the number of frozen frogs: $X$ is the number of frogs reachable from the frog at $r$ whose path visits $y$.

\begin{proof}
If $f_u$ is woken and $f_u\to f_w$, then $f_u$ is at $w$ at some time, so $f_w$ is woken by then; by induction along a path of $D$, every frog reachable from $f_\varnothing$ is woken. Conversely, by induction on the time at which it wakes, a frog woken at a time $t>0$ was woken by an awake frog $f_u$ at its vertex at time $t$, so $f_u\to f_w$ with $f_u$ woken before $t$. A pair $(v,t)$ counted in~\eqref{eq:V} is mapped to $(v,t-s_v)$, injectively, and the image is the set on the right. This is \decl{FrogModel.woken_iff_reach} in the formalization, for the first claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Pieces, segments and stages}\label{sec:stages}
The paths of the frogs are cut at their visits to the root. A \emph{segment} is a pair $\sigma=(u,k)$ of a vertex $u$ and an integer $k\ge0$. To each segment we attach a \emph{piece} $\omega_\sigma=(\omega_\sigma(n))_{n\ge0}$, a simple random walk on $\T_d$ started at $u$ if $k=0$ and at $\varnothing$ if $k\ge1$, all pieces independent. The \emph{cut} of a piece is $\tau_\sigma=\min\{n\ge1:\omega_\sigma(n)=\varnothing\}\in\Nb$, and $\sigma$ is \emph{closed} when $\tau_\sigma<\infty$. The \emph{glued path} $S_u$ follows $\omega_{(u,0)}$ up to its cut, then $\omega_{(u,1)}$ up to its cut, and so on, up to the first piece with an infinite cut.

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A} builds the frog model from the pieces, which Lemma~\ref{lem:glue} allows.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:glue}\lean{FrogModel.map_glue, FrogModel.lintegral_visits_eq}
The glued paths $(S_u)_u$ are independent simple random walks on $\T_d$, $S_u$ started at $u$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By the strong Markov property of simple random walk at its successive visits to $\varnothing$, a simple random walk from $u$ is the concatenation of independent pieces with these laws, cut at their returns to $\varnothing$. The pieces of different frogs are independent. This is \decl{FrogModel.map_glue} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

So the frog model may be built from the pieces, and we do so in the rest of this section. Two kinds of arcs join segments. A segment $\sigma$ \emph{wakes} the segment $(w,0)$ when $w\neq\varnothing$ and $\omega_\sigma(n)=w$ for some $n\le\tau_\sigma$; the \emph{successor} of a closed segment $(u,k)$ is $(u,k+1)$. For a set $B$ of segments, its \emph{closure} is the smallest set of segments that contains $B$ and contains every segment woken by one of its members. The stages are
\[
\cS_0=\emptyset,\qquad \cS_{i+1}=\text{the closure of } B_i,\qquad B_i=\{(\varnothing,0)\}\cup\cS_i\cup\{(u,k+1):(u,k)\in\cS_i \text{ closed}\},
\]
and the segments that enter at stage $i+1$ are $Z_i=B_i\setminus\cS_i$, of number $R_i=|Z_i|\in\Nb$. So $Z_0=\{(\varnothing,0)\}$, $R_0=1$, and for $i\ge1$ every segment of $Z_i$ is the successor of a closed segment of $\cS_i$, a piece that starts at the root. The stages increase with~$i$.

A piece that starts at the root steps first to a child $j$ of the root, and stays in $\T(j)$ until its cut; a piece $\omega_{(w,0)}$ with $w\in\T(j)$ stays in $\T(j)$ until its cut. For a child $j$ of the root, let $Z_i(j)$ be the set of segments of $Z_i$ whose piece steps first to $j$, and
\[
\cA_j=\begin{cases}Z_i(j)\cup\{(j,0)\} & \text{if } Z_i(j)\neq\emptyset \text{ and } (j,0)\notin\cS_i,\\ Z_i(j) & \text{otherwise},\end{cases}
\qquad W_{i+1}=\#\{j:\ Z_i(j)\neq\emptyset,\ (j,0)\notin\cS_i\}.
\]
So $\sum_j|\cA_j|=R_i+W_{i+1}$. The segment $(j,0)$ is listed apart because a frog entering $\T(j)$ for the first time also wakes the frog of $j$, which then starts excursions of its own: $W_{i+1}$ counts the children whose frog is first woken at stage $i+1$.

\subsection{The lemmas and the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}}
Lemma~\ref{lem:visits} is the bound of $V$ by the stages, Lemma~\ref{lem:refresh} the independence statement that Lemma~\ref{lem:stage} uses, and Lemma~\ref{lem:children} the bound on the corrections $W_{i+1}$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:visits}\lean{FrogModel.encard_visits_glue_le, FrogModel.reach_mem_stage, FrogModel.visits_subset}
For every family of pieces, $V\le\sum_{i\ge1}R_i$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[refresh]\label{lem:refresh}\lean{FrogModel.Stage.map_refresh, FrogModel.Stage.lintegral_refresh, FrogModel.Stage.IsStoppingSet, FrogModel.Stage.IsDetermined}
Let $(E_s,\mu_s)_{s\in I}$ be probability spaces, $I$ countable, and $P=\bigotimes_s\mu_s$ on $\Omega=\prod_sE_s$. Let $\Gamma$ map each $\omega\in\Omega$ to a subset $\Gamma(\omega)$ of $I$, with $\{s\in \Gamma\}$ measurable for every $s$, and such that $\Gamma(\omega')=\Gamma(\omega)$ whenever $\omega'$ agrees with $\omega$ on $\Gamma(\omega)$. Let $G$ be a measurable event determined by $\Gamma$: $\omega\in G$ if and only if $\omega'\in G$, whenever $\omega'$ agrees with $\omega$ on $\Gamma(\omega)$. For $\omega,\omega'\in\Omega$ let $\omega^{\omega'}$ take the values of $\omega'$ on $\Gamma(\omega)$ and those of $\omega$ elsewhere. Then the image of $(P\otimes P)$ restricted to $G\times\Omega$ under $(\omega,\omega')\mapsto\omega^{\omega'}$ is $P(G)\,P$.
\end{lemma}

In words: on an event determined by a stopping set of coordinates, replacing the coordinates of the stopping set by fresh ones gives a family with the original law, independent of the event.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:stage}\lean{FrogModel.lemma31, FrogModel.stageR_succ_le, FrogModel.lintegral_enterSet, FrogModel.count_enterSet}
For every $i\ge0$, $\E R_{i+1}\le x_d\,(\E R_i+\E W_{i+1})$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:children}\lean{FrogModel.sum_stageW_le, FrogModel.child_mem_stage}
For every family of pieces, $\sum_{i\ge0}W_{i+1}\le d$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}]
Write $x=x_d$, $r_i=\E R_i$ and $w_i=\E W_i$, so that $r_0=1$, $r_{i+1}\le x(r_i+w_{i+1})$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:stage}, and $\sum_iw_{i+1}\le d$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:children}. Let $s_n=r_1+\dots+r_n$. Summing the recursion over $i<n$ gives $s_n\le x(1+s_{n-1}+d)$, and by induction on $n$, with every $s_n$ finite since $x<1$,
\[
s_n\le x(1+d)\,(1+x+\dots+x^{n-1})\le\frac{(d+1)x}{1-x}.
\]
By Lemmas~\ref{lem:glue} and~\ref{lem:visits} and monotone convergence, $\E V\le\sum_{i\ge1}r_i=\lim_ns_n$, which gives the bound. A random variable with finite mean is finite almost surely. This is \decl{FrogModel.theoremA} in the formalization, and \texttt{FrogModel.transient\_of\_meanX\_lt\_one} for the transience.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proofs of the lemmas}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:visits}]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:reach} and the gluing, $V$ counts the pairs $(u,k)$ such that $f_u$ is reachable from $f_\varnothing$ and the pieces $\omega_{(u,0)},\dots,\omega_{(u,k)}$ are closed: the $(k+1)$-th visit of $S_u$ to the root at a time $n\ge1$ is the end of the piece $\omega_{(u,k)}$. If $f_u$ is reachable, then $(u,0)$ lies in some stage, by induction along a path of $D$ (a segment of a stage that wakes $(w,0)$ puts it in the same stage, and the next segment of a closed segment of $\cS_m$ lies in $\cS_{m+1}$). Then $(u,k)\in\cS_{m+k}$ when $(u,0)\in\cS_m$ and $(u,0),\dots,(u,k-1)$ are closed, and the successor $(u,k+1)$ of the closed segment $(u,k)$ lies in some stage. Since $(u,k+1)\neq(\varnothing,0)$, the first stage that contains it is some $\cS_{i+1}$ with $i\ge1$, and $(u,k+1)\in Z_i$. The map $(u,k)\mapsto(u,k+1)$ is injective, so $V\le|\bigcup_{i\ge1}Z_i|\le\sum_{i\ge1}R_i$. This is \decl{FrogModel.encard_visits_glue_le} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:refresh}]
It suffices to compare the two measures on the cylinders $\{\omega:\omega_s\in A_s\text{ for } s\in F\}$, $F\subseteq I$ finite, which determine a measure on $\Omega$. Split the event according to the set $F\cap \Gamma(\omega)$: the cylinder event for $\omega^{\omega'}$ is the disjoint union over $C\subseteq F$ of
\[
G\cap\{F\cap \Gamma=C\}\cap\{\omega_s\in A_s,\ s\in F\setminus C\}\cap\{\omega'_s\in A_s,\ s\in C\}.
\]
The event $G_C=G\cap\{F\cap \Gamma=C\}$ does not change when the coordinates in $F\setminus C$ change: such a change leaves $\omega$ unchanged on $\Gamma(\omega)$, hence leaves $\Gamma$ and $G$ unchanged. So $G_C$ is independent of the coordinates in $F\setminus C$ under the product measure, and the term of $C$ has probability $P(G_C)\prod_{s\in F}\mu_s(A_s)$. Summing over $C$ gives $P(G)\prod_{s\in F}\mu_s(A_s)$. This is \decl{FrogModel.Stage.map_refresh} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:stage}]
\emph{A pathwise bound.} Fix the pieces $\omega$, a second family of pieces $\omega'$, and let $\tilde\omega=\omega^{\omega'}$ be $\omega$ with the pieces of the segments of $\cS_i$ replaced by those of $\omega'$, as in Lemma~\ref{lem:refresh} with $\Gamma=\cS_i$. For a child $j$ of the root and a segment $a\in\cA_j$, let $\eta_{j,a}$ be the following family of paths of $\Ts$, identified with $\T(j)\cup\{\varnothing\}$ as in Section~\ref{sec:reduction}: the frog at $r=j$ follows $\omega_a$, after its first step if $a$ starts at the root, and the frog at a vertex $jw\ne j$ follows $\tilde\omega_{(jw,0)}$, each stopped at its first visit to $y=\varnothing$. We show that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pathwise}
R_{i+1}\le\sum_j\sum_{a\in\cA_j}X(\eta_{j,a}).
\end{equation}
The members of $\cS_i$ that are closed have their successors in $B_i\subseteq\cS_{i+1}$, so every segment of $Z_{i+1}$ is the successor of a closed segment of $\cS_{i+1}\setminus\cS_i$, and $R_{i+1}$ is at most the number of closed segments of $\cS_{i+1}\setminus\cS_i$. Every segment $\tau$ of $\cS_{i+1}\setminus\cS_i$ is reached from $B_i$ by a chain of wake arcs; since $\cS_i$ is closed under these arcs, the chain may be taken outside $\cS_i$, and it starts in $Z_i$. Its first segment steps first to some child $j$, and the whole chain stays in $\T(j)$ until its cuts. Cut the chain at its last member in $\cA_j$, which exists since $Z_i(j)\subseteq\cA_j$: the rest is a chain from some $a\in\cA_j$ whose other members are segments $(jw,0)$ with $w$ nonempty, outside $\cS_i$, since $(j,0)$ belongs to $\cA_j$ when it lies outside $\cS_i$ and some member of $Z_i(j)$ is there. On these segments $\tilde\omega=\omega$, so the chain is a chain of the reachability graph of $\eta_{j,a}$ from its frog at $r$, and the closed segments on it are frogs of $\eta_{j,a}$ whose path visits $y$. Summing over $j$ and $a$ gives~\eqref{eq:pathwise}: replacing pieces inside $\cS_i$ only adds frogs that the chains do not need.

\emph{Expectation.} Integrate~\eqref{eq:pathwise} over $\omega'$ and then over $\omega$, term by term in $(j,a)$, and write the term of $(j,a)$ as the event $\{a\in\cA_j\}$ times $X(\eta_{j,a})$. Suppose first that $a$ starts at the root. Then $\{a\in\cA_j\}$ is the intersection of $G=\{a\in Z_i\}$, an event determined by $\cS_i$, with the event that $\omega_a$ steps first to $j$; and $a\notin\cS_i$ on $G$, so the refresh does not touch $\omega_a$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:refresh}, on $G$ the family $\tilde\omega$ is a fresh family of pieces independent of $G$; for a fresh family, the first step of $\omega_a$ and the family $\eta_{j,a}$ are independent, the latter with the law of the frog model on $\Ts$, by the Markov property of $\omega_a$ at time $1$. So the term equals $\P(a\in\cA_j)\,x_d$. Suppose now that $a=(j,0)$. The event $\{(j,0)\in\cA_j\}$ is determined by the stopping set $\cS_i\cup Z_i$, on which it reads the first steps of the pieces of $Z_i$, and no segment $(v,0)$ with $v\ne\varnothing$ lies in $Z_i$; so $\eta_{j,(j,0)}$ is unchanged if the refresh also replaces the pieces of $Z_i$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:refresh} with $\Gamma=\cS_i\cup Z_i$ gives again $\P(a\in\cA_j)\,x_d$. Summing, $\E R_{i+1}\le x_d\,\E\sum_j|\cA_j|=x_d\,(\E R_i+\E W_{i+1})$. This is \decl{FrogModel.lemma31} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:children}]
If $j$ is counted in $W_{i+1}$, a segment of $Z_i$ steps first to $j$ and wakes $(j,0)$, so $(j,0)\in\cS_{i+1}\setminus\cS_i$. Since the stages increase, $j$ is counted for at most one $i$, and there are $d$ children. This is \decl{FrogModel.sum_stageW_le} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

\section{Response curves}\label{sec:curves}

This section writes $x_d$ as a limit of means of the first values of random curves, the response curves of $\Ts$ at finite depth (Lemma~\ref{lem:trunc}), and shows that their laws satisfy an exact recursion through the subtrees of the children of $r$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:recursion}). The recursion is a map $\Psi$ on laws of curves, built from a deterministic map (Lemma~\ref{lem:psi}), and it is monotone for the coupling order (Lemma~\ref{lem:mono}). The section ends with the block laws on which the finite criterion of Section~\ref{sec:criterion} works.

\subsection{Kill depth}
The depth $|v|$ of a vertex $v$ of $\Ts$ other than $y$ is its distance to $r$, and $V_K$ is the finite set of vertices with $1\le|v|\le K$. For $K\ge0$, the frog model on $\Ts$ \emph{at kill depth} $K$ is the frog model of Definition~\ref{def:planted} in which every path is stopped at its first visit to $y$, where the frog is frozen, or to a vertex of depth $K+1$, where it is \emph{killed}. A killed frog stops on arrival, so the frogs at depth $K+1$ or more are never woken. As in Lemma~\ref{lem:reach}, let $X^{(K)}$ be the number of frogs reachable from the frog at $r$, along the arcs to the vertices of $V_K$ visited by the stopped paths, whose stopped path is frozen.

Lemma~\ref{lem:trunc}, used in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:super}, reduces $x_d$ to the model at finite kill depth.

\begin{lemma}[truncation]\label{lem:trunc}\lean{FrogModel.LemmaX.truncation_holds, FrogModel.LemmaX.meanTruncation_holds, FrogModel.LemmaX.Truncation, FrogModel.LemmaX.MeanTruncation, FrogModel.LemmaX.frozenCountK}
For every family of paths, $X^{(K)}\le X^{(K+1)}$ for every $K$, and $X^{(K)}$ increases to $X$. Hence $x_d=\sup_K\E X^{(K)}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The path stopped at kill depth $K$ is an initial segment of the path stopped at kill depth $K+1$. So every arc of the reachability graph at depth $K$ is an arc at depth $K+1$, and a frog frozen at depth $K$ is frozen at depth $K+1$. A frog frozen in the model without killing is reached from the frog at $r$ along a finite chain of frogs, each arc witnessed at a finite time of the path of its tail, and its own path up to $y$ is finite; these finitely many path prefixes visit vertices of bounded depth, so it is frozen at kill depth $K$ for every large $K$. The mean follows by monotone convergence. This is \decl{FrogModel.LemmaX.truncation_holds} in the formalization, and \texttt{FrogModel.LemmaX.meanTruncation\_holds} for the mean.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Curves and the coupling order}
A \emph{curve} is a non-decreasing map $G:\N\to\Nb$ with $G(0)=0$, and $\cC$ is the set of curves, ordered pointwise. For $J\ge1$, $\cC_J$ is the set of non-decreasing vectors $B=(B(1),\dots,B(J))\in\Nb^J$, ordered componentwise, with the convention $B(0)=0$. For probability laws $P,P'$ on $\cC$ or on $\cC_J$, we write $P\stle P'$ if there is a coupling $(G,G')$ of $P$ and $P'$ with $G\le G'$ almost surely. The restriction $P|_J$ of a law $P$ on $\cC$ is the law of $(G(1),\dots,G(J))$; if $P\stle P'$ then $P|_J\stle P'|_J$. On $\cC_J$, which is countable, the order $\stle$ is transitive: if $\pi_1$ couples $P\stle Q$ and $\pi_2$ couples $Q\stle R$, then $\pi(B,B'')=\sum_{B'}\pi_1(B,B')\,\pi_2(B',B'')/Q(B')$, the sum over the atoms of $Q$, couples $P\stle R$.

Let $K\ge0$, and let $a_1,a_2,\dots$ be frogs placed at $r$, all awake, with independent simple random walk paths from $r$, independent of the paths of the sleeping frogs of $V_K$; at kill depth $K$ there is no other frog at $r$. The \emph{response curve} $G_K$ at kill depth $K$ is
\[
G_K(m)=\text{the number of frogs reachable from } \{a_1,\dots,a_m\} \text{ whose stopped path is frozen},
\]
the frogs $a_1,\dots,a_m$ included. The reachable set increases with $m$, so $G_K$ is a curve, with $G_K(m)\le m+|V_K|$. Let $N_K$ be the law of $G_K$ on $\cC$ and $E_K=N_K|_J$. If $a_1$ follows the path of the frog at $r$, then $G_K(1)=X^{(K)}$; so $G_K(1)$ has the law of $X^{(K)}$.

\subsection{The map \texorpdfstring{$\Psi$}{Psi}}
The map $\Psi$ assembles the response of $\Ts$ from the responses of the subtrees of the $d$ children of $r$ and from the directions of the steps taken at $r$. Fix curves $G_1,\dots,G_d$, indexed by the children $c$ of $r$, and directions $D_1,D_2,\dots$ in $\{0,1,\dots,d\}$, where $0$ is the step to $y$ and $c$ the step to the child $c$. For $n\in\Nb$ let $n_c(n)=\#\{k\le n:D_k=c\}$, let $F_c(0)=0$ and $F_c(m)=G_c(m+1)$ for $m\ge1$, extended to $\Nb$ by $F_c(\infty)=\sup_mF_c(m)$, and for $m\in\N$ let
\[
\Lambda_m(n)=m+\sum_{c=1}^dF_c\bigl(n_c(n)\bigr),\qquad n\in\Nb.
\]
The shift in $F_c$ is the frog of the child $c$: the first step from $r$ into $c$ wakes it, and it is the first of the frogs that start at $c$. The map $\Lambda_m$ is non-decreasing on $\Nb$. Let $\nu(m)$ be the least $n\in\Nb$ with $\Lambda_m(n)\le n$, which exists since $\Lambda_m(\infty)\le\infty$, and
\[
\Psi(G_1,\dots,G_d;D)(m)=\#\{k\le\nu(m):\ D_k=0\}.
\]
In the frog model, $\nu(m)$ is the number of steps taken at $r$ when $m$ frogs start there: each step from $r$ uses one frog, and the frogs at $r$ are the $m$ initial ones and those returned by the children.

Lemma~\ref{lem:psi} collects the properties of $\nu$ and of $\Psi$ that the proofs of Lemmas~\ref{lem:recursion}, \ref{lem:mono} and~\ref{lem:root} use.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:psi}\lean{FrogModel.LemmaX.psiLeastFixed_holds, FrogModel.LemmaX.psiIterate_holds, FrogModel.LemmaX.psiStep_holds, FrogModel.LemmaX.psiCurve_holds, FrogModel.LemmaX.psiMono_holds, FrogModel.LemmaX.psiZero_holds}
Let $G=\Psi(G_1,\dots,G_d;D)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The number $\nu(m)$ is a fixed point of $\Lambda_m$, it lies below every fixed point of $\Lambda_m$, and it is the supremum of the iterates $\Lambda_m^t(0)$, $t\ge0$.
\item For every integer $k<\nu(m)$, $k<\Lambda_m(k)$.
\item The function $G$ is a curve.
\item If $G_c\le G'_c$ pointwise for every $c$, then $\nu(m)\le\nu'(m)$ and $G(m)\le G'(m)$ for every $m$, where $\nu'$ and $G'$ are built from $G'_1,\dots,G'_d$ and the same directions.
\item If $G_1=\dots=G_d=0$, then $\nu(m)=m$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) The set $\{n:\Lambda_m(n)\le n\}$ is mapped into itself by the non-decreasing map $\Lambda_m$, so $\Lambda_m(\nu(m))$ belongs to it and $\Lambda_m(\nu(m))\ge\nu(m)$ by minimality; hence $\nu(m)$ is a fixed point, the least one (Knaster and Tarski). The iterates $\Lambda_m^t(0)$ increase, stay below $\nu(m)$, and their supremum $n^*$ satisfies $\Lambda_m(n^*)\le n^*$, because $n\mapsto F_c(n_c(n))$ commutes with increasing suprema; so $n^*=\nu(m)$. (2) If $\Lambda_m(k)\le k$ then $\nu(m)\le k$. (3) $\nu(0)=0$ since $\Lambda_0(0)=0$, and $\Lambda_m$ increases with $m$, so $\nu$ and $G$ are non-decreasing. (4) $\Lambda_m\le\Lambda'_m$ pointwise, so a fixed point of $\Lambda'_m$ satisfies $\Lambda_m(n)\le n$, which gives $\nu(m)\le\nu'(m)$; and $n\mapsto\#\{k\le n:D_k=0\}$ is non-decreasing. (5) $\Lambda_m$ is the constant $m$. This is \decl{FrogModel.LemmaX.psiLeastFixed_holds} in the formalization, and the next five declarations for (1) to (5).
\end{proof}

For a probability law $P$ on $\cC$, let $G_1,\dots,G_d$ be independent with law $P$ and $D_1,D_2,\dots$ independent and uniform on $\{0,1,\dots,d\}$, independent of the $G_c$, and let $\Psi(P)$ be the law of $\Psi(G_1,\dots,G_d;D)$. Let $\delta_0$ be the point mass at the zero curve.

Lemma~\ref{lem:recursion} is the recursion of this section, and the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:super} uses it at every kill depth.

\begin{lemma}[exact recursion]\label{lem:recursion}\lean{FrogModel.Recursion.curveLawSucc_holds, FrogModel.Recursion.curveLawZero_holds, FrogModel.Recursion.CurveLawSucc, FrogModel.Recursion.CurveLawZero, FrogModel.Recursion.curveLaw, FrogModel.Recursion.psiLaw, FrogModel.Pool.mapPoolSeq_holds, FrogModel.Pool.mapPoolRead_holds}
The laws satisfy $N_0=\Psi(\delta_0)$, and $N_K=\Psi(N_{K-1})$ for every $K\ge1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Pools.} Build the model at kill depth $K$ from the following independent random elements: the directions $D_1,D_2,\dots$ of the successive steps taken at $r$; for each child $c$, a sequence $P_{c,1},P_{c,2},\dots$ of independent \emph{entry pieces}, simple random walks from $c$ stopped at their first visit to $r$ or to depth $K+1$; and for each child $c$ the paths of the frogs of $\T(c)$, stopped at $r$ or at depth $K+1$. The frogs at $r$ form a queue. The frog at its head takes the next unused direction $D_k$: if $D_k=0$ it is frozen, and if $D_k=c$ and this is the $m$-th step from $r$ into $c$, its excursion in $\T(c)$ is $P_{c,m}$; a frog that returns to $r$ joins the queue. Each new piece is the first unused element of one pool, chosen by a rule that depends only on the pieces already used; by induction on the number of pieces used, these pieces, listed in the order of use, are independent with the laws of their pools. The path of a frog is the concatenation of its pieces, a direction at $r$ and then an excursion from a child back to $r$, and so on; by the strong Markov property at the visits to $r$, the paths are independent simple random walks with the right stopping. So this is the frog model on $\Ts$ at kill depth $K$.

\emph{Children.} After $m\ge1$ entries into $\T(c)$, the frogs that have started at $c$ are the frog of $c$ and the $m$ entering frogs. By Lemma~\ref{lem:reach} applied to $\T(c)\cup\{r\}$, with $r$ in the role of $y$, the number of frogs returned from $\T(c)$ to $r$ is $G_c(m+1)$, where $G_c(i)$ is the number of frozen frogs of $\T(c)\cup\{r\}$ when the frog of $c$ and the pieces $P_{c,1},\dots,P_{c,i-1}$ start at $c$; it does not depend on how the entries into $c$ are interleaved with those into the other children. The graph $\T(c)\cup\{r\}$ is a copy of $\Ts$ with root $c$, and depth $K+1$ below $r$ is depth $K$ below $c$; so $G_c$ is the response curve at kill depth $K-1$, with law $N_{K-1}$. The curves $G_c$ are independent of each other and of the directions.

\emph{Fixed point.} With $m$ initial frogs, let $\nu$ be the number of steps taken at $r$. If the processing ends, every frog that came to $r$ has stepped, so $\nu=m+\sum_cF_c(n_c(\nu))=\Lambda_m(\nu)$. If $\nu'$ is a fixed point of $\Lambda_m$ and $k\le\nu'$ steps have been taken, a $(k+1)$-th step needs a frog, so $k+1\le\Lambda_m(k)\le\Lambda_m(\nu')=\nu'$. So the number of steps never exceeds a fixed point, $\nu=\nu(m)$, as in the least action principle of Bond and Levine for abelian networks \cite[Lemma~4.3]{BL16}, and if the processing does not end there is no finite fixed point and $\nu(m)=\infty$. The frozen frogs are the steps with $D_k=0$ among the first $\nu(m)$, so their number is $\Psi(G_1,\dots,G_d;D)(m)$. Processing $a_1,\dots,a_m$ to the end before $a_{m+1}$ is one processing order, so the curve built in this way is the nested curve $G_K$. For $K=0$ every step into a child is killed, and $F_c=0$. This is \decl{FrogModel.Recursion.curveLawSucc_holds} in the formalization, with the case $K=0$ in the previous declaration.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:mono} is used in Lemma~\ref{lem:blockmono} and in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:super}.

\begin{lemma}[monotonicity]\label{lem:mono}\lean{FrogModel.LemmaX.psiLawMono_holds, FrogModel.LemmaX.PsiLawMono, FrogModel.LemmaX.CouplingLE}
If $P\stle P'$ on $\cC$, then $\Psi(P)\stle\Psi(P')$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Take $d$ independent copies $(G_c,G'_c)$ of a coupling with $G_c\le G'_c$, and one sequence of directions for both sides. By Lemma~\ref{lem:psi}(4), $\Psi(G_1,\dots,G_d;D)\le\Psi(G'_1,\dots,G'_d;D)$ pointwise. This is \decl{FrogModel.LemmaX.psiLawMono_holds} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Block laws}
For a law $H$ on $\cC_J$, let $B_1,B_2,\dots$ be independent with law $H$, and let $\BR(H)$ be the law of the curve
\[
\BR(B_1,B_2,\dots)(qJ+s)=B_1(J)+\dots+B_q(J)+B_{q+1}(s),\qquad q\ge0,\ 0\le s<J,
\]
made of the blocks laid end to end. Let $\Phi_J(H)=\Psi(\BR(H))|_J$.

Lemma~\ref{lem:blockmono} carries the monotonicity to block laws, for the induction in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:super}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:blockmono}\lean{FrogModel.LemmaX.brLawMono_holds, FrogModel.LemmaX.phiLawMono_holds, FrogModel.LemmaX.restrictMono_holds, FrogModel.Order.brCurve_mono}
If $H\stle H'$ on $\cC_J$, then $\BR(H)\stle\BR(H')$ on $\cC$ and $\Phi_J(H)\stle\Phi_J(H')$ on $\cC_J$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Couple independent pairs of blocks $B_b\le B'_b$. The curve of the blocks is non-decreasing in each block, so $\BR(B_1,B_2,\dots)\le\BR(B'_1,B'_2,\dots)$; then Lemma~\ref{lem:mono}, and restriction to the first $J$ values. This is \decl{FrogModel.LemmaX.brLawMono_holds} in the formalization, and \texttt{FrogModel.LemmaX.phiLawMono\_holds} for $\Phi_J$.
\end{proof}

\section{Sub-renewal at the root}\label{sec:renewal}

Lemma~\ref{lem:recursion} is a recursion on laws of whole curves. The finite criterion of Section~\ref{sec:criterion} works with the first $J$ values only, and this section closes the recursion on them, at the cost of an inequality: by Proposition~\ref{prop:renewal}, the response curve at kill depth $K$ is dominated by a curve of independent blocks, each with the law $E_K$ of its own first $J$ values. Combined with Lemma~\ref{lem:recursion}, this makes $E_K$ a sub-solution of $\Phi_J$, in the sense that $E_K\stle\Phi_J(E_{K-1})$, and every law $H^*$ that dominates its own image under $\Phi_J$ then bounds $x_d$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:super}).

\begin{proposition}[sub-renewal at the root]\label{prop:renewal}\lean{FrogModel.LemmaX.claimA_holds, FrogModel.LemmaX.ClaimA}
For $K\ge0$ and $J\ge1$, $N_K\stle\BR(E_K)$ on $\cC$.
\end{proposition}

The proof plays blocks of $J$ frogs one after the other at $r$, each block on a tree in which the frogs woken by the previous block have been replaced by fresh ones. Fix $K\ge0$ and $J\ge1$, and let $I_J=V_K\cup\{1,\dots,J\}$, a finite set of labels. A \emph{block configuration} $\omega$ gives each label a path stopped at kill depth $K$: to $v\in V_K$ a path from $v$, that of the frog sleeping at $v$, and to $t\le J$ a path from $r$, that of the $t$-th frog started at $r$. Under its law $\mu_J$ the paths are independent simple random walks. As for the response curve, a label has an arc to every vertex of $V_K$ that its path visits; for $0\le s\le J$ let $\cW_s(\omega)$ be the set of labels reachable from $\{1,\dots,s\}$, let $\cW(\omega)=\cW_J(\omega)$ be the \emph{woken set} of the block, and let $B(\omega)\in\cC_J$ have coordinates
\[
B(\omega)(s)=\#\{l\in\cW_s(\omega):\ \text{the path of $l$ is frozen}\},\qquad 1\le s\le J.
\]
The response curve at $m\le J$ reads only the paths of $a_1,\dots,a_m$ and of the sleeping frogs, so $B$ has law $E_K$ under $\mu_J$.

Let $\omega_1,\omega_2,\dots$ be independent block configurations with law $\mu_J$. The \emph{root-refreshed configurations} are $\tilde\omega_1=\omega_1$ and, for $b\ge1$, the configuration $\tilde\omega_{b+1}$ that takes the values of $\omega_{b+1}$ on $\cW(\tilde\omega_b)$ and those of $\tilde\omega_b$ elsewhere; let $B_b=B(\tilde\omega_b)$. So block $b+1$ is played on the tree in which every vertex whose frog woke during block $b$ holds a new sleeping frog with a fresh path, every other vertex keeps the frog it held during block $b$, and $J$ new frogs start at $r$, since $\cW(\tilde\omega_b)$ contains $1,\dots,J$. The \emph{original configuration} $\hat\omega$ gives the frog sleeping at $v\in V_K$ the path $\omega_1(v)$, and the frog $a_{(b-1)J+t}$, for $b\ge1$ and $1\le t\le J$, the path $\omega_b(t)$. Let $G$ be the response curve of $\hat\omega$. Lemma~\ref{lem:refreshed} gives the three facts that the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:renewal} assembles.

\begin{lemma}[root-refreshed process]\label{lem:refreshed}\lean{FrogModel.LemmaX.claimAPathwise_holds, FrogModel.LemmaX.claimAIid_holds, FrogModel.LemmaX.claimAOrig_holds, FrogModel.LemmaX.ClaimAPathwise, FrogModel.LemmaX.ClaimAIid, FrogModel.LemmaX.ClaimAOrig, FrogModel.LemmaX.refreshSeq, FrogModel.LemmaX.blockWoken, FrogModel.LemmaX.origActive}
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every choice of the paths and every $m\ge0$, $G(m)\le\BR(B_1,B_2,\dots)(m)$.
\item The blocks $B_1,B_2,\dots$ are independent with law $E_K$.
\item The curve $G$ has law $N_K$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:renewal}]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:refreshed}(3) and~(2), $G$ has law $N_K$ and $\BR(B_1,B_2,\dots)$ has law $\BR(E_K)$, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:refreshed}(1) the first lies below the second at every sample point. So the law of the pair is a coupling of $N_K\stle\BR(E_K)$. This is \decl{FrogModel.LemmaX.claimA_holds} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:refreshed}]
(1) Fix the paths and $m=qJ+s$ with $q\ge0$ and $0\le s<J$. Let $\cW^{(b)}=\cW(\tilde\omega_b)$ for $b\le q$ and $\cW^{(q+1)}=\cW_s(\tilde\omega_{q+1})$, and let $\cU$ be the set of vertices that lie in some $\cW^{(b)}$, $b\le q+1$. Each $\cW^{(b)}$ is closed under the arcs of $\tilde\omega_b$.

\emph{The sleeping frogs.} Let $v\in\cU$, and let $b_0$ be the least $b$ with $v\in\cW^{(b)}$. Every $b<b_0$ is at most $q$, and $v\notin\cW(\tilde\omega_b)$, so $\tilde\omega_{b+1}(v)=\tilde\omega_b(v)$; hence $\tilde\omega_{b_0}(v)=\omega_1(v)=\hat\omega(v)$. Since $\cW^{(b_0)}$ is closed under the arcs of $\tilde\omega_{b_0}$, every vertex of $V_K$ that the path $\hat\omega(v)$ visits lies in $\cU$.

\emph{The frogs started at $r$.} Let $i\le m$ and write $i=(b-1)J+t$ with $1\le t\le J$, so that $b\le q$, or $b=q+1$ and $t\le s$. For $b\ge2$ the label $t$ lies in $\cW(\tilde\omega_{b-1})$, so $\tilde\omega_b(t)=\omega_b(t)=\hat\omega(a_i)$, and this holds for $b=1$ as well. The label $t$ lies in $\cW^{(b)}$, so every vertex of $V_K$ that the path $\hat\omega(a_i)$ visits lies in $\cU$.

\emph{Closure and counting.} By the two steps, the set $\mathcal{R}=\{a_1,\dots,a_m\}\cup\cU$ is closed under the arcs of $\hat\omega$, and it contains $a_1,\dots,a_m$; so it contains every frog reachable from them. Send $a_i$, with $i=(b-1)J+t$, to the label $t$ of block $b$, and $v\in\cU$ to the label $v$ of block $b_0(v)$. This map from $\mathcal{R}$ to the disjoint union of the sets $\cW^{(b)}$ is injective, and by the two steps it keeps paths, hence frozen frogs. So
\[
G(m)\le\#\{f\in\mathcal{R}:\ f\text{ frozen}\}\le\sum_{b\le q}B_b(J)+B_{q+1}(s)=\BR(B_1,B_2,\dots)(m). % english-ok: formula
\]

(2) For a label $l$ and a vertex $v$, the event that the path of $l$ visits $v$ is a countable union of cylinder events, and so is the event that it is frozen; $\cW_s(\omega)$ is a closure over the finite set $I_J$; so the events $\{l\in\cW(\omega)\}$ are measurable, and so is $B$. Let $\omega'$ agree with $\omega$ on $\cW(\omega)$, and let $s\le J$. The set $\cW_s(\omega)$ lies in $\cW(\omega)$, contains $\{1,\dots,s\}$ and is closed under the arcs of $\omega'$, which are those of $\omega$ from its labels; so $\cW_s(\omega')\subseteq\cW_s(\omega)$. Then $\omega$ and $\omega'$ agree on $\cW_s(\omega')$, and the same argument gives the reverse inclusion. So $\cW_s(\omega')=\cW_s(\omega)$ for every $s$, and $B(\omega')=B(\omega)$. Thus $\cW$ satisfies the hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:refresh} on the product space of block configurations, and for every set $S\subseteq\cC_J$ the event $\{B\in S\}$ is determined by $\cW$. Lemma~\ref{lem:refresh} with $\Gamma=\cW$ and the event $\{B\in S\}$, evaluated on a measurable set $A$ of configurations, gives
\[
\mu_J\otimes\mu_J\bigl(B(\omega)\in S,\ \omega^{\omega'}\in A\bigr)=E_K(S)\,\mu_J(A),
\]
and since these rectangles determine a law on the product, $(B(\omega),\omega^{\omega'})$ has law $E_K\otimes\mu_J$ under $\mu_J\otimes\mu_J$. We show by induction on $n\ge1$ that $(B_1,\dots,B_{n-1},\tilde\omega_n)$ has law $E_K^{\otimes(n-1)}\otimes\mu_J$; for $n=1$ this is the law of $\omega_1$. The vector $(B_1,\dots,B_{n-1},\tilde\omega_n)$ is a function of $\omega_1,\dots,\omega_n$, hence independent of $\omega_{n+1}$, and $(B_n,\tilde\omega_{n+1})=(B(\tilde\omega_n),\tilde\omega_n^{\,\omega_{n+1}})$; so the step above applied to $(\tilde\omega_n,\omega_{n+1})$ gives the law at $n+1$. Hence $(B_1,\dots,B_n)$ has law $E_K^{\otimes n}$ for every $n$. The configuration $\tilde\omega_{n+1}$ is not independent of $\omega_1,\dots,\omega_n$, since it keeps the paths of $\tilde\omega_n$ outside $\cW(\tilde\omega_n)$; the induction uses only its independence from $B_1,\dots,B_n$.

(3) The configuration $\hat\omega$ takes distinct coordinates of $(\omega_b)_{b\ge1}$: the vertex coordinates of $\omega_1$ and the coordinates $1,\dots,J$ of every $\omega_b$. These are independent simple random walks from the right vertices, so $\hat\omega$ is the frog model at kill depth $K$ with the frogs $a_1,a_2,\dots$ at $r$, and $G$ has law $N_K$. This is \decl{FrogModel.LemmaX.claimAPathwise_holds} in the formalization, for (1), and \texttt{FrogModel.LemmaX.claimAIid\_holds} and \texttt{FrogModel.LemmaX.claimAOrig\_holds} for (2) and~(3).
\end{proof}

The refresh replaces the frogs woken during the last block only, and this is what the proof of~(2) needs. Refreshing at every vertex visited by any earlier block would serve the comparison~(1) equally well, but the set of these vertices is not determined by $\tilde\omega_b$ alone, so Lemma~\ref{lem:refresh} would not apply block by block, while the woken set $\cW$ of one block is a stopping set of its own configuration.

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B} applies Theorem~\ref{thm:super} with $J=4$ and the law of Section~\ref{sec:certificate}.

\begin{theorem}[supersolution principle]\label{thm:super}\lean{FrogModel.LemmaX.lemmaXOfSuper_holds, FrogModel.LemmaX.blockInduction_holds, FrogModel.LemmaX.meanOfBlockBound_holds, FrogModel.LemmaX.phiLaw, FrogModel.LemmaX.brLaw, FrogModel.LemmaX.blockLaw}
Let $J\ge1$ and let $H^*$ be a probability law on $\cC_J$ with $\Phi_J(H^*)\stle H^*$, and $B^*$ a random vector with law $H^*$. Then $E_K\stle H^*$ for every $K\ge0$, and $x_d\le\E B^*(1)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We show $E_K\stle H^*$ by induction on $K$. The point mass at the zero curve is below $\BR(H^*)$, so Lemmas~\ref{lem:recursion} and~\ref{lem:mono} give $N_0=\Psi(\delta_0)\stle\Psi(\BR(H^*))$, and restriction to the first $J$ values gives $E_0\stle\Phi_J(H^*)$. For $K\ge1$, Lemma~\ref{lem:recursion} gives $N_K=\Psi(N_{K-1})$, Proposition~\ref{prop:renewal} at kill depth $K-1$ gives $N_{K-1}\stle\BR(E_{K-1})$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:mono} gives $\Psi(N_{K-1})\stle\Psi(\BR(E_{K-1}))$; restricting to the first $J$ values, $E_K\stle\Phi_J(E_{K-1})$. If $E_{K-1}\stle H^*$, Lemma~\ref{lem:blockmono} and the hypothesis give
\[
E_K\stle\Phi_J(E_{K-1})\stle\Phi_J(H^*)\stle H^*.
\]
In both cases $E_K\stle H^*$ follows by transitivity on $\cC_J$. A coupling $(B,B')$ of $E_K\stle H^*$ has $B(1)\le B'(1)$, so $\E X^{(K)}=\E G_K(1)\le\E B^*(1)$ for every $K$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:trunc} gives $x_d\le\E B^*(1)$. The induction is the monotone iteration of Amann, whose iterates from below stay below a supersolution \cite[Theorem~6.1 and Corollary~6.3]{Ama76}. This is \decl{FrogModel.LemmaX.lemmaXOfSuper_holds} in the formalization, which takes Lemma~\ref{lem:recursion} and Proposition~\ref{prop:renewal} as hypotheses; the formal proof of transience supplies them from their declarations.
\end{proof}

Nothing asks $H^*$ to be the law of the first $J$ values of an actual curve: every law with $\Phi_J(H^*)\stle H^*$ will do. The recursion below $r$ runs on laws only, and Proposition~\ref{prop:renewal} is applied at one level at a time, to the children of $r$.

\section{A finite criterion}\label{sec:criterion}

From here on $d=4$: a frog at $r$ steps to $y$ or to each child with probability $1/5$. This section gives a class of laws $H^*$ on $\cC_J$ described by a finite table, and finitely many inequalities on the table and on two finite linear systems which imply $\Phi_J(H^*)\stle H^*$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:check}). The image $\Phi_J(H^*)$ is the output law of a Markov chain at the root (Lemmas~\ref{lem:child} and~\ref{lem:root}); a potential bounds the exponential moments of its output (Lemma~\ref{lem:potential}); and replacing the state of a child by a dominating one can only raise the output (Lemma~\ref{lem:maxchain}), which makes the chain finite. The statements are written for every table and proved here from conditions (C1) and (C2) below; the formalization proves them for the table of Section~\ref{sec:certificate}, the one the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B} uses.

\subsection{Table laws}\label{sec:table}
A \emph{table} consists of integers $J\ge2$, $T\ge1$ and $T'\ge T$; rationals $\eps,\rho\in(0,1)$, $\phi>1$ and $\kappa\ge1$; finite disjoint sets of labels $S_0=\{\mathrm{root}\}$, $S_1,\dots,S_{J-1}$ and $S_J=\{\mathrm{end}\}$; and for every $q<J$ and every $s\in S_q$ a \emph{row}, a finite list of transitions $(\pi,\delta,s')$ with $\pi>0$ rational, $\delta\ge0$ an integer and $s'\in S_{q+1}$. A path $\mathrm{root}=s_0,s_1,\dots,s_J=\mathrm{end}$ along transitions $(\pi_k,\delta_k,s_k)$ has probability $\pi_1\cdots\pi_J$ and gives the block $B(q)=\delta_1+\dots+\delta_q$. Let $P_{\mathrm{tab}}$ be the law of the block, $h=(1-\eps)P_{\mathrm{tab}}$, $Z_t=(t,\dots,t)\in\cC_J$, and
\[
H^*=h+\eps\sum_{t>T}(1-\rho)\rho^{\,t-T-1}\,\delta_{Z_t}.
\]
The vector $Z_t$ dominates every $B\in\cC_J$ with $B(J)\le t$. Let $\cC_{J,T}=\{B\in\cC_J:B(J)\le T\}$. The first condition is
\begin{itemize}
\item[(C1)] the $\pi$ of every row sum to $1$.
\end{itemize}
Under (C1), $H^*$ is a probability law on $\cC_J$, and for $B^*$ with law $H^*$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:EB1}
\E B^*(1)=(1-\eps)\sum\pi\,\delta+\eps\Bigl(T+\frac{1}{1-\rho}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
the sum over the transitions $(\pi,\delta,s_1)$ of the row of $\mathrm{root}$.

\subsection{The child chain}
A child of $r$ whose curve has law $\BR(H^*)$ is represented by a Markov chain. Its states are $\mathsf F$ (never entered), $\mathsf B$ (a block is complete), $(q,s)$ for $1\le q\le J-1$ and $s\in S_q$, and $(q,\mathsf{tail})$ for $1\le q\le J-1$. Each state has a row of transitions $(\pi,\delta,c')$, with $\pi$ the probability, $\delta$ the number of frogs delivered to $r$ and $c'$ the next state, where $(J,\cdot)$ is read as $\mathsf B$:
\begin{itemize}
\item from $\mathsf F$: $((1-\eps)\pi_1\pi_2,\ \delta_1+\delta_2,\ (2,s_2))$ for every pair of transitions $(\pi_1,\delta_1,s_1)$ of $\mathrm{root}$ and $(\pi_2,\delta_2,s_2)$ of $s_1$, and $(\eps(1-\rho)\rho^{\,t-T-1},\ t,\ (2,\mathsf{tail}))$ for every $t>T$;
\item from $\mathsf B$: $((1-\eps)\pi_1,\ \delta_1,\ (1,s_1))$ for every transition of $\mathrm{root}$, and $(\eps(1-\rho)\rho^{\,t-T-1},\ t,\ (1,\mathsf{tail}))$ for every $t>T$;
\item from $(q,s)$: $(\pi,\delta,(q+1,s'))$ for every transition $(\pi,\delta,s')$ of $s$;
\item from $(q,\mathsf{tail})$: $(1,0,(q+1,\mathsf{tail}))$.
\end{itemize}
The first entry uses two transitions at once because it brings two frogs to the child, the frog of the child and the entering one (the shift $F_c(m)=G_c(m+1)$ of Section~\ref{sec:curves}).

Lemma~\ref{lem:child}, used in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:root}, says that this chain represents a child whose curve has law $\BR(H^*)$.

\begin{lemma}[child chain]\label{lem:child}\lean{FrogModel.LemmaX.childLaw_holds, FrogModel.LemmaX.ChildLaw, FrogModel.LemmaX.childPsiLaw_holds, FrogModel.LemmaX.rowLaw_holds}
Assume (C1). Let $G$ have law $\BR(H^*)$, $F(0)=0$ and $F(m)=G(m+1)$ for $m\ge1$. Then the sequence $\bigl(F(m)-F(m-1)\bigr)_{m\ge1}$ has the law of the sequence of deliveries of the child chain started at $\mathsf F$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Under $\BR(H^*)$ the increments $G(m)-G(m-1)$ are read block by block, and the blocks are independent with law $H^*$. A block of law $H^*$ is drawn index by index: with probability $1-\eps$ by running the table along a path from $\mathrm{root}$, one transition per index; with probability $\eps(1-\rho)\rho^{\,t-T-1}$ as $Z_t$, which delivers $t$ at its first index and $0$ at the others. These are the rows of $\mathsf B$, $(q,s)$ and $(q,\mathsf{tail})$. The first increment of $F$ is $G(2)$, the first two indices of the first block, which is the row of $\mathsf F$. This is \decl{FrogModel.LemmaX.childLaw_holds} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The root chain}
The \emph{root chain} has states $(i,e,a,\xi)$: $i\in\{1,\dots,J\}$ is the frog of $r$ being processed, $e$ the number of frozen frogs so far, $a\ge0$ the number of frogs waiting at $r$, and $\xi$ a multiset of four child states. It starts at $(1,0,1,\{\mathsf F,\mathsf F,\mathsf F,\mathsf F\})$. A step moves one waiting frog: with probability $1/5$ it is frozen, $(e,a)\mapsto(e+1,a-1)$; and for every child state $c$ of multiplicity $m_c$ in $\xi$ and every transition $(\pi,\delta,c')$ of its row, with probability $m_c\pi/5$ it enters that child, $a\mapsto a-1+\delta$ and one $c$ in $\xi$ becomes $c'$. When $a=0$ after a step, $G(i)=e$ is recorded; then, if $i<J$, the next frog starts, $(i,e,0,\xi)\mapsto(i+1,e,1,\xi)$, and if $i=J$ the chain is \emph{absorbed}, with output $(G(1),\dots,G(J))$.

Lemma~\ref{lem:root} identifies $\Phi_J(H^*)$ with the output law of the root chain, for the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:check}.

\begin{lemma}[root chain]\label{lem:root}\lean{FrogModel.Engine.law_outPsi_start, FrogModel.Engine.outPsi, FrogModel.Engine.rstep}
Assume (C1), and suppose that the root chain is absorbed almost surely. Then its output has law $\Phi_J(H^*)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $G_1,\dots,G_4$ be independent with law $\BR(H^*)$ and $D_1,D_2,\dots$ the directions of Section~\ref{sec:curves}. By Lemma~\ref{lem:child}, the deliveries of child $c$ at its successive entries may be drawn from an independent child chain, one transition per entry; the four children are exchangeable, so the multiset of their states is again a Markov chain, with the transition probabilities above. Process the frogs of $r$ one at a time: frog $i$ starts, and the steps go on until no frog waits at $r$. After $n$ steps of phase $i$ the number of waiting frogs is $\Lambda_i(n)-n$, so phase $i$ ends after $\nu(i)$ steps in total: for $k<\nu(i-1)$ one has $\Lambda_i(k)=\Lambda_{i-1}(k)+1>k$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:psi}(2), and the first $n\ge\nu(i-1)$ with $\Lambda_i(n)=n$ is $\nu(i)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:psi}(1). So $G(i)$ is the number of exits among the first $\nu(i)$ directions, which is $\Psi(G_1,\dots,G_4;D)(i)$. This is \decl{FrogModel.Engine.law_outPsi_start} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A potential}
Let $r_J(\mathrm{end})=1$ and $r_q(s)=\sum\pi\,\phi^{\delta}\,r_{q+1}(s')$ over the row of $s$, so that $r_q(s)=\E[\phi^{B(J)-B(q)}\mid s_q=s]\ge1$; let
\[
M=(1-\eps)\,r_0(\mathrm{root})+\eps(1-\rho)\frac{\phi^{T+1}}{1-\phi\rho}=\E\,\phi^{B^*(J)}
\]
when $\phi\rho<1$, and $\theta=5\phi-4\kappa$. Give the child states the weights
\[
w(\mathsf F)=\kappa^J,\quad w(\mathsf B)=\kappa^{J-1},\quad w((q,s))=\kappa^{q-1}r_q(s),\quad w((q,\mathsf{tail}))=\kappa^{q-1},
\]
and the states of the root chain the potential
\[
U(i,e,a,\xi)=\theta^{e}\,\phi^{\,a+J-i}\prod_{c\in\xi}w(c),
\]
the product over the four elements of $\xi$ with their multiplicities. The second condition is
\begin{itemize}
\item[(C2)] $\phi\rho<1$, $\kappa\ge1$, $M\le\kappa^J$, $\theta>1$ and $\theta\rho\ge1$.
\end{itemize}

The potential plays the role of the exponential weight of Hoffman, Johnson and Junge for $d\ge6$ \cite[Proposition~18]{HJJ17}: each exit multiplies it by $\theta$, each waiting frog by $\phi$ and each child by its weight, and the choice $\theta=5\phi-4\kappa$ makes one step a supermartingale step. For branching Markov chains, a positive function $f$ with $Mf\le f$ for the mean offspring operator $M$ is the criterion of transience \cite[Theorem~2.1]{Mul08a}. Lemma~\ref{lem:potential} is used in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:check}, to bound the stopped parts of the output and to show that the chains are absorbed.

\begin{lemma}[potential]\label{lem:potential}\lean{FrogModel.LemmaX.lemma61_holds, FrogModel.LemmaX.Lemma61, FrogModel.Cert.Data.child_mean_le, FrogModel.Cert.Data.r_step, FrogModel.Cert.Data.one_le_r, FrogModel.Cert.Data.one_le_weight} % english-ok: Lean declaration names
\begin{enumerate}
\item Assume (C1) and (C2). Then $w\ge1$, and every child state $c$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:onestep}
\sum\pi\,\phi^{\delta}\,w(c')\le\kappa\,w(c),
\end{equation}
the sum over the row of $c$.
\item Consider the root chain with rows of the child states and weights $w\ge1$ that satisfy~\eqref{eq:onestep}, and assume $\phi>1$, $\kappa\ge1$ and $\theta>1$. Then from every state the chain is absorbed almost surely, and $\E[\theta^{G(J)}\mid\text{state}]\le U(\text{state})$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) By (C1) and $\phi>1$, $r_q(s)\ge1$, so $w\ge1$. From $(q,s)$ the left side of~\eqref{eq:onestep} is $\sum\pi\phi^\delta\kappa^{q}r_{q+1}(s')=\kappa^{q}r_q(s)$, with $\kappa^{J-1}=w(\mathsf B)$ when $q+1=J$; from $(q,\mathsf{tail})$ it is $\kappa^{q}$. From $\mathsf B$ it is $(1-\eps)\sum\pi_1\phi^{\delta_1}r_1(s_1)+\eps\sum_{t>T}(1-\rho)\rho^{\,t-T-1}\phi^t=M\le\kappa^J$, and from $\mathsf F$ it is $\kappa M\le\kappa^{J+1}$; both use $\phi\rho<1$ for the tail.

(2) In one step from $(i,e,a,\xi)$ with $a\ge1$, the factor $\theta^e\phi^a\prod w$ is multiplied in conditional mean by at most
\[
\frac15\cdot\frac{\theta}{\phi}+\sum_{c\in\xi}\frac15\cdot\frac{\kappa}{\phi}=\frac{\theta+4\kappa}{5\phi}=1,
\]
the exit contributing $\theta/\phi$ and an entry into a child in state $c$ contributing at most $\kappa w(c)/(\phi\,w(c))$ by~\eqref{eq:onestep}. Starting the next frog changes $(i,a)$ from $(i,0)$ to $(i+1,1)$ and leaves $a+J-i$ unchanged. So $U$ along the chain, stopped at absorption, is a nonnegative supermartingale and converges almost surely. Each step is an exit with probability $1/5$ independently of the past, so a run that is never absorbed has infinitely many exits, and then $U\ge\theta^e\to\infty$, since all other factors are at least $1$; so the chain is absorbed almost surely. At absorption $a=0$, $i=J$, and $U=\theta^{G(J)}\prod w\ge\theta^{G(J)}$. Fatou's lemma gives the bound. This is \decl{FrogModel.LemmaX.lemma61_holds} in the formalization, for~(2), and \texttt{FrogModel.Cert.Data.child\_mean\_le} for~(1).
\end{proof}

By Markov's inequality, the output of the root chain from a state $s$ satisfies $\P(G(J)\ge t\mid s)\le\theta^{-t}U(s)$ for every $t$.

\subsection{Max chains}
For $1\le q\le J-1$ and $k\ge0$ let $\bar\pi_q(k)$ be the largest, over the labels $s\in S_q$, of the sum of the $\pi$ over the transitions of $s$ with $\delta\ge k$, so that $\bar\pi_q(0)=1$. The \emph{max chain} of level $q$ is a child state $(q,\mathsf M)$ with the row $(\bar\pi_q(k)-\bar\pi_q(k+1),\ k,\ (q+1,\mathsf M))$ over the $k$ with $\bar\pi_q(k)>\bar\pi_q(k+1)$, where $(J,\mathsf M)$ is read as $\mathsf B$, and with the weight $w((q,\mathsf M))=\kappa^{q-1}r_q(\mathsf M)$, where $r_J(\mathsf M)=1$ and $r_q(\mathsf M)=\sum_k(\bar\pi_q(k)-\bar\pi_q(k+1))\phi^kr_{q+1}(\mathsf M)$. These weights satisfy~\eqref{eq:onestep} with equality and are at least $1$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:potential}(2) applies to the root chain with the child states extended by the max chains. The \emph{lump} of a child state replaces $(q,s)$ by $(q,\mathsf M)$ and fixes every other child state; the lump of a multiset is taken element by element.

Lemma~\ref{lem:maxchain}, used in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:check}, allows the check to replace a child by its max chain, which makes the set of states finite.

\begin{lemma}[max chains]\label{lem:maxchain}\lean{FrogModel.Engine.couplingLE_redirect, FrogModel.Engine.isSim_childStep}
Assume (C1), and let $n_0\ge0$. Consider the root chain in which, at the arrival in a state, a rule that depends only on the past replaces some of the child states $(q,s)$ of the state by $(q,\mathsf M)$, and makes no replacement from the first state with more than $n_0$ exits on. Let $\mathrm{Out}$ and $\mathrm{Out}'$ be the laws of the outputs of the chain and of the modified chain, with the value $\infty$ for the frogs that are never finished. Then $\mathrm{Out}\stle\mathrm{Out}'$ on $\cC_J$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Number the children $1,\dots,4$, and drive both chains by the same directions and, for every child $c$ and every $j\ge1$, the same uniform variable $\zeta_{c,j}$ for the $j$-th entry into $c$, which draws the transition of the row of the current state of $c$ by the quantile of its delivery; this is a sequential coupling in the sense of Kamae, Krengel and O'Brien \cite[Proposition~1]{KKO77}. From $(q,s)$ and from $(q,\mathsf M)$, the delivery of the max chain is then at least that of $s$, since $\bar\pi_q$ dominates the tail of the delivery of every row of level $q$; both move to level $q+1$, and at the end of the block both reach $\mathsf B$ and coincide from then on. So a child replaced at some time has, after every further number of entries, delivered at least as many frogs as the same child not replaced.

The output from a state is non-decreasing in the future deliveries of the four children, pathwise with the same directions: from a state $(i,e,a,\xi)$, the number of further steps until frog $i+k$ is finished is the least fixed point of $n\mapsto a+k+\sum_c\tilde F_c(n_c(n))$, where $\tilde F_c$ counts the future deliveries of child $c$ and $n_c(n)$ the entries into $c$ among the next $n$ directions, and $G(i+k)-e$ counts the exits among these steps; Lemma~\ref{lem:psi}(4) applies. So one replacement at a stopping time raises the output pathwise. The rule acts only before the $(n_0+1)$-th exit, and each step is an exit with probability $1/5$ independently of the past, so it makes finitely many replacements almost surely. For $m\ge0$, let the $m$-replacement chain apply the rule at its first $m$ occasions only. The $m$- and $(m+1)$-replacement chains agree up to the $(m+1)$-th occasion and differ there by one replacement, so their outputs are ordered pathwise, and the output of the original chain is below that of every $m$-replacement chain. On the event of finitely many occasions, the $m$-replacement chain is the modified chain for $m$ large. Hence $\mathrm{Out}\stle\mathrm{Out}'$. This is \decl{FrogModel.Engine.couplingLE_redirect} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The backward check}
The check runs on a finite set of states of a redirected root chain, and on two finite linear systems over that set. A \emph{state} is now $s=(q,\xi,a)$ with $1\le q\le J$, $\xi$ a multiset of four child states (max chains included) and $1\le a\le T'$; the linear systems below count the frozen frogs. The start is $s_0=(1,\{\mathsf F,\mathsf F,\mathsf F,\mathsf F\},1)$. Write $\xi[c\to c']$ for $\xi$ with one $c$ replaced by $c'$, and, for $c\in\{\mathsf F,\mathsf B\}$, $c_{\mathsf{tail}}=(2,\mathsf{tail})$ if $c=\mathsf F$ and $(1,\mathsf{tail})$ if $c=\mathsf B$. The \emph{moves} of $s$ are triples of a weight $\omega>0$, an exit flag $f\in\{0,1\}$ and a pair $(\xi',a')$:
\begin{itemize}
\item the exit: $\omega=1/5$, $f=1$, $(\xi,a-1)$;
\item for every child state $c$ of multiplicity $m_c$ in $\xi$ and every transition $(\pi,\delta,c')$ of its row, except the tail transitions of $\mathsf F$ and $\mathsf B$: $\omega=m_c\pi/5$, $f=0$, $(\xi[c\to c'],a-1+\delta)$;
\item for $c\in\{\mathsf F,\mathsf B\}$ in $\xi$ and every $t$ with $T+1\le t\le T'-a+1$: $\omega=m_c\eps(1-\rho)\rho^{\,t-T-1}/5$, $f=0$, $(\xi[c\to c_{\mathsf{tail}}],a-1+t)$.
\end{itemize}
A move with $a'>T'$ is a \emph{stop}. A move with $a'=0$ and $q=J$ is \emph{absorbed}. Otherwise it \emph{reaches} $t=(q,\xi',a')$ if $a'\ge1$, and $t=(q+1,\xi',1)$ if $a'=0$, the next frog starting. Given a finite set $\Sigma$ of states, each with a flag in $\{0,1\}$, the \emph{target} of the move is $t$ if $t\in\Sigma$ with flag $1$, and the lump $(q_t,\mathrm{lump}(\xi'),a_t)$ of $t$ otherwise. The other stops of $s$ are the exit when no exit is left (below), and, for $c\in\{\mathsf F,\mathsf B\}$ in $\xi$, the tail atoms with $t\ge t_0=\max(T+1,T'-a+2)$, taken together.

For $1\le q\le J$ let $\cC^{(q)}$ be the set of vectors $x=(x_q,\dots,x_J)$ of nonnegative integers with $x_q+\dots+x_J\le T$, the numbers of frozen frogs in the rest of phase $q$ and in each later phase. The unknowns are functions $\ell(s)[x]\ge0$ for $s\in\Sigma$, $x\in\cC^{(q)}$, and $u(s)[b]\ge0$ for $s\in\Sigma$, $b\in\{0,\dots,T\}$, the number of exits still allowed. For a move $\mu$ of $s$ with target $t$, let
\[
v_\mu(x)=\begin{cases}
\ell(t)[x-f\,\one_q] & \text{if } t \text{ is in phase } q \text{ and } x_q\ge f,\\
\ell(t)[(x_{q+1},\dots,x_J)] & \text{if } t \text{ is in phase } q+1 \text{ and } x_q=f,\\
1 & \text{if } \mu \text{ is absorbed and } x=(f),\\
0 & \text{otherwise, and for a stop},
\end{cases}
\]
where $\one_q$ is the unit vector of the coordinate $x_q$. For $s=(q,\xi,a)$, write $U(s)=\phi^{\,a+J-q}\prod_{c\in\xi}w(c)$, the potential with no exit counted. The conditions on $(\Sigma,\ell,u)$ are:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(C3)] $\Sigma$ contains $s_0$, and every move of every state of $\Sigma$ that is neither a stop nor absorbed has its target in $\Sigma$;
\item[(C4)] for every $s\in\Sigma$ and $x\in\cC^{(q)}$, $\ell(s)[x]\le\sum_\mu\omega_\mu\,v_\mu(x)$, the sum over the moves of $s$;
\item[(C5)] for every $s=(q,\xi,a)\in\Sigma$ and $b\in\{0,\dots,T\}$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
u(s)[b]\ge{}&X_b(s)+\sum_{\mu:\ f=0}\omega_\mu\,u(t_\mu)[b]+\sum_{\mu \text{ stop}}\omega_\mu\,U(q,\xi'_\mu,a'_\mu)\\
&+\sum_{c\in\{\mathsf F,\mathsf B\}}\frac{m_c\,\eps(1-\rho)\rho^{\,t_0-T-1}\phi^{t_0}}{5(1-\phi\rho)}\,U(q,\xi[c\to c_{\mathsf{tail}}],a-1),
\end{aligned}
\]
where the first sum is over the moves with $f=0$ that are neither stops nor absorbed, with targets $t_\mu$, and $X_b(s)$ is the term of the exit, with target $t$: $\frac{\theta}{5}u(t)[b-1]$ if $b\ge1$ and the exit is not absorbed, $0$ if $b\ge1$ and it is absorbed, and $\frac{\theta}{5}U(q,\xi,a-1)$ if $b=0$;
\item[(C6)] $u(s_0)[T]\le\eps\,\theta^{T+1}$;
\item[(C7)] with $L(B)=\ell(s_0)[(B(1),B(2)-B(1),\dots,B(J)-B(J-1))]$ for $B\in \cC_{J,T}$, there are amounts $\alpha(B,B')\ge0$, for $B\in \cC_{J,T}$ and $B'$ an atom of $h$, positive only if $B\le B'$ componentwise, with $\sum_B\alpha(B,B')\ge h(B')$ for every $B'$ and $\sum_{B'}\alpha(B,B')\le L(B)$ for every $B$.
\end{itemize}
Sub- and super-solutions of such linear systems certify bounds on reachability probabilities of Markov chains in the same way \cite[Propositions~3 and~4]{CQSWWZ25}. In words: $\ell$ is a sub-solution of the system whose least solution is the law of the frozen frogs, phase by phase, over the runs absorbed before their first stop; $u$ is a super-solution of the system whose least solution is the total potential of the stopped runs; and (C7) is a transport plan from the measure $L$ to the finite part $h$ of $H^*$ along the order of $\cC_J$.

Theorem~\ref{thm:check} is the criterion that Section~\ref{sec:certificate} checks.

\begin{theorem}[backward check]\label{thm:check}\lean{FrogModel.Engine.G3.phiSuper_of_check, FrogModel.LemmaX.lemma62_holds, FrogModel.LemmaX.Lemma62, FrogModel.Engine.domSplit_of_domCert, FrogModel.Engine.G3.domCert_of_check, FrogModel.Order.quantile_coupling, FrogModel.Cert.inv_theta_pow_le, FrogModel.LemmaX.PhiSuper}
Let a table satisfy (C1) and (C2), and let $\Sigma$ with its flags, $\ell$ and $u$ satisfy (C3) to (C7). Then $\Phi_J(H^*)\stle H^*$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Least solutions.} The right sides of (C4) and (C5) are affine maps $\ell\mapsto\beta+\Pi\ell$ and $u\mapsto\beta'+\Pi'u$ with nonnegative coefficients on the finite set $\Sigma$, by (C3). Both kernels are transient. The weights of the moves of a state sum to at most $1$, the exit has weight $1/5$, and a product of coefficients along a sequence of moves vanishes once it contains more than $T+1$ exits, since the exits lower the coordinate $x_q$ or the budget $b$; so the entries of $\Pi^n$ and of $\Pi'^n$ are at most $\theta^{T+1}$ times the probability of at most $T+1$ exits in $n$ independent trials of probability $1/5$, and tend to $0$. Let $\ell^\circ$ and $u^\circ$ be their least nonnegative solutions, the limits of the iterates from $0$, as for the hitting probabilities of a Markov chain \cite[Theorem~1.3.2]{Nor97}. A sub-solution satisfies $\ell\le\beta+\Pi\beta+\dots+\Pi^{n-1}\beta+\Pi^n\ell$, and the last term tends to $0$, so $\ell\le\ell^\circ$; a super-solution satisfies $u\ge\beta'+\dots+\Pi'^{n-1}\beta'$ for every $n$, so $u\ge u^\circ$.

\emph{The redirected chain.} Let $P_{\mathrm{dom}}$ be the root chain in which, until the first stop, every arrival at a state is replaced by its target, and which, from the first stop on, follows the rows of the child states, max chains included, with no further replacement. A stop is tested on the move, before its target is taken. Its rule depends only on the state reached and on whether a stop has occurred, and a move to a state with more than $T$ exits is a stop, so it makes no replacement from such a state on, and Lemma~\ref{lem:maxchain} with $n_0=T$ gives $\mathrm{Out}\stle\mathrm{Out}_{\mathrm{dom}}$, where $\mathrm{Out}$ and $\mathrm{Out}_{\mathrm{dom}}$ are the output laws of the root chain and of $P_{\mathrm{dom}}$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:potential}(2) both chains are absorbed almost surely, $P_{\mathrm{dom}}$ after its first stop, and $\mathrm{Out}=\Phi_J(H^*)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:root}. The function $\ell^\circ(s_0)$, read through the increments, is the law $A$ of the outputs of the runs of $P_{\mathrm{dom}}$ absorbed before their first stop, a measure on $\cC_{J,T}$ since such a run has at most $T$ exits; and $u^\circ(s_0)[T]$ is the sum, over the stops of the runs, of their probability times $U$ at the state where they stop, with $\theta^e$ for the exits made, the tail atoms above $T'$ summed in closed form. So $\mathrm{Out}_{\mathrm{dom}}=A+D$, where $D$ is the law of the outputs of the stopped runs, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:potential}(2) after the stop and Markov's inequality, $D(G(J)\ge t)\le\theta^{-t}u^\circ(s_0)[T]\le\theta^{-t}u(s_0)[T]$ for every $t$. Also $L\le A$ atom by atom.

\emph{The coupling.} By (C7), lowering amounts where an atom of $h$ is overfilled, there is a measure $\varpi$ on pairs $(B,B')$ with $B\le B'$, second marginal $h$ and first marginal $\lambda\le L\le A$. Let $\nu=\mathrm{Out}_{\mathrm{dom}}-\lambda=(A-\lambda)+D$, a nonnegative measure of mass $1-(1-\eps)=\eps$, and let $Q=\eps\sum_{t>T}(1-\rho)\rho^{\,t-T-1}\delta_{Z_t}$, so that $Q(Z\ge t)=\eps\rho^{\,t-T-1}$ for $t\ge T+1$. For $t\le T+1$, $\nu(G(J)\ge t)\le\eps=Q(Z\ge t)$. For $t\ge T+1$, $A-\lambda$ lives on $\cC_{J,T}$, so by (C6) and $\theta\rho\ge1$,
\[
\nu(G(J)\ge t)=D(G(J)\ge t)\le\theta^{-t}\eps\,\theta^{T+1}\le\eps\rho^{\,t-T-1}=Q(Z\ge t).
\]
Two measures of equal finite mass on $\Nb$ whose tails compare in this way are coupled with the first coordinate below the second, by the quantile coupling. A vector $G\in\cC_J$ satisfies $G\le Z_t$ if and only if $G(J)\le t$, so $\nu\stle Q$. Adding $\varpi$ and this coupling gives $\mathrm{Out}_{\mathrm{dom}}\stle h+Q=H^*$, and $\Phi_J(H^*)=\mathrm{Out}\stle\mathrm{Out}_{\mathrm{dom}}\stle H^*$ by transitivity on $\cC_J$. This is \decl{FrogModel.Engine.G3.phiSuper_of_check} in the formalization, with (C3) to (C7) in the form in which the kernel evaluates them (Section~\ref{sec:comp-check}).
\end{proof}

\end{proof}

\section{Recurrence on the 3-ary tree}\label{sec:three}

This section reduces Theorem~\ref{thm:three} to three statements about the planted tree $\Ts$ of Definition~\ref{def:planted} with $d=3$: a criterion of recurrence for every $d\ge2$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:crit}), one step from a planted curve to the counts of that criterion (Lemma~\ref{lem:rootstep}), and a linear lower bound on the mean of the planted curve (Theorem~\ref{thm:linear}). Sections~\ref{sec:crit} to~\ref{sec:proofthree} prove them. They set their own notation: of Sections~\ref{sec:curves} to~\ref{sec:certificate} they use the pool argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:recursion} and the map $\Psi$ of Section~\ref{sec:curves}, nothing else, and they give new meanings to some letters of those sections.

\subsection*{Planted curves}
On $\Ts$ a vertex at depth $i$ has $3^i$ descendants at each depth $i+j$. For an integer $m\ge0$, the \emph{planted tree of height} $m$ is $\Ts$ with one sleeping frog at every vertex of depth at most $m$, the vertex $r$ included, and no frog below depth $m$; a vertex at depth $i\le m$ has \emph{height} $m-i$. The frogs follow independent simple random walks on $\Ts$, which are not stopped at depth $m$, and a frog is frozen at its first visit to $y$.

\begin{definition}[planted curve]\label{def:plantedcurve}\lean{FrogModel.D3.Iface.plantedG, FrogModel.D3.Iface.Woken, FrogModel.D3.Iface.pathMeasure, FrogModel.D3.Iface.mu, FrogModel.D3.Iface.deficit}
Let $m,k\ge0$, and start $k$ awake frogs, the \emph{entrants}, at $r$ in the planted tree of height $m$. The \emph{woken set} is the least set of frogs that contains the entrants and the frog of every vertex of depth at most $m$ visited by the path of one of its members, and $G_m(k)\in\Nb$ is the number of frogs of the woken set whose path visits $y$. The function $k\mapsto G_m(k)$ is the \emph{planted curve} of height $m$, and
\[
\mu_m(k)=\frac{m+1+k}3,\qquad\Delta_m(k)=\mu_m(k)-\E G_m(k).
\]
\end{definition}

By Lemma~\ref{lem:reach}, the woken set is the set of frogs that the entrants wake in the frog model on $\Ts$, and $G_m(k)$ is the number of frozen frogs. The first entrant wakes the frog of $r$ at once, so with $k\ge1$ entrants there are $k+1$ frogs at $r$ from the start. The number $\mu_m(k)$ is the mean number of frogs that would reach $y$ if every frog were awake (Lemma~\ref{lem:awake}), so $\Delta_m(k)\ge0$ is the \emph{deficit} of the planted curve. The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:three} shows that the deficit at $k=1$ stays below $2/5$ of $\mu_m(1)$ at every large height.

\subsection*{The three statements}
For $m\ge0$, let $Z_m$ be the number of frogs that are woken and frozen in the frog model on $\Ts$ of Definition~\ref{def:planted} in which only the frogs at depth at most $m$ are present: the frog at $r$ starts awake, one frog sleeps at every vertex of depth $1$ to $m$, and there is no other frog. Theorem~\ref{thm:A} says that the frog model on $\T_d$ is transient when the mean of the frozen count $X$ is below $1$; the criterion below goes the other way, from a linear growth of the means of the truncated frozen counts $Z_m\le X$. It holds for every $d\ge2$.

\begin{theorem}[recurrence criterion]\label{thm:crit}\lean{FrogModel.LemmaR.recurrent_of_plantedCount, FrogModel.LemmaR.plantedCount, FrogModel.LemmaR.plantedLemmaR', FrogModel.ZeroOne.recurrent_of_frozenCount}
Let $d\ge2$. If $\limsup_{m\to\infty}\E Z_m/m>0$, then the frog model on $\T_d$ is recurrent.
\end{theorem}

From here to the end of Section~\ref{sec:proofthree}, $d=3$. The count $Z_{m+1}$ and the planted curve $G_m$ are linked by one step of the frog at $r$.

\begin{lemma}[root step]\label{lem:rootstep}\lean{FrogModel.D3.LaneA.lemma19_proof, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma19}
For every $m\ge0$, $\E Z_{m+1}\ge\bigl(1+\E G_m(1)\bigr)/4$.
\end{lemma}

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:three} combines the two with a linear lower bound on $\E G_m(1)$, proved in Section~\ref{sec:proofthree}.

\begin{theorem}[linear growth]\label{thm:linear}\lean{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.linear_three', FrogModel.D3.Assembly.linear_three}
Let $m_1=1778279$. For every $m>m_1-30$,
\[
\E G_m(1)\ge\frac35\,\mu_m(1)=\frac{m+2}5.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:three}]
Let $m\ge m_1$. Theorem~\ref{thm:linear} gives $\E G_m(1)\ge(m+2)/5$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:rootstep} gives
\[
\E Z_{m+1}\ge\frac14\Bigl(1+\frac{m+2}5\Bigr)=\frac{m+7}{20}\ge\frac{m+1}{20}.
\]
So $\E Z_n/n\ge1/20$ for every $n>m_1$, the upper limit of $\E Z_n/n$ is at least $1/20$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:crit} with $d=3$ gives recurrence. This is \decl{FrogModel.recurrent_three} in the formalization.\computed{code/formal-proof/main_axioms.lean, .github/workflows/ci.yml}
\end{proof}

The constant $3/5$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:linear} has no role beyond being positive: any linear lower bound on $\E G_m(1)$ would do. The height $m_1$ and the constant come from the way the bound is proved, in three stages.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{A seed at height $100$} (Section~\ref{sec:lower}). A reading of the frog paths that wakes fewer frogs than the model, and whose count has a law given by a finite recursion, gives lower bounds on $\E G_{100}(k)$ and upper bounds on $\P(G_{100}(k)\le g)$ for $k\le32$ and $g\le47$, from a stored run of the recursion (Proposition~\ref{prop:m1run}(1)). It leaves the deficit $\Delta_{100}(1)$ at about $0.44\,\mu_{100}(1)$, too large for the last stage.
\item \emph{A certified chain of steps} (Sections~\ref{sec:closure} to~\ref{sec:states}). A map $\Phi$ on finite tables of such bounds carries bounds valid at height $m$ to bounds valid at height $m+1$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:step}), from inequalities on one vertex and its three subtrees. Five hundred and five steps and a table $T$ that $\Phi$ maps below itself give bounds valid at every height of $[605,m_1]$, with deficits $\Delta_m(1)\le0.135\,\mu_m(1)$ and $\Delta_m(62)\le0.071\,\mu_m(62)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:m1run}(2)). It leaves a bound that holds on a finite range only.
\item \emph{An induction above $m_1$} (Section~\ref{sec:dprime}). From those bounds at $m_1$, an induction on the height that tracks $\E G_m(J)$ for one growing index $J=J(m)$ gives Theorem~\ref{thm:linear} at every larger height (Theorem~\ref{thm:dprime}), provided two inequalities hold at every height; they are checked on $95$ intervals from $m_1$ to $10^{30}$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:dcheck}) and proved beyond (Lemma~\ref{lem:tail}).
\end{enumerate}
Section~\ref{sec:proofthree} assembles the three stages into the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:linear}.

\section{Proof of the recurrence criterion}\label{sec:crit}

This section proves Theorem~\ref{thm:crit}. Lemma~\ref{lem:secondmoment} turns the linear growth of $\E Z_m$ into infinitely many frozen frogs with positive probability, by the second moment method, and Theorem~\ref{thm:Bzero} turns infinitely many frozen frogs with positive probability into recurrence almost surely. Here $d\ge2$, and $X$ is the frozen count of Definition~\ref{def:planted}.

The second moment method needs a variable above $Z_m$ whose second moment is of order $m^2$. Let $U'_m$ be the number of frogs at depth at most $m$ of $\Ts$ whose path visits $y$, all frogs counted, woken or not. Lemma~\ref{lem:hit} bounds its terms.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:hit}\lean{FrogModel.LemmaR.hitStar_le}
A simple random walk on $\Ts$ from a vertex at depth $i$ visits $y$ with probability at most $d^{-(i+1)}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $f(y)=1$ and $f(v)=d^{-(|v|+1)}$ for the other vertices. At a vertex of depth $i\ge1$, the mean of $f$ over the $d+1$ neighbours is $(d^{-i}+d\cdot d^{-(i+2)})/(d+1)=d^{-(i+1)}$, and at $r$ it is $(1+d\cdot d^{-2})/(d+1)=d^{-1}$; so $f$ is harmonic off $y$. By induction on $t$, the probability to visit $y$ within $t$ steps from $v$ is at most $f(v)$, since it is $1=f(y)$ at $y$ and the mean of these probabilities at the neighbours elsewhere. Letting $t\to\infty$ gives the bound. This is \decl{FrogModel.LemmaR.hitStar_le} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:secondmoment}, used in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:crit}, is the second moment method along the heights.

\begin{lemma}[second moment]\label{lem:secondmoment}\lean{FrogModel.LemmaR.plantedLemmaR', FrogModel.LemmaR.measure_top_pos_of_linear, FrogModel.LemmaR.paleyZygmund}
If $\limsup_m\E Z_m/m>0$, then $\P(X=\infty)>0$.
\end{lemma}

Theorem~\ref{thm:Bzero}, the second step of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:crit}, plays the role of the zero-one law of Hoffman, Johnson and Junge \cite[Theorem~4]{HJJ17}, by which the frog model on $\T_d$ is recurrent with probability $0$ or $1$; it starts from the planted tree instead.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:Bzero}\lean{FrogModel.ZeroOne.recurrent_of_frozenCount}
If $\P(X=\infty)>0$, then the frog model on $\T_d$ is recurrent.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:crit}]
Lemma~\ref{lem:secondmoment} gives $\P(X=\infty)>0$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:Bzero} gives recurrence. This is \decl{FrogModel.LemmaR.recurrent_of_plantedCount} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:secondmoment}]
\emph{Comparisons.} The frogs counted by $Z_m$ are reached from the frog at $r$ along arcs to vertices of depth at most $m$, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:reach} they are woken in the model with all frogs present, and $Z_m\le X$; they are frogs at depth at most $m$ whose path visits $y$, so $Z_m\le U'_m$.

\emph{Second moment of $U'_m$.} The variable $U'_m$ is a sum of independent indicators, one per vertex of depth at most $m$, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:hit} the $d^i$ vertices of depth $i$ contribute at most $d^i\,d^{-(i+1)}\le1$ to its mean. So $\E U'_m\le m+1$ and $\E U_m'^2\le\E U'_m+(\E U'_m)^2\le(m+1)+(m+1)^2\le6m^2$ for $m\ge1$.

\emph{Paley and Zygmund.} Let $Z\le U$ be nonnegative random variables with $\E U^2<\infty$, and $A=\{Z>\E Z/2\}$. Then $\E Z\le\E Z/2+\E[U\one_A]\le\E Z/2+(\E U^2)^{1/2}\P(A)^{1/2}$ by the Cauchy and Schwarz inequality, so $(\E Z)^2\le4\,\P(A)\,\E U^2$. Let $c>0$ with $\E Z_m\ge cm$ for infinitely many $m$. For each of them, with $Z=Z_m$ and $U=U'_m$,
\[
\P\bigl(X>cm/2\bigr)\ge\P\bigl(Z_m>\E Z_m/2\bigr)\ge\frac{(\E Z_m)^2}{4\,\E U_m'^2}\ge\frac{c^2}{24}.
\]
The events $\{X>cm/2\}$ decrease to $\{X=\infty\}$ along these $m$, so $\P(X=\infty)\ge c^2/24$. This is \decl{FrogModel.LemmaR.plantedLemmaR'} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:Bzero}]
Let $\beta=\P(X=\infty)>0$ and $R=\{V=\infty\}$ for the frog model on $\T_d$. For $n\ge0$ let $\mathcal F_n$ be the $\sigma$-field of the paths of the frogs at depth at most $n$ of $\T_d$. These $\sigma$-fields increase and generate the $\sigma$-field of all the paths. We show that $\P(R\cap A)\ge\beta\,\P(A)$ for every $n$ and every $A\in\mathcal F_n$. Then $\P(R\mid\mathcal F_n)\ge\beta$ almost surely for every $n$, and by L\'evy's upward theorem $\P(R\mid\mathcal F_n)$ tends to $\one_R$ almost surely, so $\one_R\ge\beta>0$ and $\P(R)=1$.

Fix $n$. The path of the frog at the root reaches depth $n+1$ almost surely; let $u$ be the first vertex of depth $n+1$ it visits, a function of that path, hence $\mathcal F_n$-measurable, and $p(u)$ the parent of $u$, at depth $n$. The graph $\T(u)\cup\{p(u)\}$ is a copy of $\Ts$ with $r=u$ and $y=p(u)$ (Section~\ref{sec:reduction}). Let $X_u$ be the frozen count of this copy: the frog of $u$ starts awake, the frogs of $\T(u)$ follow their paths stopped at their first visit to $p(u)$. The paths of the frogs of $\T(u)$ are independent of $\mathcal F_n$, and for each fixed vertex $v$ of depth $n+1$ the paths of the frogs of $\T(v)$ give $X_v$ the law of $X$; summing over the values of $u$, $\P(\{X_u=\infty\}\cap A)=\beta\,\P(A)$ for $A\in\mathcal F_n$.

On $\{X_u=\infty\}$, the frog of $u$ is woken by the frog of the root, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:reach} every frog of $\T(u)$ that is reachable in the copy is woken in the frog model on $\T_d$, and infinitely many of them visit $p(u)$. After its first visit to $p(u)$, the path of each of these frogs continues with steps that are independent of the path up to that visit and of the other frogs, by the strong Markov property of each path at its first visit to $p(u)$. With probability $(d+1)^{-n}>0$ these steps go straight up to the root, independently from one frog to the next. On $\{X_u=\infty\}$ there are infinitely many such independent trials, so almost surely infinitely many succeed, and each success is a visit to the root. So $R$ contains $\{X_u=\infty\}$ up to a null set, and $\P(R\cap A)\ge\beta\,\P(A)$. This is \decl{FrogModel.ZeroOne.recurrent_of_frozenCount} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

\section{The closure at the root}\label{sec:closure}

This section writes the planted curve of height $m+1$ through the planted curves of its three children (Lemma~\ref{lem:closid}) and proves the properties of planted curves that the bounds of Sections~\ref{sec:onebounds} and~\ref{sec:states} use: the all-awake count (Lemma~\ref{lem:awake}), own-path coins (Lemma~\ref{lem:coins}), the domination of the future by a fresh curve (Lemma~\ref{lem:future}), an identity for the mean by optional stopping (Lemma~\ref{lem:D1}) and the recursion of the deficits that follows (Lemmas~\ref{lem:loss} to~\ref{lem:deadchild}). It ends with the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:rootstep}.

\subsection*{The closure}
The \emph{closure} is the map $\Psi$ of Section~\ref{sec:curves} with $d=3$, written for planted curves. Its data are three curves $G_1,G_2,G_3:\N\to\Nb$, indexed by the children $c$ of $r$, and directions $D_1,D_2,\dots$ in $\{0,1,2,3\}$, where $0$ is the step to $y$ and $c$ the step into the child $c$. For $n\in\N$ and $a\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ let $n_a(n)=\#\{i\le n:D_i=a\}$, and for $q\ge1$ let
\[
\Lambda_q(n)=q+\sum_{c=1}^3G_c\bigl(n_c(n)\bigr),\qquad\nu(q)=\min\{n\ge q:\ \Lambda_q(n)\le n\}\in\Nb,
\]
with $\nu(q)=\infty$ if there is no such $n$, and $n_a(\infty)=\sup_nn_a(n)$. The \emph{output} of the closure with $j\ge1$ entrants is $n_0(\nu(j+1))$, the number of steps to $y$ among the first $\nu(j+1)$, and its output with no entrant is $0$; the number of \emph{entries} into $c$ is $e_c=n_c(\nu(j+1))$. When the children are planted trees of height $m$ with curves $G_c$, the $j$ entrants and the frog of $r$ are the $q=j+1$ frogs at $r$ at the start; each step from $r$ takes one frog, $n_c(n)$ frogs have entered the subtree of $c$ after $n$ steps, the subtree has returned $G_c(n_c(n))$ frogs to $r$, its own frog included, and $\nu(q)$ is the number of steps taken from $r$ when no frog waits there any more. In the notation of Section~\ref{sec:curves}, $\Lambda_q$ is $\Lambda_m$ with $m=q$ and $F_c=G_c$: the shift there accounts for the frog of the child, which a planted curve counts already.

Lemma~\ref{lem:closid} is the construction of the planted model from its children, and every bound of Sections~\ref{sec:closure} and~\ref{sec:onebounds} on one closure goes through it.

\begin{lemma}[closure identity]\label{lem:closid}\lean{FrogModel.D3.LaneA.Pool.curveLaw_succ_proof, FrogModel.D3.LaneA.measurable_plantedPair_proof, FrogModel.D3.LaneA.measurable_closPair_proof, FrogModel.D3.Iface.curveLaw_succ, FrogModel.D3.Iface.closMeasure, FrogModel.D3.Iface.closCurve}
Let $m\ge0$. Let $G_1,G_2,G_3$ be independent planted curves of height $m$, and $D_1,D_2,\dots$ independent and uniform on $\{0,1,2,3\}$, independent of the curves. Then the planted curve of height $m+1$, together with the number of pairs of a woken frog and a time at which it is at $r$, as a function of the number of entrants, has the joint law of the outputs of the closure and of the function $j\mapsto\nu(j+1)$ $(j\ge1)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is the argument of the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:recursion} with $d=3$, in which the subtrees of the children are planted trees of height $m$ and no frog is killed. Build the planted model of height $m+1$ from the directions of the successive steps taken from $r$, from a sequence of independent entry pieces for each child (simple random walks from the child, stopped at their first return to $r$), and from the paths of the frogs of the subtrees of the children, stopped at $r$. By the pool argument of that proof, processing the frogs at $r$ one at a time, each drawing the next direction and, into a child, the next entry piece of that child, builds the frog model with independent simple random walk paths. The number of frogs that the subtree of $c$ returns to $r$ after $e$ entries is $G_c(e)$, by Lemma~\ref{lem:reach} applied to the subtree of $c$ with $r$ in the role of $y$, whatever the order of the entries into the different children; $G_c$ is a planted curve of height $m$, and the three are independent and independent of the directions. With $q$ frogs at $r$ at the start, the number of steps taken from $r$ is the least fixed point $\nu(q)$ of $\Lambda_q$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:psi}), the frozen frogs are the steps to $y$ among them, and each step from $r$ is a visit of a woken frog to $r$. This is \decl{FrogModel.D3.LaneA.Pool.curveLaw_succ_proof} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{The all-awake count and monotonicity}
Lemma~\ref{lem:awake} is used by Lemma~\ref{lem:tails} and by every bound that compares $G_m(k)$ with its mean.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:awake}\lean{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lintegral_plantedG_le_proof, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lintegral_plantedG_le_of, FrogModel.D3.LaneA.prob_reach_proof, FrogModel.D3.Iface.plantedG_le_frogs_proof, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lintegral_plantedG_le, FrogModel.D3.Iface.prob_reach, FrogModel.D3.Iface.plantedG_mono, FrogModel.D3.Iface.plantedG_le_frogs} % english-ok: display formula
Let $m,k\ge0$, and let $U_m(k)$ be the number of frogs, among the $k$ entrants and the frogs of the vertices of depth at most $m$, whose path visits $y$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A path from a vertex at depth $i$ visits $y$ with probability $3^{-(i+1)}$; $U_m(k)$ is a sum of independent indicators with $\E U_m(k)=\mu_m(k)$, and $G_m(k)\le U_m(k)$.
\item For every family of paths, $G_m(k)\le G_m(k+1)$ and $G_m(k)\le k+(3^{m+1}-1)/2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) The depth of a simple random walk on $\Ts$ away from $y$ moves up with probability $1/4$ and down with probability $3/4$, so from depth $i$ it reaches depth $-1$, the vertex $y$, with probability $3^{-(i+1)}$ (gambler's ruin with ratio $1/3$). The $3^i$ frogs of depth $i$ contribute $3^i\cdot3^{-(i+1)}=1/3$ together, each entrant $1/3$, so $\E U_m(k)=(m+1+k)/3$; the paths are independent. The frogs counted by $G_m(k)$ are counted by $U_m(k)$. (2) The woken set increases with the set of entrants, and it holds at most the $k$ entrants and the $(3^{m+1}-1)/2$ frogs of depth at most $m$. This is \decl{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lintegral_plantedG_le_proof} in the formalization, with \texttt{FrogModel.D3.LaneA.prob\_reach\_proof}, \texttt{FrogModel.D3.Iface.plantedG\_mono} and \texttt{FrogModel.D3.Iface.plantedG\_le\_frogs\_proof}.
\end{proof}

So $\Delta_m(k)\ge0$, and $\Delta_m(0)=\mu_m(0)=(m+1)/3$.

\subsection*{Coins and the future}
An entrant whose own path visits $y$ is frozen whatever the other frogs do. Lemma~\ref{lem:coins} records this as a coin attached to each entrant; it is used in Lemmas~\ref{lem:D1}, \ref{lem:deficit} and~\ref{lem:deadchild}, and in the lower curves of Section~\ref{sec:states}.

\begin{lemma}[coins]\label{lem:coins}\lean{FrogModel.D3.LaneA.coin_coupling_proof, FrogModel.D3.Iface.coin_coupling}
Let $m\ge0$. There are a random curve $G$ with the law of $G_m$ and random variables $\xi_1,\xi_2,\ldots\in\{0,1\}$, on one probability space, such that $G(0)=0$ and, for every $e\ge1$, $\P(\xi_e=1)=1/3$, $\xi_e$ is independent of $(G(0),\dots,G(e-1),\xi_1,\dots,\xi_{e-1})$, and $G(e)\ge G(e-1)+\xi_e$ almost surely. In particular $G_m(k)$ stochastically dominates a binomial variable $\Bin(k,1/3)$, and $\E G_m(k+1)\ge\E G_m(k)+1/3$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Take $G=G_m$ on the space of the paths, and let $\xi_e=1$ if the path of the $e$-th entrant visits $y$; by Lemma~\ref{lem:awake}(1), $\P(\xi_e=1)=1/3$. The woken set with $e-1$ entrants holds no entrant of index $e$ or more and reads only the paths of its own frogs, so $G_m(0),\dots,G_m(e-1)$ and $\xi_1,\dots,\xi_{e-1}$ are functions of the paths of the frogs other than the entrants $e,e+1,\dots$, independent of the path of the $e$-th entrant. If $\xi_e=1$, that entrant is counted by $G_m(e)$ and not by $G_m(e-1)$, and the woken set only grows. This is \decl{FrogModel.D3.LaneA.coin_coupling_proof} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

The second property says that what the entrants after the $e$-th add is at most what a fresh planted tree would return. Lemma~\ref{lem:loss} uses it.

\begin{lemma}[future domination]\label{lem:future}\lean{FrogModel.D3.LaneA.future_dom_proof, FrogModel.D3.Iface.future_dom}
Let $m\ge0$, $0\le e\le J$, and let $A$ be an event determined by $(G_m(0),\dots,G_m(e))$. Then
\[
\E\bigl[G_m(J)\one_A\bigr]\le\E\bigl[G_m(e)\one_A\bigr]+\P(A)\,\E G_m(J-e).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $W_e$ be the woken set with $e$ entrants. Whether a frog lies in $W_e$ depends only on the paths of the frogs of $W_e$, so $W_e$ is a stopping set of coordinates in the sense of Lemma~\ref{lem:refresh}, and $A$ is determined by it. Replace the paths of the frogs of $W_e$ by independent copies, drop the entrants $1,\dots,e$, and call the entrants $e+1,e+2,\dots$ the entrants $1,2,\dots$ of the new family; let $G'$ be its planted curve. A frog woken with $J$ entrants and not with $e$ is reached from the entrants $e+1,\dots,J$ along a chain that avoids $W_e$, since $W_e$ is closed under the arcs; the paths along that chain are not replaced, so the frog is woken in the new family with $J-e$ entrants. Hence $G_m(J)\le G_m(e)+G'(J-e)$ for every family of paths. By Lemma~\ref{lem:refresh}, on $A$ the new family has the law of the frog paths and is independent of $A$, so $\E[G'(J-e)\one_A]=\P(A)\,\E G_m(J-e)$. This is \decl{FrogModel.D3.LaneA.future_dom_proof} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{The mean of the closure}
From here on, the closure at height $m+1$ is the closure of Lemma~\ref{lem:closid}: its children are independent planted curves $G_1,G_2,G_3$ of height $m$, each with its coins of Lemma~\ref{lem:coins}, and its directions are independent and uniform. With $j\ge1$ entrants it has $q=j+1$ frogs at $r$ at the start, $\nu=\nu(j+1)$ steps and $e_c$ entries into $c$, and its output has the law of $G_{m+1}(j)$. Lemma~\ref{lem:D1} is the identity on which the recursion of the deficits rests.

\begin{lemma}[optional stopping]\label{lem:D1}\lean{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.d1_proof, FrogModel.D3.Iface.d1, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lossL}
Let $j\ge1$ and $J\ge0$, and
\[
L_{j,J}=\frac13\sum_{c=1}^3\E\Bigl[\bigl(G_c(J)-J/3\bigr)-\bigl(G_c(e_c\wedge J)-(e_c\wedge J)/3\bigr)\Bigr].
\]
Then $\E G_{m+1}(j)\ge\E G_m(J)+(1+j-J)/3-L_{j,J}$.
\end{lemma}

The term $L_{j,J}$ is the loss caused by children entered fewer than $J$ times. Lemma~\ref{lem:loss}, used by Lemma~\ref{lem:deficit} and Theorem~\ref{thm:dprime}, bounds it.

\begin{lemma}[loss]\label{lem:loss}\lean{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemma8a_proof, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma8b_of, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma8a, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma8b}
Let $j\ge1$ and $J\ge0$. Then $L_{j,J}\le\E G_m(J)\,\P(e_1<J)$, and $\P(e_1<J)\le\P(\nu<k)+\P(\Bin(k,1/4)<J)$ for every $k\ge1$.
\end{lemma}

The two lemmas give the recursion of the deficits from one height to the next, used by the step map of Section~\ref{sec:states}.

\begin{lemma}[deficit recursion]\label{lem:deficit}\lean{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemma17b_proof, FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemma17a_proof, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma17b, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma17a}
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every $m,k\ge0$, $\Delta_m(k+1)\le\Delta_m(k)$.
\item Let $j\ge1$, $J\ge0$ and $\gamma\ge\P(e_1<J)$ in the closure at height $m+1$ with $j$ entrants. Then
\[
\Delta_{m+1}(j)\le\Delta_m(J)\,(1-\gamma)+\mu_m(J)\,\gamma,
\]
and for $\gamma\le1$ the right side is nondecreasing in $\Delta_m(J)$ and in $\gamma$. For $J=0$ it reads $\Delta_{m+1}(j)\le\mu_m(0)=(m+1)/3$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

The second bound uses the event $\{e_1<J\}$, whose probability is small only when the children are likely to return many frogs. The next one uses a closure in which one child is dead, and works from the start. A random variable \emph{does not read} the child $c$ if it is a function of the directions, of the curves and coins of the two other children, and of auxiliary random variables independent of all the paths.

\begin{lemma}[a dead child]\label{lem:deadchild}\lean{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemmaS2'_proof, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemmaS2'}
Let $j\ge1$. For each child $c$ let $e'_c\in\N$ be a random variable that does not read $c$, with $e'_c\le e_c$. Then
\[
\Delta_{m+1}(j)\le\frac13\sum_{c=1}^3\E\,\Delta_m(e'_c).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:D1}]
\emph{Finiteness.} Let $s=(3^{m+1}-1)/2$, so that $G_c(e)\le e+s$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:awake}(2), and $F=q+3s$. Then $\Lambda_q(n)\le q+\bigl(n-n_0(n)\bigr)+3s$, so $\Lambda_q(n)>n$ forces $n_0(n)<F$. Infinitely many directions are $0$ almost surely, so $\nu$ is finite almost surely and $n_0(\nu\wedge n)\le F$ for every $n$. The event $\{\nu\ge i\}$ is decided by $D_1,\dots,D_{i-1}$ and the curves, so it is independent of $D_i$, and $\E n_a(\nu\wedge n)=\E(\nu\wedge n)/4$ for each $a$. By monotone convergence $\E\nu\le4F$, $\E e_c=\E n_0(\nu)=\E\nu/4$, and $\E G_c(e_c)\le\E\nu$ is finite.

\emph{The identity.} Each step from $r$ goes to $y$ or into a child, so $\nu=n_0(\nu)+\sum_ce_c$, and $\nu=\Lambda_q(\nu)=q+\sum_cG_c(e_c)$. Taking means, with $\E n_0(\nu)=\E e_c=\E\nu/4$,
\[
3\,\E n_0(\nu)=q+\sum_{c=1}^3\E\bigl[G_c(e_c)-e_c/3\bigr].
\]

\emph{The submartingale.} Fix $c$, let $\mathcal R_c$ be the $\sigma$-field of the directions and of the curves and coins of the two other children, and $\mathcal H_i$ the $\sigma$-field generated by $\mathcal R_c$, $G_c(0),\dots,G_c(i)$ and the coins $\xi^c_1,\dots,\xi^c_i$ of $c$. Then $M(i)=G_c(i)-i/3$ is $\mathcal H_i$-measurable, and $M(i)-M(i-1)\ge\xi^c_i-1/3$, where $\xi^c_i$ is independent of $\mathcal H_{i-1}$: it is independent of $G_c(0),\dots,G_c(i-1),\xi^c_1,\dots,\xi^c_{i-1}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:coins}, and all of these are independent of $\mathcal R_c$. So $M$ is a submartingale. Let $\tau_i$ be the time of the $i$-th direction $c$. Then $e_c\ge i$ if and only if $\Lambda_q(n)>n$ for every $n$ with $q\le n<\tau_i$, and for these $n$, $n_c(n)\le i-1$; so $\{e_c\ge i\}\in\mathcal H_{i-1}$, and $e_c$ is a stopping time. By optional sampling at the bounded times $e_c\wedge J\wedge n\le e_c\wedge n$, then monotone convergence in $n$ on each of the four terms, all of finite mean,
\[
\E\bigl[G_c(e_c)-e_c/3\bigr]\ge\E\bigl[G_c(e_c\wedge J)-(e_c\wedge J)/3\bigr].
\]
Summing over $c$, with $\E G_c(J)=\E G_m(J)$ and the definition of $L_{j,J}$, the identity gives $3\,\E n_0(\nu)\ge q+3\,\E G_m(J)-J-3L_{j,J}$, which is the claim since $q=j+1$ and $n_0(\nu)$ has the law of $G_{m+1}(j)$. This is \decl{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.d1_proof} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:loss}]
The term of $c$ in $L_{j,J}$ vanishes on $\{e_c\ge J\}$ and is at most $G_c(J)-G_c(e_c)$ on $\{e_c<J\}$. The closure reads the subtree of $c$ only through $G_c(0),\dots,G_c(e_c)$, so for $e<J$ the event $\{e_c=e\}$ is $\{(G_c(0),\dots,G_c(e),R)\in B_e\}$ for a set $B_e$, where $R$, the randomness off the child $c$, is independent of $G_c$. Conditionally on $R$, Lemma~\ref{lem:future} gives $\E[(G_c(J)-G_c(e))\one\{e_c=e\}]\le\P(e_c=e)\,\E G_m(J-e)\le\P(e_c=e)\,\E G_m(J)$. Summing over $e<J$ and using that the children are exchangeable gives the first bound. If $\nu\ge k$, the first $k$ directions are all used, so $e_1\ge n_1(k)$, which has the law $\Bin(k,1/4)$. This is \decl{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemma8a_proof} in the formalization, and \texttt{FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma8b\_of} for the second bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:deficit}]
(1) By Lemma~\ref{lem:coins}, $\E G_m(k+1)\ge\E G_m(k)+1/3$, and $\mu_m(k+1)=\mu_m(k)+1/3$. (2) Lemmas~\ref{lem:D1} and~\ref{lem:loss} give $\E G_{m+1}(j)\ge\E G_m(J)(1-\gamma)+(1+j-J)/3$, and $\mu_{m+1}(j)-(1+j-J)/3=\mu_m(J)$; so $\Delta_{m+1}(j)\le\mu_m(J)-\E G_m(J)(1-\gamma)$, which is the claim. For $\gamma\le1$ its derivatives in $\Delta_m(J)$ and in $\gamma$ are $1-\gamma\ge0$ and $\mu_m(J)-\Delta_m(J)=\E G_m(J)\ge0$. For $J=0$, $\P(e_1<0)=0$. This is \decl{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemma17b_proof} in the formalization, and \texttt{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemma17a\_proof} for~(1).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:deadchild}]
With $M$, $\mathcal R_c$ and $\mathcal H_i$ as in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:D1}, extend $\mathcal R_c$ by the auxiliary variables. Then $e'_c$ is $\mathcal H_0$-measurable, hence a stopping time with $e'_c\le e_c$, and optional sampling at $e'_c\wedge n\le e_c\wedge n$, then $n\to\infty$, gives $\E M(e_c)\ge\E M(e'_c)$. Since $e'_c$ is independent of the curve $G_c$, $\E M(e'_c)=\sum_k\P(e'_c=k)\bigl(\E G_m(k)-k/3\bigr)$. By the identity of that proof, $\Delta_{m+1}(j)=\mu_{m+1}(j)-\E n_0(\nu)=(m+1)/3-\frac13\sum_c\E M_c(e_c)$, where $M_c$ is the process $M$ of the child $c$. So
\[
\Delta_{m+1}(j)\le\frac13\sum_{c=1}^3\sum_k\P(e'_c=k)\Bigl(\frac{m+1+k}3-\E G_m(k)\Bigr)=\frac13\sum_{c=1}^3\E\,\Delta_m(e'_c).
\]
This is \decl{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemmaS2'_proof} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:rootstep}]
In the model of $Z_{m+1}$, consider the first step of the frog $f$ at $r$. If it is the step to $y$, which has probability $1/4$, then $f$ is frozen and $Z_{m+1}\ge1$. If it is the step into the child $c$, which has probability $1/4$ for each $c$, restrict the process to $\T(c)\cup\{r\}$: the frogs of $\T(c)$ at depth at most $m+1$, every path stopped at its first visit to $r$. This is the planted tree of height $m$ at $c$, with $r$ in the role of $y$ and the single entrant $f$, whose path from $c$ is a simple random walk independent of that first step; so the number $R_c$ of its frogs that visit $r$ has the law of $G_m(1)$. Each arc of the restricted process is an arc of the model of $Z_{m+1}$, so these frogs are woken there. After its first visit to $r$, the path of each of them is a simple random walk from $r$, independent of the paths up to their first visits to $r$ and of the other frogs, by the strong Markov property; it visits $y$ with probability $1/3$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:awake}(1)), and then the frog is counted by $Z_{m+1}$. So $\E[Z_{m+1}\mid f\text{ steps into }c]\ge\E G_m(1)/3$, and
\[
\E Z_{m+1}\ge\frac14+\frac34\cdot\frac13\,\E G_m(1)=\frac{1+\E G_m(1)}4.
\]
This is \decl{FrogModel.D3.LaneA.lemma19_proof} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

\section{Bounds for one closure}\label{sec:onebounds}

This section gives the bounds on one closure that the step map of Section~\ref{sec:states} and the induction of Section~\ref{sec:dprime} evaluate: tails of a variable below a sum of independent indicators (Lemma~\ref{lem:tails}), the end configurations of a closure (Lemmas~\ref{lem:ends} and~\ref{lem:pseudo}), and two lower bounds on the number of steps from $r$ (Lemmas~\ref{lem:fresh} and~\ref{lem:deep}). Here $\Bin(n,p)$ is a binomial variable, and for $0\le a\le1$ and $0<p<1$,
\[
\KL(a,p)=a\log\frac ap+(1-a)\log\frac{1-a}{1-p},
\]
with $0\log0=0$, is the relative entropy of the Bernoulli laws of parameters $a$ and $p$.

\subsection*{Tails below the all-awake count}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:awake}, $G_m(k)$ lies below the sum $U_m(k)$ of independent indicators, of mean $\mu_m(k)$, and the deficit $\Delta_m(k)$ measures how far below on average. Lemma~\ref{lem:tails} turns an upper bound on the deficit into upper bounds on the distribution function.

\begin{lemma}[tails]\label{lem:tails}\lean{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemma12_G_proof, FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemma12'_G_proof, FrogModel.D3.Assembly.cdfG_le_binCdf_proof, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma12_G, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma12_general_proof, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma12'_general_proof, FrogModel.D3.Iface.chernoff_binLt_proof, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma12_general, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma12'_general, FrogModel.D3.Iface.chernoff_binLt, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma12'_G, FrogModel.D3.Iface.cdfG_le_binCdf}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Let $U$ be the sum of the indicators of finitely many independent events, $\mu=\E U$, $X$ a random variable with $0\le X\le U$, and $\Delta=\mu-\E X$. For $x<\mu$,
\[
\P(X<x)\le\frac{\Delta+\mu^{1/2}/2}{\mu-x},
\]
and for $0<t<1$ and $g<(1-t)\mu$,
\[
\P(X\le g)\le\frac{\Delta}{(1-t)\mu-g}+e^{-t^2\mu/2}.
\]
\item For $n\ge1$, $0<p<1$ and an integer $k<np$, $\P(\Bin(n,p)<k)\le\exp\bigl(-n\KL(k/n,p)\bigr)$.
\item Let $m,k,v\ge0$, $\mu=\mu_m(k)$ and $D\ge\Delta_m(k)$. Then $\P(G_m(k)\le v)\le\P(\Bin(k,1/3)\le v)$; if $v+1<\mu$, then $\P(G_m(k)\le v)\le(D+\mu^{1/2}/2)/(\mu-v-1)$; and if $0<t<1$ and $v<(1-t)\mu$, then $\P(G_m(k)\le v)\le D/((1-t)\mu-v)+e^{-t^2\mu/2}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) Let $B=\{X<x\}$. Since $U-X\ge0$,
\[
\Delta\ge\E\bigl[(U-X)\one_B\bigr]\ge\E[U\one_B]-x\,\P(B)\ge\mu\,\P(B)-\E(\mu-U)_+-x\,\P(B),
\]
and $\E(\mu-U)_+=\E|U-\mu|/2\le(\mathrm{Var}\,U)^{1/2}/2\le\mu^{1/2}/2$, the variance of a sum of independent indicators being at most its mean. For the second bound, on $\{X\le g\}\cap\{U\ge(1-t)\mu\}$ the difference $U-X$ is at least $(1-t)\mu-g>0$, so $\Delta\ge((1-t)\mu-g)\,\P(X\le g,\ U\ge(1-t)\mu)$. Let $p_i$ be the probabilities of the events and $\lambda=-\log(1-t)$. Then
\[
\E e^{-\lambda U}=\prod_i\bigl(1-p_it\bigr)\le e^{-t\mu},\qquad\P\bigl(U<(1-t)\mu\bigr)\le e^{\lambda(1-t)\mu}\,\E e^{-\lambda U}\le e^{-\mu((1-t)\log(1-t)+t)}, % english-ok: display formula
\]
and $(1-t)\log(1-t)+t\ge t^2/2$ on $[0,1)$, since both sides vanish at $0$ and the derivatives are $-\log(1-t)\ge t$. (2) For $k\le0$ the probability is $0$. Otherwise let $a=k/n<p$ and $s=a(1-p)/((1-a)p)\in(0,1)$. Then $\P(\Bin(n,p)<k)\le\E s^{\Bin(n,p)-k}=s^{-k}(1-p+ps)^n=\exp(-n\KL(a,p))$. (3) The first bound is Lemma~\ref{lem:coins}. The other two are (1) with $X=G_m(k)$, which is finite by Lemma~\ref{lem:awake}(2), $U=U_m(k)$ and $\mu=\mu_m(k)$, at $x=v+1$ and $g=v$; the right sides are nondecreasing in $\Delta$. This is \decl{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemma12_G_proof} in the formalization, with \lname{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemma12'_G_proof} and \lname{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.cdfG_le_binCdf_proof}, from \lname{FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma12_general_proof}, \lname{FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma12'_general_proof} and \lname{FrogModel.D3.Iface.chernoff_binLt_proof}.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{End configurations}
Fix $m\ge0$, $j\ge1$ and $q=j+1$, and consider the closure at height $m+1$ with $j$ entrants of Section~\ref{sec:closure}: independent planted curves $G_1,G_2,G_3$ of height $m$, independent directions, and $\nu=\nu(q)$. For $n\ge0$ and $k=(k_0,k_1,k_2,k_3)\in\N^4$ with $k_0+k_1+k_2+k_3=n$, let
\[
\Mult(n;k)=\frac{n!}{k_0!\,k_1!\,k_2!\,k_3!}\,4^{-n}
\]
be the probability that the first $n$ directions take each value $a$ exactly $k_a$ times. For $e=(e_1,e_2,e_3)\in\N^3$ let $|e|=e_1+e_2+e_3$ and $S_e=G_1(e_1)+G_2(e_2)+G_3(e_3)$. For integers $v\ge0$, $\nu_0>q$ and $K>q$, and $s\ge0$, let
\begin{align*}
W^A_e(s)&=\Mult\bigl(q+s;(q+s-|e|,e)\bigr)\,\one\{|e|\le q+s,\ q+s-|e|\le v,\ q+s<\nu_0\},\\
W^B_e(s)&=\Mult\bigl(q+s;(q+s-|e|,e)\bigr)\,\one\{|e|\le q+s<K\}.
\end{align*}
Lemma~\ref{lem:ends} bounds the distribution function of the output and the probability of few entries into a child by the closures that end before a given time with few entries into each child.

\begin{lemma}[end configurations]\label{lem:ends}\lean{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemma16A_proof, FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemma16B_proof, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma16A, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma16B}
Let $E\ge1$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(A)] For $v\ge0$ and $\nu_0>q$,
\begin{multline*}
\P\bigl(G_{m+1}(j)\le v\bigr)\le\sum_{e\in[0,E)^3}\sum_{s<\nu_0}W^A_e(s)\,\P(S_e=s)\\
+3\,\P\bigl(\Bin(E+v,\tfrac12)\ge E\bigr)\,\P\bigl(G_m(E)\le \nu_0-q-1\bigr)+\P\bigl(\Bin(\nu_0,\tfrac14)\le v\bigr). % english-ok: display formula
\end{multline*}
\item[(B)] For $1\le J\le E$ and $K>q$, with $e$ running over the triples with $e_1<J$ and $e_2,e_3<E$,
\begin{multline*}
\P(e_1<J)\le\sum_{e}\sum_{s<K}W^B_e(s)\,\P(S_e=s)\\
+2\,\P\bigl(\Bin(E+J-1,\tfrac12)\ge E\bigr)\,\P\bigl(G_m(E)\le K-q-1\bigr)+\P\bigl(\Bin(K,\tfrac14)<J\bigr).
\end{multline*}
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:closid} we may bound the output $X=n_0(\nu)$ of the closure. (A) If $\nu\ge \nu_0$, then $X\ge n_0(\nu_0)$, which has the law $\Bin(\nu_0,1/4)$. If $\nu<\nu_0$ and $e_c\ge E$ for some child $c$, let $\tau\le\nu$ be the time of the $E$-th direction $c$. Then $G_c(E)\le G_c(e_c)\le\Lambda_q(\nu)-q=\nu-q\le \nu_0-q-1$, and $X$ is at least the number of steps to $y$ before $\tau$, so at most $v$ steps to $y$ precede the $E$-th direction $c$. In the subsequence of the directions in $\{0,c\}$, the terms are independent and fair, and this happens if and only if at least $E$ of the first $E+v$ terms are $c$. The first event reads the curve of $c$ and the second the directions, so they are independent; summing over $c$ gives the middle term. Otherwise $\nu=n<\nu_0$ and $e=(n_1(n),n_2(n),n_3(n))\in[0,E)^3$; then $\Lambda_q(n)=n$, that is $S_e=n-q$, and $n_0(n)=X\le v$. So the event lies in the union over $n<\nu_0$ and over $k=(k_0,e)$ with $k_0\le v$, $e\in[0,E)^3$ and $|k|=n$ of $\{(n_a(n))_a=k\}\cap\{S_e=n-q\}$. These two events are independent, the first has probability $\Mult(n;k)$, and with $s=n-q\ge0$ the union bound is the double sum. (B) The same, with three changes: if $\nu\ge K$ then $e_1\ge n_1(K)$, of law $\Bin(K,1/4)$; if $\nu<K$ and $e_c\ge E$ for $c\in\{2,3\}$, then fewer than $J$ directions $1$ precede the $E$-th direction $c$, so at least $E$ of the first $E+J-1$ terms of the subsequence in $\{1,c\}$ are $c$; and $e_1\ge E$ is excluded by $e_1<J\le E$. This is \decl{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemma16A_proof} in the formalization, and \texttt{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemma16B\_proof} for (B).
\end{proof}

The double sums of Lemma~\ref{lem:ends} need the laws of the $S_e$, which are not known. Lemma~\ref{lem:pseudo} replaces them by distribution functions bounded from above. Let $G_M\ge0$, and let $F(e,g)\in[0,1]$, for $0\le e\le E$ and $0\le g\le G_M$, be nondecreasing in $g$. The \emph{pseudo-law} of the row $e$ puts the mass $p_e(g)=F(e,g)-F(e,g-1)$ at $g\le G_M$, with $F(e,-1)=0$, and the rest at $+\infty$; for $e\in[0,E]^3$ and $s\ge0$ let
\[
\pi_e(s)=\sum_{g_1+g_2+g_3=s}p_{e_1}(g_1)\,p_{e_2}(g_2)\,p_{e_3}(g_3),
\]
with $p_e(g)=0$ for $g>G_M$, the mass at $s$ of the sum of three independent variables of these pseudo-laws. For $W:\N\to[0,\infty)$ and $\bar s\ge0$ let $\overline W(s)=\max_{s\le s'\le \bar s}W(s')$, the least nonincreasing function above $W$ on $[0,\bar s]$.

\begin{lemma}[pseudo-laws]\label{lem:pseudo}\lean{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemma16C_proof, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma16C, FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemma16AC_proof, FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemma16BC_proof, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma16AC, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma16BC, FrogModel.D3.Iface.boundA, FrogModel.D3.Iface.boundB}
Assume $\P(G_m(e)\le g)\le F(e,g)$ for $0\le e\le E$ and $0\le g\le G_M$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $e\in[0,E]^3$, $W\ge0$ vanishes beyond $G_M$ and $\bar s\ge0$, then $\sum_{s<\bar s}W(s)\,\P(S_e=s)\le\sum_{s<\bar s}\overline W(s)\,\pi_e(s)$.
\item If $\nu_0-q-1\le G_M$, the bound (A) of Lemma~\ref{lem:ends} holds with $W^A_e$ replaced by $\overline{W^A_e}$ (with $\bar s=\nu_0$), $\P(S_e=s)$ by $\pi_e(s)$, and $\P(G_m(E)\le \nu_0-q-1)$ by $F(E,\nu_0-q-1)$. If $K-q-1\le G_M$, the same holds for (B), with $\bar s=K$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) Let $\widehat W=\overline W$ on $[0,\bar s)$ and $\widehat W(\bar s)=0$. Then $\widehat W$ is nonincreasing and nonnegative, $\widehat W\ge W$, and $\widehat W$ vanishes beyond $G_M$. Summation by parts gives $\sum_{s<\bar s}\widehat W(s)\,\P(S_e=s)=\sum_{s<\bar s}(\widehat W(s)-\widehat W(s+1))\,\P(S_e\le s)$, with nonnegative coefficients, which vanish for $s>G_M$. For $s\le G_M$, let $Y_1,Y_2,Y_3$ be independent with the pseudo-laws of the rows $e_1,e_2,e_3$. Since $\P(G_c(e_c)\le g)\le\P(Y_c\le g)$ for $g\le G_M$, the variable $\min(Y_c,G_M+1)$ lies stochastically below $\min(G_c(e_c),G_M+1)$; coupling the two by quantiles, independently over $c$, gives $\P(S_e\le s)\le\P(Y_1+Y_2+Y_3\le s)=\sum_{s'\le s}\pi_e(s')$. Summation by parts back gives the bound. (2) Every $W^A_e(s)$ vanishes for $s\ge \nu_0-q$, hence beyond $G_M$, and the same for $W^B_e$ with $K$; apply (1) to each $e$, and the hypothesis to $\P(G_m(E)\le \nu_0-q-1)$. This is \decl{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemma16C_proof} in the formalization, with \texttt{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemma16AC\_proof} and \texttt{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemma16BC\_proof} for (2).
\end{proof}

We write $A_F(j,v,\nu_0)$ and $B_F(j,J,K)$ for the right sides of (2).

\subsection*{Fresh children}
The term $\P(\nu<k)$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:loss} asks for lower bounds on the number of steps from $r$. The first one waits for one of the children entered by the first $q$ steps to return many frogs.

\begin{lemma}[fresh children]\label{lem:fresh}\lean{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemma9_proof, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma9, FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemma9_two_proof, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma9_two, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma9Bound, FrogModel.D3.Iface.SPoly}
Let $q\ge2$, $x\ge1$ and $p=\P(G_m(1)\ge x)$, and let $(a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3)$ be the numbers of directions $0,1,2,3$ among the first $q$. In the closure at height $m+1$,
\[
\P\bigl(\nu(q)<x\bigr)\le\E\Bigl[(1-p)^{\#\{c:\ a_c\ge1\}}\Bigr],
\]
and for $q=2$ this is $1-\chi(p)$, with $\chi(p)=\frac{15}{16}-\frac9{16}(1-p)-\frac38(1-p)^2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $\nu\ge q$ and $\Lambda_q$ is nondecreasing, $\nu\ge\Lambda_q(\nu)\ge\Lambda_q(q)=q+\sum_cG_c(a_c)$ when $\nu$ is finite. If some child $c$ with $a_c\ge1$ has $G_c(1)\ge x$, then $G_c(a_c)\ge x$ and $\nu\ge x$. The $G_c(1)$ are independent, of the law of $G_m(1)$, and independent of the directions. For $q=2$ the first two directions are two steps to $y$ with probability $1/16$, one step to $y$ and one into a child with probability $6/16$, twice the same child with probability $3/16$, and two different children with probability $6/16$, which gives $1/16+(9/16)(1-p)+(6/16)(1-p)^2=1-\chi(p)$. This is \decl{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemma9_proof} in the formalization, and \texttt{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemma9\_two\_proof} for $q=2$.
\end{proof}

The second one sends the first $q$ frogs deep into the tree, where they meet fresh subtrees with high probability.

\begin{lemma}[deep fresh trials]\label{lem:deep}\lean{FrogModel.D3.LaneA.lemma10_proof, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma10}
Let $q\ge2$, $1\le w\le m+1$, $x,x'\ge1$, and $p_w=\P(G_{m+1-w}(1)\ge x')$. In the closure at height $m+1$,
\[
\P\bigl(\nu(q)<x\bigr)\le\Bigl(1-\bigl(\tfrac23-(q-1)\,3^{-w}\bigr)p_w\Bigr)^q+\P\bigl(\Bin(x',3^{1-w})<x\bigr).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:closid}, $\nu(q)$ has the law of the number of pairs of a woken frog and a time at which it is at $r$, in the planted model of height $m+1$ with $q-1$ entrants; we bound that number from below on the frog paths, by waking only some of the frogs, which can only lower it. Number the $q$ frogs at $r$ at the start $1,\dots,q$, and follow the path of the frog $i$ until it reaches depth $w$ or visits $y$. It reaches depth $w$ before visiting $y$ with probability at least $2/3$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:awake}(1)), and then its first vertex $v_i$ at depth $w$ is uniform over the $3^w$ vertices of that depth, by the symmetries of the tree that fix $r$, independently of the frogs $1,\dots,i-1$; so it reaches depth $w$ at a vertex $v_i$ not among $v_1,\dots,v_{i-1}$ with probability at least $\frac23-(i-1)3^{-w}$. Before depth $w$ the path stays above it, so it enters no $\T(v_l)$. If $v_i$ is new, run in $\T(v_i)$ the planted model of height $m+1-w$ with the single entrant $i$ and the parent of $v_i$ in the role of $y$: the number of frogs it returns to the parent of $v_i$ has the law of $G_{m+1-w}(1)$, and it is independent of the paths used before, which lie in other subtrees or are earlier segments. With probability at least $(\frac23-(q-1)3^{-w})p_w$, given the past, the frog $i$ succeeds: $v_i$ is new and at least $x'$ frogs return. Then the continuations of the paths of $x'$ of these frogs after their first visits to the parent of $v_i$, at depth $w-1$, are independent simple random walks, and each visits $r$ with probability $3^{1-w}$; each visit is a pair counted by $\nu(q)$. So $\P(\nu(q)<x)$ is at most the probability that all $q$ frogs fail, plus $\P(\Bin(x',3^{1-w})<x)$. This is \decl{FrogModel.D3.LaneA.lemma10_proof} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

\section{States and the step map}\label{sec:states}

This section turns the bounds of Sections~\ref{sec:closure} and~\ref{sec:onebounds} into a map $\Phi$ on finite tables, which carries upper bounds on the distribution functions and the deficits of the planted curves from one height to the next (Lemma~\ref{lem:step}). Two more bounds enter first: closures with lower curves for the children, whose laws are given by a finite recursion (Lemma~\ref{lem:lower}).

\subsection*{Lower closures}
Let $m\ge0$ and $G_M\ge0$, and let $F_1:\{0,\dots,G_M\}\to[0,1]$ be nondecreasing with $\P(G_m(1)\le g)\le F_1(g)$ for $g\le G_M$. Put $\widetilde F(g)=F_1(g)$ for $g\le G_M$ and $\widetilde F(G_M+1)=1$, and let $\rho(0)=\widetilde F(0)$ and $\rho(i)=\widetilde F(i)-\widetilde F(i-1)$ for $1\le i\le G_M+1$: the law of a variable $R$ with $\P(R\le g)=\widetilde F(g)$.

For a cap $\bar k\ge1$, numbers $p_{\mathrm c},p_{\mathrm g},p_{\mathrm n}\ge0$ and a family of laws $A'(u)$ on $\{0,\dots,\bar k\}$, $u\ge0$, the \emph{stage} with these data is the family of laws $A(u)$ on $\{0,\dots,\bar k\}$ given by $A(0)=\delta_0$ and
\[
A(u+1)=p_{\mathrm c}\,\mathrm{up}\,A(u)+p_{\mathrm g}\,A(u)+p_{\mathrm n}\sum_{i=0}^{G_M+1}\rho(i)\,A'(u+i),
\]
where $\mathrm{up}$ moves the mass at $k$ to $k+1$ for $k<\bar k$ and keeps the mass at $\bar k$. A stage reads the pool size $u$ of a closure in which every child is either dead (it returns nothing), or live and fresh (its first entry returns $R$ frogs), or live and entered before (each later entry returns its frog with probability $1/3$); $p_{\mathrm c}$ is the probability that the next step is counted, $p_{\mathrm g}$ that its frog is gone, and $p_{\mathrm n}$ that it enters a fresh live child, which moves to the next stage $A'$, all for the jump chain in which a frog that comes back at once is not a step.

Let $a^{(2)}_q$ be the law $A_0(q)$ of the stages $A_2,A_1,A_0$ with cap $E\ge1$ and data $(p_{\mathrm c},p_{\mathrm g},p_{\mathrm n})$ equal to $(\frac3{10},\frac7{10},0)$, $(\frac3{11},\frac5{11},\frac3{11})$ and $(\frac14,\frac14,\frac12)$, where $A_2$ uses $A'=0$, $A_1$ uses $A'=A_2$ and $A_0$ uses $A'=A_1$. Let $a^{(3)}_q$ be the law $A_0(q)$ of the stages $A_3,\dots,A_0$ with cap $G_M+1$ and data $(\frac13,\frac23,0)$, $(\frac3{10},\frac4{10},\frac3{10})$, $(\frac3{11},\frac2{11},\frac6{11})$ and $(\frac14,0,\frac34)$.

\begin{lemma}[lower closures]\label{lem:lower}\lean{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemmaS2_proof, FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemmaS3_proof, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemmaS2, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemmaS3, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lawS2, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lawS3, FrogModel.D3.Iface.stageLaw}
Let $j\ge1$ and $F_1$ be as above.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $E\ge1$ and $D(k)\ge\Delta_m(k)$ for $0\le k\le E$, then
\[
\Delta_{m+1}(j)\le\sum_{k=0}^Ea^{(2)}_{j+1}(k)\,\min_{k'\le k}D(k').
\]
\item For $v\le G_M$, $\P(G_{m+1}(j)\le v)\le\sum_{x\le v}a^{(3)}_{j+1}(x)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Lower curves.} Consider the closure at height $m+1$ with $j$ entrants, $q=j+1$, with the coins $\xi_{s,e}$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:coins} for each child $s$, and an auxiliary uniform variable $\vartheta_s$ on $(0,1)$ for each child, independent of everything. Let $\upsilon_s=\P(G_m(1)<G_s(1))+\vartheta_s\,\P(G_m(1)=G_s(1))$, where the probabilities are those of the law of $G_m(1)$ evaluated at the value $G_s(1)$; then $\upsilon_s$ is uniform on $(0,1)$. Let $R_s=\min\{g\le G_M+1:\widetilde F(g)\ge \upsilon_s\}$ and $G''_s(0)=0$, $G''_s(k)=R_s+\xi_{s,2}+\dots+\xi_{s,k}$. If $G_s(1)\le G_M$, then $\widetilde F(G_s(1))\ge\P(G_m(1)\le G_s(1))\ge \upsilon_s$, so $R_s\le G_s(1)$; otherwise $R_s\le G_M+1\le G_s(1)$. With $G_s(e)\ge G_s(e-1)+\xi_{s,e}$, $G''_s\le G_s$. The variable $R_s$ has the law $\rho$, and $R_s,\xi_{s,2},\xi_{s,3},\dots$ are independent, since $\xi_{s,e}$ is independent of $(G_s(0),\dots,G_s(e-1),\xi_{s,2},\dots,\xi_{s,e-1})$.

\emph{The two lower closures.} For (1), let the child $1$ be dead and the two others answer $G''_s$: $\Lambda''(n)=q+\sum_{s=2,3}G''_s(n_s(n))$, $\nu''$ the least $n\ge q$ with $\Lambda''(n)\le n$, and $e''=n_1(\nu'')$. Since $\Lambda''\le\Lambda_q$, $\nu''\le\nu$ and $e''\le e_1$; and $e''$ does not read the child $1$, the variables $\vartheta_s$ being auxiliary. By Lemma~\ref{lem:deadchild}, applied with the same construction for each child by symmetry, $\Delta_{m+1}(j)\le\E\Delta_m(e'')$, and $\Delta_m(k)\le\Delta_m(\min(k,E))\le\min_{k'\le\min(k,E)}D(k')$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:deficit}(1). For (2), let the three children answer $G''_s$, with $\nu''$ defined in the same way and $X''=n_0(\nu'')$; then $X''\le n_0(\nu)$, which has the law of $G_{m+1}(j)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:closid}.

\emph{The laws.} Process the frogs at $r$ one at a time. A step goes to $y$ with probability $1/4$ and into each child with probability $1/4$; into a dead child the frog is lost; into a live child not entered before it adds $R_s$ frogs to the pool, independently of the past; into a live child entered before, the frog comes back with probability $1/3$, independently. A frog that comes back at once does not change the counts, so they have the law of the jump chain without these returns. With $f$ the number of live children entered before, one step of the jump chain of (1) is: into the dead child, to a fresh live child or gone, with probabilities $(\frac14,\frac12,\frac14)$, $(\frac3{11},\frac3{11},\frac5{11})$ and $(\frac3{10},0,\frac7{10})$ for $f=0,1,2$; and of (2): to $y$, to a fresh child or lost, with probabilities $(\frac14,\frac34,0)$, $(\frac3{11},\frac6{11},\frac2{11})$, $(\frac3{10},\frac3{10},\frac4{10})$ and $(\frac13,0,\frac23)$ for $f=0,1,2,3$. A first-step analysis gives that the law of the count, capped at $E$ in (1) and at $G_M+1$ in (2), from a pool of $u$ frogs at stage $f$ is $A_f(u)$; so $\min(e'',E)$ has the law $a^{(2)}_q$, and $\P(X''\le v)=\sum_{x\le v}a^{(3)}_q(x)$ for $v\le G_M$. This is \decl{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemmaS2_proof} in the formalization, and \texttt{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemmaS3\_proof} for~(2).
\end{proof}

\subsection*{States}
The parameters of a state are integers $E\ge1$, $G_M$, $V_M\le G_M$ and $J_M\le E$: the number of entrants tracked, the range of the distribution functions, the largest value at which Lemma~\ref{lem:pseudo}(A) is evaluated, and the largest comparison index.

\begin{definition}[states]\label{def:state}\lean{FrogModel.D3.Iface.StParams, FrogModel.D3.Iface.State, FrogModel.D3.Iface.State.WF, FrogModel.D3.Iface.State.Le, FrogModel.D3.Iface.Valid}
A \emph{state} is a pair $S=(F,\delta)$ of tables $F(k,g)\in[0,1]$, for $0\le k\le E$ and $0\le g\le G_M$, nondecreasing in $g$, and $\delta(k)\in[0,1]$, for $0\le k\le E$, with $F(0,\cdot)=1$ and $\delta(0)=1$. It is \emph{valid at height} $m$ if, for all $k\le E$ and $g\le G_M$,
\[
\P\bigl(G_m(k)\le g\bigr)\le F(k,g),\qquad\Delta_m(k)\le\delta(k)\,\mu_m(k). % english-ok: display formula
\]
States are ordered componentwise, and a state above a valid state is valid.
\end{definition}

\subsection*{The step map}
Let $a\le b$ be heights; the map $\Phi_{[a,b]}$ bounds a parent at any height $m+1$ with $m\in[a,b]$ from children valid for $S$ at height $m$. For a state $S=(F,\delta)$, let $\lambda(D,\mu,v)$ be the minimum of the two bounds of Lemma~\ref{lem:tails}(3), the second one for $t=i/200$, $i=1,\dots,199$, each read as $1$ where its condition fails; let
\[
r(k',j)=\max_{h\in\{a,b\}}\frac{\mu_h(k')}{\mu_{h+1}(j)},\qquad\widetilde\delta_j(k)=\min_{k'\le k}\delta(k')\,r(k',j),
\]
and let $\mathcal G(q)$ be the set of the integers $\min(q+o,q+G_M+1)$ for $o\in\{8,16,24,32,48,\allowbreak64,96,128,192,256\}$, together with $q+G_M+1$. With $A_F$ and $B_F$ the bounds of Lemma~\ref{lem:pseudo}(2), $q=j+1$, let $\gamma_B(j,J)=\min_{K\in\mathcal G(j+1)}B_F(j,J,K)$, and let $a^{(2)}_q$ and $a^{(3)}_q$ be the laws of Lemma~\ref{lem:lower} with $F_1=F(1,\cdot)$.

\begin{definition}[the step map]\label{def:phi}\lean{FrogModel.D3.Iface.phiS, FrogModel.D3.Iface.deltaNew, FrogModel.D3.Iface.dTerms, FrogModel.D3.Iface.Fnew, FrogModel.D3.Iface.preF, FrogModel.D3.Iface.L12, FrogModel.D3.Iface.PB, FrogModel.D3.Iface.PA, FrogModel.D3.Iface.grid, FrogModel.D3.Iface.ratio, FrogModel.D3.Iface.Dt}
The state $\Phi_{[a,b]}(S)$ is $(F',\delta')$, where $\delta'(0)=1$, $F'(0,\cdot)=1$, and for $j=1,\dots,E$, in this order:
\begin{align*}
\delta'(j)&=\min\Bigl\{r(0,j);\ \bigl(\delta(J)(1-\gamma_B(j,J))+\gamma_B(j,J)\bigr)\,r(J,j)\ (1\le J\le J_M);\\
&\qquad\qquad\sum_{k=0}^Ea^{(2)}_{j+1}(k)\,\widetilde\delta_j(k);\ \delta'(j-1)\ (j\ge2)\Bigr\},
\end{align*}
then, for $j=1,\dots,E$ and $v\le G_M$, $F'(j,v)=\min_{v\le v'\le G_M}f(j,v')$ with
\begin{align*}
f(j,v)=\min\Bigl\{&\P\bigl(\Bin(j,\tfrac13)\le v\bigr);\ \lambda\bigl(\delta'(j)\,\mu_{a+1}(j),\mu_{a+1}(j),v\bigr);\ \min_{\nu_0\in\mathcal G(j+1)}A_F(j,v,\nu_0)\ (v\le V_M);\\
&\textstyle\sum_{x\le v}a^{(3)}_{j+1}(x);\ F'(j-1,v)\Bigr\}.
\end{align*}
We write $\Phi_m=\Phi_{[m,m]}$.
\end{definition}

The grids $\mathcal G(q)$ hold the times $\nu_0$ and $K$ at which Lemma~\ref{lem:ends} cuts the closure; their largest element $q+G_M+1$ keeps $\nu_0-q-1$ and $K-q-1$ in the range of the table. A state $S'$ at larger parameters is obtained from $S$ by the extension of Definition~\ref{def:ext}.

\begin{definition}[extension]\label{def:ext}\lean{FrogModel.D3.Iface.ext, FrogModel.D3.Iface.deltaExt, FrogModel.D3.Iface.FExt, FrogModel.D3.Iface.preExt}
Let $S=(F,\delta)$ be a state at parameters with $(E_0,G_0)$, let $E\ge E_0$ and $G_M\ge G_0$, and let $h$ be a height. The extension $\mathrm{ext}_h(S)=(\widehat F,\widehat\delta)$ at $(E,G_M)$ is given by $\widehat\delta(0)=1$, $\widehat F(0,\cdot)=1$, and for $k=1,\dots,E$:
\begin{align*}
\widehat\delta(k)&=\min\Bigl\{1;\ \frac{\mu_h(0)}{\mu_h(k)};\ \widehat\delta(k-1);\ \delta(k)\text{ if }k\le E_0,\ \text{else }\delta(E_0)\frac{\mu_h(E_0)}{\mu_h(k)}\Bigr\},\\
\widehat F(k,g)&=\min_{g\le g'\le G_M}\min\Bigl\{F(k,g')\text{ if }k\le E_0\text{ and }g'\le G_0;\ \P\bigl(\Bin(k,\tfrac13)\le g'\bigr);\\ % english-ok: display formula
&\qquad\qquad\lambda\bigl(\widehat\delta(k)\mu_h(k),\mu_h(k),g'\bigr);\ \widehat F(k-1,g')\Bigr\}. % english-ok: display formula
\end{align*}
\end{definition}

Lemma~\ref{lem:step}, used in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:linear}, says that $\Phi$ and the extension preserve validity, and how a certificate of steps is read.

\begin{lemma}[step]\label{lem:step}\lean{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemma18_1_proof, FrogModel.D3.LaneC.lemma18_3_proof, FrogModel.D3.Assembly.ext_valid_proof, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma18_1, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma18_3, FrogModel.D3.Iface.ext_valid, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma18_4_of, FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma18_6_of, FrogModel.D3.Iface.CertStep}
Let $S$ be a state.
\begin{enumerate}
\item (One step.) If $S$ is valid at $m$, then $\Phi_m(S)$ is valid at $m+1$.
\item (Worst case.) If $a\le m\le b$, then $\Phi_m(S)\le\Phi_{[a,b]}(S)$.
\item (Extension.) If $S$ is valid at $h$, then $\mathrm{ext}_h(S)$ is valid at $h$.
\item (Interval.) If $S$ is valid at $a$, and $\Phi_{[a,b-1]}(S)\le S$, then $S$ is valid at every height of $[a,b]$.
\item (Certificate.) Let $S_0$ be a state valid at $h_0$. For $i<n$ let $S_{i+1}$ be a state with either $S_{i+1}\ge\Phi_{h_i}(S_i)$ at the same parameters and $h_{i+1}=h_i+1$, or $S_{i+1}\ge\mathrm{ext}_{h_i}(S_i)$ at larger $E$ and $G_M$ and $h_{i+1}=h_i$. If $T\ge S_n$ is a state and $\Phi_{[h_n,b-1]}(T)\le T$, then $T$ is valid at every height of $[h_n,b]$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) The parent curve at height $m+1$ with $j$ entrants is the output of the closure with $j$ entrants whose children have curves of height $m$, for which $S$ is valid. Deficits, with $a=b=m$, so that $r(k',j)=\mu_m(k')/\mu_{m+1}(j)$: the first term is Lemma~\ref{lem:deficit}(2) with $J=0$; for $1\le J\le J_M$, $\gamma_B(j,J)\ge\P(e_1<J)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:pseudo}(2); if $\gamma_B(j,J)\le1$, Lemma~\ref{lem:deficit}(2) with $\Delta_m(J)\le\delta(J)\mu_m(J)$ gives the second, and if $\gamma_B(j,J)>1$, Lemma~\ref{lem:deficit}(2) with $\gamma=1$ gives $\Delta_{m+1}(j)\le\mu_m(J)\le(\delta(J)(1-\gamma_B(j,J))+\gamma_B(j,J))\mu_m(J)$, since $\delta(J)\le1$; the third is Lemma~\ref{lem:lower}(1) with $D(k)=\delta(k)\mu_m(k)$; the fourth is $\Delta_{m+1}(j)\le\Delta_{m+1}(j-1)\le\delta'(j-1)\mu_{m+1}(j-1)\le\delta'(j-1)\mu_{m+1}(j)$, by Lemma~\ref{lem:deficit}(1) and $\delta'(j-1)\ge0$. Distribution functions: the binomial term and the term $\lambda$ are Lemma~\ref{lem:tails}(3), with $\Delta_{m+1}(j)\le\delta'(j)\mu_{m+1}(j)$; the term $A_F$ is Lemma~\ref{lem:pseudo}(2); the term $a^{(3)}$ is Lemma~\ref{lem:lower}(2); the term $F'(j-1,v)$ holds since $G_{m+1}(j)\ge G_{m+1}(j-1)$; and a distribution function is nondecreasing, which gives the minimum over $v'\ge v$. Each term is a valid bound given the components computed before it. (2) The bounds of Lemmas~\ref{lem:pseudo} and~\ref{lem:lower} do not depend on the height. The ratio $\mu_h(k')/\mu_{h+1}(j)=(h+1+k')/(h+2+j)$ is monotone in $h$, so its maximum over $[a,b]$ is at $a$ or $b$; the bounds of Lemma~\ref{lem:tails}(3) at a fixed ratio $D/\mu$ decrease in $\mu$, so over $[a+1,b+1]$ they are largest at $\mu_{a+1}$; and the terms that read components computed before are nondecreasing in them. So the comparison holds component by component, in the order of the definition. (3) Rows $k\le E_0$ at $g\le G_0$ are those of $S$. The term $1$ holds since $\E G_h(k)\ge0$, the term $\mu_h(0)/\mu_h(k)$ since $\E G_h(k)\ge k/3$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:coins}), the term $\widehat\delta(k-1)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:deficit}(1) and $\mu_h(k-1)\le\mu_h(k)$, and for $k>E_0$ the last one since $\Delta_h(k)\le\Delta_h(E_0)$. The terms of $\widehat F$ are those of (1). (4) By induction on $m\in[a,b-1]$: if $S$ is valid at $m$, then $\Phi_m(S)$ is valid at $m+1$ by (1), and $\Phi_m(S)\le\Phi_{[a,b-1]}(S)\le S$ by (2). (5) By induction on $i$, with (1) and (3) and the order, $S_n$ is valid at $h_n$, so $T$ is, and (4) applies. This is \decl{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.lemma18_1_proof} in the formalization for (1), with \texttt{FrogModel.D3.LaneC.lemma18\_3\_proof}, \texttt{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.ext\_valid\_proof}, \texttt{FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma18\_4\_of} and \texttt{FrogModel.D3.Iface.lemma18\_6\_of}.
\end{proof}

\section{A lower model and the seed}\label{sec:lower}

The step map needs a state valid at some height to start from. This section builds one at height $100$ from a reading of the frog paths that wakes fewer frogs than the planted model (Lemma~\ref{lem:dom}) and whose count has a law given by a finite recursion (Lemma~\ref{lem:m1law}); Proposition~\ref{prop:F3} turns a stored run of the recursion, rounded down, into bounds on $G_m(k)$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:seed} into a valid state.

\subsection*{The rule}
Fix the planted tree of height $m\ge1$ with $k\ge1$ entrants at $r$, and integers $\bar a\ge1$ (an answer cap), $P\ge2$ (a pool cap) and $L\ge2$ (a ghost depth). For a vertex $v$ of depth at most $m$, of height $h(v)$, the rule keeps a set of \emph{marked} vertices, initially empty, and $f(v)$ denotes the number of unmarked children of $v$. The rule reads the paths one step at a time, together with independent \emph{kill coins}; the keep probabilities $\kappa^R_h(f;a,f')$ and $\kappa^H_h(f;a,f')$ in $[0,1]$, for $0\le h<m$, are equal to $1$ at $(a,f')=(0,0)$, and at $h=0$ they depend on $a$ only. An R closure is the first closure at its vertex, so it starts with $f_0=3$, and we write $\kappa^R_h(a,f')$ for $\kappa^R_h(3;a,f')$.

A \emph{closure} $C(v,Q,t)$ at a vertex $v$, with a list $Q$ of frogs at $v$ and a type $t\in\{R,H\}$, holds a pool, initially $Q$, and a list of \emph{ups}, initially empty, and records $f_0=f(v)$ at its start. While the pool is not empty, it takes the frog $\varphi$ at the head of the pool and reads the next step of its path.
\begin{itemize}
\item A step to the parent of $v$: $\varphi$ joins the ups if there are fewer than $\bar a$, and is discarded otherwise.
\item A step to a child $z$, when $h(v)=0$, or when $t=H$ and $z$ is marked (a \emph{ghost entry}): the rule reads the steps of $\varphi$ until it is back at $v$, and then puts it at the head of the pool, or at depth $L$ below $v$, and then discards it.
\item A step to an unmarked child $z$, when $h(v)\ge1$: mark $z$, wake the frog $\psi_z$ of $z$, run $C(z,(\varphi,\psi_z),R)$ (an \emph{R entry}).
\item A step to a marked child $z$, when $t=R$ and $h(v)\ge1$: run $C(z,(\varphi),H)$ (an \emph{H entry}).
\item The frogs returned by an entry are appended to the pool, which is then cut to its first $P$ frogs.
\end{itemize}
When the pool is empty, a closure at $v\ne r$ has the outcome $(a,f')$, with $a$ the number of its ups and $f'=f(v)$ (and $f'=0$ if $h(v)=0$); it reads a kill coin, which keeps the outcome with probability $\kappa^t_{h(v)}(f_0;a,f')$. A kept closure returns its ups to its parent; a killed one returns nothing and marks every child of $v$, so that its outcome becomes $(0,0)$. The run wakes the frog of $r$ and runs $C(r,(\text{entrants},\psi_r),R)$; its count $\underline G\le \bar a$ is the number of ups of this closure, which are frozen at $y$. We call this rule $M1_L$.

\begin{lemma}[domination]\label{lem:dom}\lean{FrogModel.D3.f1_gen}
For every family of paths and coins, and after any number of reads, the number of ups of the top closure of $M1_L$ is at most $G_m(k)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The rule moves every frog along its own path. By induction on the reads, every frog the rule holds is in the woken set of Definition~\ref{def:plantedcurve}: the entrants are; the frog of $r$ is woken at the start, and $r$ is the start of the path of an entrant; the frog of $z$ is woken right after a step of a frog of the woken set that ends at $z$, which then lies in the range of its path. A frog that is an up of the top closure has stepped from $r$ to $y$, so its path visits $y$; and the ups are distinct frogs. This is \decl{FrogModel.D3.f1_gen} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{The recursion}
Let $p_L=(3^{L-1}-1)/(3^L-1)$, the probability that a walk on $\{0,\dots,L\}$ with steps $+1$ with probability $3/4$ and $-1$ with probability $1/4$, started at $1$, hits $0$ before $L$: the probability that a ghost entry brings its frog back. Let $q_L=1/(4-3p_L)$, the probability that a frog at height $0$ steps up before it is lost. The laws below are on pairs $(a,f')\in\{0,\dots,\bar a\}\times\{0,\dots,3\}$; the \emph{bottom} is $(0,0)$; $\mathrm{up}(\ell)$ is the law $\ell$ shifted by one in its first coordinate, with $\bar a$ absorbing, and $\bar\ell$ is the first marginal of $\ell$. At height $0$, $\widehat R_0$ is the law of $(\min(\Bin(2,q_L),\bar a),0)$, and $\widehat K_0(f\to\cdot)$ puts the mass $q_L$ at $(1,f)$ and $1-q_L$ at $(0,f)$. Only $\widehat K_0(0\to\cdot)$ is read by the recursions below: an outcome at height $0$ has $f'=0$, so an entered child of height $0$ has type $U_0$.

At a height $h\ge1$, given a law $\rho$, let $Y_f(0)=\delta_{(0,f)}$ and, for $s\ge1$,
\[
Y_f(s)=\frac{\frac14\,\mathrm{up}(Y_f(s-1))+\frac{3-f}4(1-p_L)\,Y_f(s-1)+\frac f4\sum_b\bar\rho(b)\,Y_{f-1}(\min(s-1+b,P))}{1-(3-f)p_L/4},
\]
and $\widehat K_h[\rho](f\to\cdot)=Y_f(1)$: the law of an H closure with one frog and $f$ unmarked children, the returns of its ghost entries solved out. Given moreover laws $K(t\to\cdot)$ on $\{0,\dots,\bar a\}\times\{0,\dots,t\}$ for $t\le3$, a \emph{child type} is $N$ (unmarked) or $U_t$ (marked, with $t$ the second coordinate of its last outcome: its number of unmarked children, and $0$ at height $0$), and for a triple $\sigma$ of child types let $W_\sigma(0)=\delta_{(0,\#N(\sigma))}$ and, for $p\ge1$,
\begin{multline*}
W_\sigma(p)=\frac1{1-\mathrm{loop}_\sigma}\Bigl(\frac14\,\mathrm{up}(W_\sigma(p-1))+\mathrm{lost}_\sigma\,W_\sigma(p-1)+\frac14\sum_{c:N}\sum_{b,f'}\rho(b,f')\,W_{\sigma(c\to U_{f'})}(p_b)\\
+\frac14\sum_{c:U_t}\sum_{a,\,f'<t}K(t\to(a,f'))\,W_{\sigma(c\to U_{f'})}(p_a)\Bigr),
\end{multline*}
where $p_b=\min(p-1+b,P)$, $\mathrm{loop}_\sigma=\frac14\sum_{c:U_t}K(t\to(1,t))$ and $\mathrm{lost}_\sigma=\frac14\sum_{c:U_t}K(t\to(0,t))$. Every other term lowers the total type of $\sigma$ ($N$ counting $4$ and $U_t$ counting $t$), so the system is triangular. Let $\widehat R_h[\rho,K]=W_{NNN}(2)$ and $\mathrm{Wtop}_q[\rho,K]=W_{NNN}(q)$. The \emph{kill} of a law $\ell$ by a keep function $\kappa$ is $\ell(o)\kappa(o)$ at each $o$ other than the bottom, the rest of the mass at the bottom.

\begin{lemma}[the law of the lower model]\label{lem:m1law}\lean{FrogModel.D3.top_law_gen, FrogModel.D3.law_gen, FrogModel.D3.chain_law, FrogModel.D3.map_fp_read, FrogModel.D3.Gen_Wrec, FrogModel.D3.Gen_Yrec}
Let $\rho_0$ and $K_0(f\to\cdot)$ be the kills of $\widehat R_0$ and $\widehat K_0(f\to\cdot)$ by $\kappa^R_0$ and $\kappa^H_0(f;\cdot)$, and for $1\le h<m$ let $\rho_h$ and $K_h(f\to\cdot)$ be the kills of $\widehat R_h[\rho_{h-1},K_{h-1}]$ and $\widehat K_h[\rho_{h-1}](f\to\cdot)$ by $\kappa^R_h$ and $\kappa^H_h(f;\cdot)$. If $k+1\le P$, the run of $M1_L$ on the frog paths and independent kill coins ends almost surely, and its count $\underline G$ has the first marginal of $\mathrm{Wtop}_{k+1}[\rho_{m-1},K_{m-1}]$ as its law.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{The values read are independent.} Each read takes the next unused step of the path of the frog the rule names, or the next unused kill coin, and the choice is a function of the values read before. By the pool argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:recursion}, with one pool for each frog and one for the coins, the sequence of values read has the law of an independent sequence of pairs of a uniform step and a coin. On that sequence the rule is a deterministic machine, so its states form a Markov chain on a countable space: a state is the stack of the open closures, innermost first, each with its vertex, type, $f_0$, pool, ups, the frog on a ghost walk and its depth, and whether it waits for its kill coin, together with the marks.

\emph{The probability of an end is the least solution.} Let $h_b(x)$ be the probability that the chain from $x$ ends with $b$ ups. Let $u\ge0$ be a function on the states with $u(x)=\one\{x\text{ has }b\text{ ups}\}$ at the ends and $u(x)\ge\E\,u(x')$ elsewhere, $x'$ the next state. Then the probability to end with $b$ ups within $n$ reads is at most $u$, by induction on $n$, and it increases to $h_b$; so $h_b\le u$.

\emph{A solution from the recursion.} Let $U_b(x)$ be the probability that the top closure ends with $b$ ups when every open closure finishes with the law the recursion gives: the innermost one from its pool, ups and child types, with probability $(3^{-d}-3^{-L})/(1-3^{-L})$ that a frog on a ghost walk at depth $d$ comes back; each outcome is then killed, and passed to the closure below, which continues in the same way. The child types are read off the marks: an R closure reads, of each marked child, only its number of unmarked children, its type $U_t$, and an H closure at a vertex $z$ reads only $f(z)$; so the marks inside the subtree of a child enter only through these numbers. Height $0$ is the exception: the steps of a closure at a vertex $z$ of height $0$ read no mark, its outcome is $(a,0)$ and its keep depends on $a$ only, so it finishes with the law $\rho_0$ (type $R$) or $K_0(0\to\cdot)$ (type $H$) whatever $f(z)$ is, and a marked child of height $0$ has type $U_0$ whatever its number of unmarked children; no closure has the law $K_0(f\to\cdot)$ with $f\ge1$. The recursions with the loops solved out give $U_b(x)\ge\E\,U_b(x')$, read by read, and $\sum_bU_b=1$. So $h_b\le U_b$.

\emph{The run ends.} The same construction with a cost of one per read, and at most $2(L-d)$ for a ghost walk at depth $d$, gives a finite function $\tau$ on the states with $\tau(x)\ge1+\E\,\tau(x')$ off the ends, $x'$ the next state. So the expected number of reads is at most $\tau(x)$, the run ends almost surely, and $\sum_bh_b=1=\sum_bU_b$; hence $h_b=U_b$. At the initial state, $U_b$ is the first marginal of $\mathrm{Wtop}_{k+1}[\rho_{m-1},K_{m-1}]$ at $b$. This is \decl{FrogModel.D3.top_law_gen} in the formalization, with \texttt{FrogModel.D3.law\_gen} for every state and \texttt{FrogModel.D3.Gen\_Wrec}, \texttt{FrogModel.D3.Gen\_Yrec} for the recursions.
\end{proof}

A stored run is a family of probability vectors below the recursion evaluated on the stored vectors themselves. Proposition~\ref{prop:F3} is what such a run gives.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:F3}\lean{FrogModel.D3.f3L_holds, FrogModel.D3.Iface.f3L, FrogModel.D3.Iface.F3Hyp, FrogModel.D3.Iface.Wtop, FrogModel.D3.Iface.Wrec, FrogModel.D3.Iface.Yrec, FrogModel.D3.Iface.KhatH, FrogModel.D3.Iface.Rhat, FrogModel.D3.Iface.Rhat0, FrogModel.D3.Iface.Khat0}
Let $\bar a\ge1$, $P\ge2$, $L\ge2$ and $m\ge1$. For $0\le h<m$ let $\rho^*_h$ be a probability vector on $\{0,\dots,\bar a\}\times\{0,\dots,3\}$ and $K^*_h(t\to\cdot)$, $t\le3$, a probability vector on $\{0,\dots,\bar a\}\times\{0,\dots,t\}$, such that, entrywise off the bottom,
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $\rho^*_0\le\widehat R_0$ and $K^*_0(t\to\cdot)\le\widehat K_0(t\to\cdot)$;
\item[(ii)] $\rho^*_h\le\widehat R_h[\rho^*_{h-1},K^*_{h-1}]$ and $K^*_h(t\to\cdot)\le\widehat K_h[\rho^*_{h-1}](t\to\cdot)$ for $1\le h<m$.
\end{enumerate}
Let $1\le k$ with $k+1\le P$, and let $\widetilde W\le\mathrm{Wtop}_{k+1}[\rho^*_{m-1},K^*_{m-1}]$ entrywise on $\{0,\dots,\bar a\}\times\{0,\dots,3\}$, and $W(x)=\sum_f\widetilde W(x,f)$. Then
\[
\P\bigl(G_m(k)\le g\bigr)\le1-\sum_{g<x\le \bar a}W(x)\quad(g<\bar a),\qquad\E G_m(k)\ge\sum_{x\le \bar a}x\,W(x). % english-ok: display formula
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By induction on $h\ge1$, let $\kappa^R_h(o)=\rho^*_h(o)/\widehat R_h(o)$ where $o$ is not the bottom and $\widehat R_h(o)>0$, and $1$ elsewhere, with $\widehat R_h$ evaluated on $\rho^*_{h-1},K^*_{h-1}$; by (i) and (ii) these numbers lie in $[0,1]$, and $\rho^*_h(o)=0$ where $\widehat R_h(o)=0$. So the kill of $\widehat R_h$ by $\kappa^R_h$ is $\rho^*_h$ off the bottom, and at the bottom too, both being probability vectors; the same for the $K^*_h$. At height $0$ the keeps must depend on $a$ only: let $\kappa^R_0(a,f')$ be the ratio above at $o=(a,0)$, for every $f'$, and $\kappa^H_0(f;a,f')$ the ratio of $K^*_0(0\to(a,0))$ to $\widehat K_0(0\to(a,0))$ for $a\ge1$ with $\widehat K_0(0\to(a,0))>0$, and $1$ elsewhere, for every $f$ and $f'$. Since $\widehat R_0$ and $\widehat K_0(0\to\cdot)$ put no mass off $f'=0$, neither do $\rho^*_0$ and $K^*_0(0\to\cdot)$, by (i), so the kills of $\widehat R_0$ and of $\widehat K_0(0\to\cdot)$ are $\rho^*_0$ and $K^*_0(0\to\cdot)$, and the recursions read no other law at height $0$. A keep probability is the mass of a set of values of a coin. Lemma~\ref{lem:m1law} then gives $\P(\underline G=x)=\sum_f\mathrm{Wtop}_{k+1}[\rho^*_{m-1},K^*_{m-1}](x,f)\ge W(x)$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:dom} gives $\underline G\le G_m(k)$, with the planted curve computed on the frog paths. This is \decl{FrogModel.D3.f3L_holds} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

The comparisons (i) and (ii) compare two evaluations of the recursion on the same stored vectors, so no monotonicity of the recursion in its inputs is used. Each operation of the recursion (sums and products of nonnegative numbers, the capped shift, the division by $1-\mathrm{loop}$) is nondecreasing in its arguments, so a computation that rounds every operation down gives arrays below the exact ones, and any probability vector below them off the bottom, with the missing mass at the bottom, satisfies (ii).

\subsection*{The seed}
Let $m_0\ge0$, $\bar a\ge1$, parameters $(E,G_M,V_M,J_M)$, and numbers $W_k(x)\ge0$ for $1\le k\le E$ and $x\le \bar a$. Put
\[
e_k=\sum_{x\le \bar a}x\,W_k(x),\qquad c_k(g)=1-\sum_{g<x\le \bar a}W_k(x),\qquad D_0(k)=\min\Bigl\{\mu_{m_0}(k);\ \min_{1\le k'\le k}\bigl(\mu_{m_0}(k')-e_{k'}\bigr)\Bigr\}. % english-ok: display formula
\]
The \emph{seed} is the pair $S_0=(F_0,\delta_0)$ with $\delta_0(k)=D_0(k)/\mu_{m_0}(k)$, $F_0(0,\cdot)=1$ and, for $k\ge1$,
\[
F_0(k,g)=\min_{g\le g'\le G_M}\min\Bigl\{c_k(g')\ (g'<\bar a);\ \P\bigl(\Bin(k,\tfrac13)\le g'\bigr);\ \lambda\bigl(D_0(k),\mu_{m_0}(k),g'\bigr);\ F_0(k-1,g')\Bigr\}. % english-ok: display formula
\]

Lemma~\ref{lem:seed}, used in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:linear}, turns bounds on the laws of the $G_{m_0}(k)$ into a valid state.

\begin{lemma}[seed]\label{lem:seed}\lean{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.seed_valid_proof, FrogModel.D3.Iface.seed_valid, FrogModel.D3.Iface.seedState, FrogModel.D3.Iface.F0, FrogModel.D3.Iface.D0}
If $\P(G_{m_0}(k)\le g)\le c_k(g)$ for $g<\bar a$ and $\E G_{m_0}(k)\ge e_k$, for $1\le k\le E$, then the seed satisfies the inequalities of validity at $m_0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $k'\le k$, $\Delta_{m_0}(k)\le\Delta_{m_0}(k')\le\mu_{m_0}(k')-e_{k'}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:deficit}(1), and $\Delta_{m_0}(k)\le\mu_{m_0}(k)$; so $\Delta_{m_0}(k)\le D_0(k)=\delta_0(k)\mu_{m_0}(k)$. The terms of $F_0$ are the hypothesis, Lemma~\ref{lem:tails}(3) with $D=D_0(k)$, and $G_{m_0}(k)\ge G_{m_0}(k-1)$; a minimum over $g'\ge g$ keeps an upper bound of a distribution function. This is \decl{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.seed_valid_proof} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

The certificate of Section~\ref{sec:proofthree} starts from a state above the seed.

\section{An induction on the height}\label{sec:dprime}

Above $m_1$ the bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:linear} is carried from one height to the next by an induction that tracks $\E G_m(J)$ for one comparison index $J=J(m)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:dprime}). Its inequalities are checked on finitely many intervals (Lemma~\ref{lem:interval}) up to $10^{30}$, and proved beyond (Lemma~\ref{lem:tail}).

Let $c_0=3/5$, $\eps=1/50$, $m_1=1778279$ and $b_1=27290443/50$. For a height $h$ and $x\ge0$ let
\[
\pi(h,x)=\max\Bigl\{0,\ 1-\frac{(1-c_0)\,\mu_h(1)+\mu_h(1)^{1/2}/2}{\mu_h(1)-x}\Bigr\}\ \text{ if }x<\mu_h(1),\qquad\pi(h,x)=0\ \text{ otherwise}.
\]
If $\E G_h(1)\ge c_0\mu_h(1)$, then $\Delta_h(1)\le(1-c_0)\mu_h(1)$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:tails}(1) gives $\P(G_h(1)\ge x)\ge\pi(h,x)$. For integers $J,w,k,k_1,k_2\ge1$ and a height $m$ with $w\le m+1$, let
\begin{align*}
\Theta_J(m)&=\Bigl(1-\max\bigl\{0,\tfrac23-J\,3^{-w}\bigr\}\,\pi(m+1-w,k_2)\Bigr)^{J+1}\\
&\qquad+\P\bigl(\Bin(k_2,3^{1-w})<k\bigr)+\P\bigl(\Bin(k,\tfrac14)<J\bigr),\\
\Theta^\circ(m)&=1-\chi\bigl(\pi(m,k_1)\bigr)+\P\bigl(\Bin(k_1,\tfrac14)<J\bigr),
\end{align*}
with $\chi$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:fresh}. Given functions $J,w,k,k_1,k_2$ of the height, the \emph{conditions at} $m$ are: $J,k,k_1,k_2\ge1$, $1\le w\le m+1$, $m+1-w>m_1-30$, and
\begin{align*}
&\text{(K1)}\quad\frac{m+J+1}3\,\Theta_J(m)\le\eps,\\
&\text{(K2)}\quad\Bigl(b_1+\bigl(\tfrac13-\eps\bigr)(m-m_1)\Bigr)\bigl(1-\Theta^\circ(m)\bigr)-\frac{J-2}3\ge c_0\,\frac{m+3}3,
\end{align*}
all parameters taken at $m$.

Theorem~\ref{thm:dprime} is the third stage of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:linear}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:dprime}\lean{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.thmDprime1_proof, FrogModel.D3.Iface.thmDprime1, FrogModel.D3.LaneE.thmDprime1_of, FrogModel.D3.Iface.DCond, FrogModel.D3.Iface.LJ, FrogModel.D3.Iface.L1, FrogModel.D3.Iface.jackLower}
Let $J,w,k,k_1,k_2:\N\to\N$ with $J(m_1)=62$, $J$ nondecreasing on $[m_1,\infty)$, and the conditions at every $m\ge m_1$. Assume
\[
\E G_h(1)\ge c_0\,\mu_h(1)\quad(m_1-30<h\le m_1),\qquad\E G_{m_1}(62)\ge b_1.
\]
Then $\E G_m(1)\ge c_0\,\mu_m(1)$ for every $m>m_1-30$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By induction on $m\ge m_1$ we prove: $\E G_h(1)\ge c_0\mu_h(1)$ for $m_1-30<h\le m$, and $\E G_m(J(m))\ge b_1+(\frac13-\eps)(m-m_1)$. At $m_1$ this is the assumption. Let it hold at $m$, and write $J=J(m)$ and so on. The heights $m+1-w$ and $m$ lie in $(m_1-30,m]$, so $\P(G_{m+1-w}(1)\ge k_2)\ge\pi(m+1-w,k_2)$ and $\P(G_m(1)\ge k_1)\ge\pi(m,k_1)$.

\emph{The index $J$.} In the closure at height $m+1$ with $J$ entrants, Lemma~\ref{lem:deep} with $q=J+1$, $x=k$ and $x'=k_2$, then Lemma~\ref{lem:loss}, give $\P(e_1<J)\le \Theta_J(m)$: the first term of $\Theta_J$ is nonincreasing in the probability it reads, and it is at least $1$ if $\frac23-J3^{-w}<0$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:loss} and Lemma~\ref{lem:awake}, $L_{J,J}\le\E G_m(J)\,\Theta_J(m)\le\mu_m(J)\,\Theta_J(m)\le\eps$ by (K1), and Lemma~\ref{lem:D1} with $j=J$ gives $\E G_{m+1}(J)\ge\E G_m(J)+\frac13-\eps$. Since $J(m+1)\ge J$ and $\E G_{m+1}$ is nondecreasing, $\E G_{m+1}(J(m+1))\ge b_1+(\frac13-\eps)(m+1-m_1)$.

\emph{One entrant.} In the closure with one entrant, Lemma~\ref{lem:fresh} with $q=2$ and $x=k_1$, then Lemma~\ref{lem:loss}, give $\P(e_1<J)\le \Theta^\circ(m)$, since $1-\chi$ is decreasing on $[0,1]$. Lemmas~\ref{lem:D1} and~\ref{lem:loss} with $j=1$ give $\E G_{m+1}(1)\ge\E G_m(J)\bigl(1-\Theta^\circ(m)\bigr)-\frac{J-2}3$. By (K2), $1-\Theta^\circ(m)>0$, since otherwise its left side is at most $\frac13$; so the induction hypothesis on $\E G_m(J)$ and (K2) give $\E G_{m+1}(1)\ge c_0(m+3)/3=c_0\mu_{m+1}(1)$. This is \decl{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.thmDprime1_proof} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Intervals}
On an interval of heights with fixed parameters the conditions reduce to finitely many inequalities between rational numbers. An \emph{entry} is a tuple $(m_\ell,m_h,J,w,\eta,a_9,s_w,s_1)$ of integers $m_\ell\le m_h$, $J,w,\eta,a_9$ and rationals $s_w,s_1\ge0$; its parameters are $k=2\eta J$, $k_2=3\eta J\,3^{w-1}$ and $k_1=a_9J$. Let $\mu_w=\mu_{m_\ell+1-w}(1)$, $\mu_1=\mu_{m_\ell}(1)$, and
\begin{align*}
p_w&=1-\frac{(1-c_0)\mu_w+s_w/2}{\mu_w-k_2},\qquad p_1=1-\frac{(1-c_0)\mu_1+s_1/2}{\mu_1-k_1},\\
t_2&=\Bigl(\frac38\Bigr)^{\eta J}\Bigl(\frac32\Bigr)^{k-1},\qquad t_3=\frac{(3/4+z_3/4)^k}{z_3^{J-1}},\qquad t_9=\frac{(3/4+z_9/4)^{k_1}}{z_9^{J-1}},
\end{align*}
with $z_3=3/(2\eta-1)$ and $z_9=3/(a_9-1)$. The entry \emph{passes} if $J\ge1$, $1\le w\le30$, $m_1\le m_\ell$, $\eta\ge2$, $a_9\ge4$, $\mu_w\le s_w^2$, $\mu_1\le s_1^2$, $k_2<\mu_w$, $k_1<\mu_1$, $\frac23-J3^{-w}\ge0$, $p_w\ge0$, and, with $\bar\Theta^\circ=1-\chi(p_1)+t_9$,
\begin{align*}
&\frac{m_h+J+1}3\Bigl(\bigl(1-(\tfrac23-J3^{-w})p_w\bigr)^{J+1}+t_2+t_3\Bigr)\le\eps,\qquad\frac{c_0}3\le\Bigl(\frac13-\eps\Bigr)(1-\bar\Theta^\circ),\\
&\frac{c_0(m_\ell+3)+J-2}3\le\Bigl(b_1+\bigl(\tfrac13-\eps\bigr)(m_\ell-m_1)\Bigr)(1-\bar\Theta^\circ).
\end{align*}

Lemma~\ref{lem:interval}, used in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:dcheck}, reduces the conditions on an interval to the test of its entry.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:interval}\lean{FrogModel.D3.LaneE.Entry.sound, FrogModel.D3.LaneE.Entry.check}
If an entry passes, then the conditions hold at every $m\in[m_\ell,m_h]$ for parameter functions equal to $J,w,k,k_1,k_2$ there.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Binomial tails.} For $0\le p\le1$ and $0<z\le1$, $\P(\Bin(n,p)<J)\le\E z^{\Bin(n,p)-(J-1)}=(1-p+pz)^n/z^{J-1}$. With $p=3^{1-w}$ and $z=2/3$, $1-p+pz=1-3^{-w}$ and $(1-3^{-w})^{k_2}\le e^{-k_2\,3^{-w}}=e^{-\eta J}\le(3/8)^{\eta J}$, so $\P(\Bin(k_2,3^{1-w})<k)\le t_2$; with $p=1/4$, $\P(\Bin(k,\frac14)<J)\le t_3$ and $\P(\Bin(k_1,\frac14)<J)\le t_9$. \emph{Jackpots.} Since $\mu_w\le s_w^2$, $\pi(m_\ell+1-w,k_2)\ge p_w$, and the same at $m_\ell$. The function $\pi(\cdot,x)$ is nondecreasing in the height: with $\mu\le\mu'$ and $s=\mu^{1/2}\le s'=\mu'^{1/2}$, the difference of the two fractions, cross-multiplied, is $-(1-c_0)x(\mu'-\mu)+ss'(s-s')/2-x(s'-s)/2\le0$. So on the interval $\Theta_J(m)\le \Theta_J(m_\ell)$ and $\Theta^\circ(m)\le \Theta^\circ(m_\ell)\le \bar\Theta^\circ$, the maps $p\mapsto(1-cp)^{J+1}$, $0\le c\le\frac23$, and $p\mapsto1-\chi(p)$ being nonincreasing on $[0,1]$. \emph{The conditions.} (K1) at $m$ follows from the first inequality since $m\le m_h$. For (K2), the second inequality says that its left side minus its right side, with $\bar\Theta^\circ$ in place of $\Theta^\circ(m)$, is nondecreasing in $m$, and the third that it is nonnegative at $m_\ell$; replacing $\bar\Theta^\circ$ by $\Theta^\circ(m)\le \bar\Theta^\circ$ only helps. The other conditions are read off the entry, with $m+1-w\ge m_1-29$. This is \decl{FrogModel.D3.LaneE.Entry.sound} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

Above $10^{30}$ the parameters are given by formulas, and Lemma~\ref{lem:tail}, used in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:linear}, proves the conditions there.

\begin{lemma}[the tail]\label{lem:tail}\lean{FrogModel.D3.LaneE.dprime_tail_holds, FrogModel.D3.Iface.dprime_tail, FrogModel.D3.Iface.Jtail, FrogModel.D3.Iface.Dtail, FrogModel.D3.Iface.ytail, FrogModel.D3.Iface.ktail}
For $m\ge10^{30}$ let $J(m)=\max\{140,\lceil\frac{11}5\log m\rceil\}$, $w(m)$ the least integer with $3^{w(m)}\ge100J(m)$, $k_2(m)=18J(m)\,3^{w(m)-1}$ and $k(m)=k_1(m)=12J(m)$. Then the conditions hold at every $m\ge10^{30}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $J=J(m)$ and so on. Since $\log x\le4x^{1/4}$, $J\le9m^{1/4}$; since $J\ge\frac{11}5\log m$ and $e^{5/11}\le197/125$, $m\le(197/125)^J$; and $J3^{-w}\le1/100$, so $\frac23-J3^{-w}\ge197/300$. The thresholds $k_2\le1800J^2$ and $k_1=12J$ are negligible against $\mu_h(1)\ge m/4$ at the heights $h\ge m+1-w$ read, so both jackpots $\pi(m+1-w,k_2)$ and $\pi(m,k_1)$ are at least $59/100$. The parameters are those of an entry with $\eta=6$ and $a_9=12$, and the tilt bounds of the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:interval} give, with $1-\frac{197}{300}\cdot\frac{59}{100}=\frac{18377}{30000}$ and $m+J+1\le2m$,
\[
\frac{m+J+1}3\Theta_J(m)\le\frac23\Bigl(\frac{197}{125}\Bigr)^J\Bigl(\Bigl(\frac{18377}{30000}\Bigr)^{J+1}+\Bigl(\bigl(\tfrac38\bigr)^6\bigl(\tfrac32\bigr)^{12}\Bigr)^J+\Bigl(\bigl(\tfrac9{11}\bigr)^{12}\tfrac{11}3\Bigr)^J\Bigr),
\]
which decreases in $J$ and is about $0.0030\le\eps$ at $J=140$. For (K2), $1-\chi(59/100)=1-\frac{51507}{80000}$ and $t_9\le10^{-10}$, so $1-\Theta^\circ(m)\ge\frac{51507}{80000}-10^{-10}$ and the slope $(\frac13-\eps)(1-\Theta^\circ(m))-\frac{c_0}3$ is at least $\frac{47}{150}(\frac{51507}{80000}-10^{-10})-\frac15>0.0017$; this slope times $m-m_1$ exceeds $\frac{J-2}3\le3m^{1/4}$ and the other terms. Finally $100J\le222\log m\le m$, so $w<1+\log_3m$ and $m+1-w>m_1-30$. This is \decl{FrogModel.D3.LaneE.dprime_tail_holds} in the formalization, with the rational inequalities checked in exact arithmetic.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:linear}}\label{sec:proofthree}

The three stages of Section~\ref{sec:three} rest on two finite computations. The first is a stored run of the recursion of Section~\ref{sec:lower} and a certificate of steps of $\Phi$ from the seed it gives; the second is a table of entries.

\begin{proposition}[the run and the certificate]\label{prop:m1run}\lean{FrogModel.D3.Chain.m1run_certS_proof, FrogModel.D3.Iface.m1run_certS, FrogModel.D3.Iface.M1Run.Holds, FrogModel.D3.Iface.CertS1, FrogModel.D3.Iface.P0, FrogModel.D3.Iface.topMass}\computed{code/m1run/run.sh, code/d3cert/run.sh, code/d3cert/out/run.out, code/d3chain/gen.sh, code/formal-proof/main_axioms.lean}
\begin{enumerate}
\item There are probability vectors $\rho^*_h$, $K^*_h(t\to\cdot)$, $0\le h\le99$, satisfying (i) and (ii) of Proposition~\ref{prop:F3} at $(\bar a,P,L,m)=(48,96,60,100)$, and arrays $\widetilde W_q\le\mathrm{Wtop}_q[\rho^*_{99},K^*_{99}]$ for $q=2,\dots,33$.
\item Let $S_0$ be the seed at $m_0=100$, $\bar a=48$ and $(E,G_M)=(32,96)$ made from $W_k(x)=\sum_f\widetilde W_{k+1}(x,f)$. There are a state $S'_0\ge S_0$ at the parameters $(32,96,16,12)$ and a chain of states as in Lemma~\ref{lem:step}(5) from $S'_0$ at height $100$, ending at a height $h_n\le m_1-30$, and a state $T$ at parameters with $E\ge62$, such that $T\ge S_n$, $\Phi_{[h_n,m_1-1]}(T)\le T$, $\delta_T(1)\le2/5$ and $\delta_T(62)\le7045771/88917100$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The certificate has $500$ plain steps at the parameters $(32,96,16,12)$ from $100$ to $600$, an extension to $(64,224,32,24)$ at $600$, five plain steps to $605$, and a state $T$ checked on $[605,m_1]$. Its values are dyadic numbers $n/2^{52}$, and $\delta_T(1)=0.1345023427\ldots$ and $\delta_T(62)=0.0708908650\ldots$, with margins $0.2654976572\ldots$ and $0.0083489100\ldots$ to the two thresholds. The run is stored over $2^{62}$.\computed{code/d3cert/run.sh, code/d3cert/out/run.out, code/m1run/run.sh}

\begin{proof}
The kernel evaluates checkers written in Lean on the stored run and on the certificate: the run against the recursions of Section~\ref{sec:lower}, each plain step against $\Phi_h$ of the previous state, the extension against Definition~\ref{def:ext}, the first state against the seed, and the last state against $\Phi_{[605,m_1-1]}$ and the two thresholds. The formal proof shows that data that pass the checkers satisfy the proposition. Section~\ref{sec:comp-three} describes the data and the checks. This is \decl{FrogModel.D3.Chain.m1run_certS_proof} in the formalization.\computed{code/formal-proof/main_axioms.lean, records/build/release-build.sh, records/build/out/release-build.out}
\end{proof}

The second computation of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:linear} is the table of entries below $10^{30}$.

\begin{proposition}[the table]\label{prop:dcheck}\lean{FrogModel.D3.LaneE.dcheck1_holds, FrogModel.D3.Iface.dcheck1}\computed{code/dcheck/dbranch_m1.gp, code/dcheck/dcheck_m1_cert.gp, code/dcheck/gen_table.gp}
There are $95$ entries, all passing, whose intervals $[M_i,M_{i+1}]$, $i=3,\dots,97$, with $M_i=\mathrm{round}(10^{5.5+i/4})$, so that $M_3=m_1$ and $M_{98}=10^{30}$, cover $[m_1,10^{30}]$, with $J$ nondecreasing from $62$ on the first interval to at most $140$ on the last. So the conditions hold at every $m\in[m_1,10^{30}]$ for the parameter functions that read the entry of the last interval starting at or below $m$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The kernel evaluates the test of each entry in exact rationals, and checks that consecutive intervals meet and that $J$ does not decrease; Lemma~\ref{lem:interval} gives the conditions on each interval. This is \decl{FrogModel.D3.LaneE.dcheck1_holds} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

The comparison index is $62$ on the first $25$ intervals. Over the $95$ entries, the largest left side of the first inequality is at most $0.9988\,\eps$, the smallest slope margin $(\frac13-\eps)(1-\bar\Theta^\circ)-\frac{c_0}3$ is at least $4.18\cdot10^{-3}$, and the third inequality holds with a slack of at least $0.00427$.\computed{code/dcheck/gen_probe.gp, code/dcheck/run.sh, code/dcheck/out/run.out}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:linear}]
By Proposition~\ref{prop:m1run}(1) and Proposition~\ref{prop:F3} with $k=1,\dots,32$ ($k+1\le33\le P$), $\P(G_{100}(k)\le g)\le c_k(g)$ for $g<48$ and $\E G_{100}(k)\ge e_k$, so the seed satisfies the inequalities of validity at $100$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:seed}), and so does $S'_0\ge S_0$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:m1run}(2) and Lemma~\ref{lem:step}(5), $T$ is valid at every height of $[h_n,m_1]$, which contains $(m_1-30,m_1]$. Validity of $T$ gives $\E G_h(1)\ge(1-\delta_T(1))\mu_h(1)\ge\frac35\mu_h(1)$ for $m_1-30<h\le m_1$, and $\E G_{m_1}(62)\ge(1-\delta_T(62))\mu_{m_1}(62)\ge b_1$, since $\mu_{m_1}(62)=1778342/3$ and $(1-\frac{7045771}{88917100})\frac{1778342}3=b_1$. Let $J,w,k,k_1,k_2$ be the parameter functions of Proposition~\ref{prop:dcheck} on $[m_1,10^{30})$ and those of Lemma~\ref{lem:tail} from $10^{30}$ on; $J$ is nondecreasing, since $J\le140$ below $10^{30}$, and the conditions hold at every $m\ge m_1$. Theorem~\ref{thm:dprime} gives $\E G_m(1)\ge\frac35\mu_m(1)=\frac{m+2}5$ for every $m>m_1-30$. This is \decl{FrogModel.D3.Assembly.linear_three'} in the formalization, with Proposition~\ref{prop:m1run} as its hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\section{The formal proof and its data}\label{sec:comp}

This section describes how the proof is checked: what the formal proof proves and what it trusts, how the kernel checks the certificate, and which programs wrote the data it checks. The repository gives the commands that rerun each check, and it maps every numbered lemma, proposition and theorem to the Lean declarations of its formal proof.

\subsection{The formal proof}\label{sec:comp-lean}\computed{FrogModel/Challenge.lean, .github/workflows/ci.yml, code/formal-proof/main_axioms.lean}
The formal proof \cite{FrogLean} is a development in Lean~4 \cite{Lean4} with Mathlib \cite{Mathlib20}, in the versions of Section~\ref{sec:comp-soft}. Its two statements for $d=4$ repeat Theorem~\ref{thm:main} for the model of the introduction; Section~\ref{sec:comp-three} gives the statement for $d=3$. A vertex of $\T_d$ is a finite word over $\{1,\dots,d\}$; every frog and every step of that frog carry one step variable, uniform on $\{1,\dots,d\}\times\{0,1,\dots,d\}$, all independent, the first coordinate choosing the child at the root and the second the parent ($0$) or a child elsewhere, so that every step is uniform on the neighbours of the current vertex; the wake times are defined by recursion on time. For $d=4$, the first statement says that the set of pairs (frog, time $t\ge1$) at which an awake frog is at the root is finite almost surely, and the second that the expected number of these pairs is at most $5.756$. The theorems \texttt{FrogModel.transient\_four} and \texttt{FrogModel.meanVisits\_four\_le} prove them. They have no hypothesis, and they depend on the three standard axioms of Lean and Mathlib, propositional extensionality, the axiom of choice and the soundness of quotients, and on no other axiom.

The Lean kernel checks each declaration of the development when it is compiled. Comparator \cite{Comparator} checks that the two theorems for $d=4$ prove the statements as written in a copy of the statement files, which imports only Mathlib, and that the proofs use no axiom other than these three. It has every declaration that the theorems depend on, those of Mathlib included, checked again by nanoda \cite{nanoda}, an implementation of the Lean kernel written separately.

The written proofs follow the route and the lemma cuts of the formal proof, with three differences of form. The formal proof states the lemmas of Section~\ref{sec:criterion} for the table of Section~\ref{sec:certificate}, and some of them for every table. Its root chain draws, at each step, a direction and one uniform variable, and the entered child takes the transition that the quantile of this variable selects in its row, which builds the coupling of the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:maxchain} into the chain. And it proves Theorems~\ref{thm:B} and~\ref{thm:main} for every data tree that passes the checks of Proposition~\ref{prop:cert}, and applies them to the data of Section~\ref{sec:certificate}.

\subsection{The check of the certificate}\label{sec:comp-check}\computed{FrogModel/G3K/Spec.lean, .github/workflows/ci.yml, code/g3k/modules.sha256}
Items (1) to (3) of Proposition~\ref{prop:cert} are decided by the kernel, by evaluation of functions of the table, with no compiled code. Items (4) to (7) are decided in the same way, on data of the following form. Each state of $\Sigma$ is an entry of a finite binary search tree, with its key (the integer that codes the phase, the four child states and the number of waiting frogs), its flag, and the values $2^{40}\ell(s)[x]$ and $2^{40}u(s)[b]$, nonnegative integers packed into one integer per state and per function, each in a field of fixed width. A checker written in Lean takes the tree and returns true or false. For every entry it bounds every field of its values, generates the moves and stops of its state from the table and the key, with the weights rounded as in Proposition~\ref{prop:cert}(5), looks up each target in the tree, reading the lump of a reached state whose key is missing or whose flag is $0$, and compares the two sides of (C4) and (C5) field by field by integer arithmetic on the packed integers; the bounds on the fields are what make these packed comparisons equal to the comparisons field by field. A second function checks the start: $s_0$ is in the tree, the atoms of a list that defines $L$ lie below $\ell(s_0)$, and (C6). A third checks the plan of (C7) on that list. The formal proof shows, for every tree and every list, that if the three functions return true then $\Phi_4(H^*)\stle H^*$; so the proof uses no property of the data that the functions do not test. The kernel evaluates the three functions on the data of Section~\ref{sec:certificate}: the tree is cut into $303$ subtrees of at most $20000$ moves each, each subtree is checked by a theorem proved by evaluation, and these theorems are assembled into the theorem for the whole tree.

\subsection{The data}\label{sec:comp-data}\computed{code/table/run_table.sh, code/table/out/iterates.out, code/certificate/run_gen.sh, code/certificate/out/gen.out, code/g3k/G3Tool/MainK.lean, code/gp/table_tex.gp}
